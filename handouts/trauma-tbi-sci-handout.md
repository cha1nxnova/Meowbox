# Trauma, TBI and Spinal Cord Injury in the ER: Topic Discussion Handout

Nov 20, 2026 · @Kadin Booker

In trauma the pharmacist's leverage is time: most of the drugs that change outcomes in the first hour (tranexamic acid, calcium, reversal agents, hyperosmolar therapy, antibiotics for open wounds) only work if they are given early and at the right dose. This handout covers blunt and penetrating trauma, traumatic brain injury and spinal cord injury, ER stabilization only. It builds on the hemorrhagic shock section of our shock handout and goes further into the trauma-specific guidelines. Grades are printed in each society's own wording. Items marked † came from a secondary summary or from recall of the source and were not confirmed in the primary document (see Notes on marked items).

## Trauma at a glance

ATLS 11 (2025) changed the primary survey from ABCDE to **xABCDE**, where x is control of exsanguinating external hemorrhage, as "for certain patients, rapid control of exsanguinating external hemorrhage is lifesaving" \[3\]. A 2026 review adds that ATLS 11 limits crystalloid and moves transfusion earlier \[4\], and blog summaries add earlier tranexamic acid†.

**Blunt vs penetrating: what changes for pharmacy**†

|  | Blunt | Penetrating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical patient | Falls and motor vehicle crashes. Older adults on antithrombotics | Gunshot and stab wounds. Younger adults |
| Injury pattern | Multisystem: brain, spine, chest wall, pelvis, solid organs | Track of the missile or blade. Hollow viscus and vascular injury |
| Bleeding | Often hidden (pelvis, retroperitoneum, spleen, liver). Coagulopathy worsened by anticoagulants | Often brisk and surgical. Penetrating mechanism is one point on the ABC score \[21\] |
| Drugs that matter most early | Reversal agents, TXA, hyperosmolar therapy, seizure prophylaxis, analgesia for rib fractures | TXA, blood, calcium, antibiotics before the OR, tetanus |
| Disposition clock | Imaging first in the stable patient | Operating room or angiography often within minutes |

**The pharmacist's first ten minutes in the bay**

| Step | What we do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Get the **time of injury** and any prehospital TXA dose | TXA after 3 hours is associated with harm, and the total should never exceed 2 g \[2\], \[6\] |
| 2 | Get a weight (estimated is fine) | Calcium, PCC, induction agents, mannitol and every pediatric dose are weight based |
| 3 | Ask about anticoagulants and antiplatelets, and when the last dose was taken | Reversal is a protocol every designated trauma center must have \[2\] |
| 4 | Draw up TXA and calcium, and confirm the massive hemorrhage protocol is active | Calcium after every 2 or more units of blood \[2\] |
| 5 | Prepare RSI drugs at shock doses, with a pressor in hand | Induction drops pressure in the bleeding patient |
| 6 | Plan antibiotics and tetanus for open wounds | Open fractures need antibiotics within the first hour, and penetrating abdominal injuries need a dose as soon as laparotomy is likely† |
| 7 | Document every drug and time | Duplicate TXA and missed calcium are the errors to catch \[11\] |

## Hemorrhage control and resuscitation: ER algorithm

The sequence is a synthesis of ATLS 11 \[3\], the ACS transfusion guideline (2026) \[2\] and the European guideline \[1\], not a published algorithm. Steps 2 to 6 happen at the same time in a real bay, and the pharmacist's lane is essentially steps 3, 4 and 6. Hemorrhagic shock itself (classes, vasopressor choice) is covered in our shock handout.

## Hemorrhage drug chart

Two documents govern this section. The European guideline, 6th edition (2023), grades each statement but its evidence stops in December 2021 \[1\]. The new ACS Best Practices Guidelines for Transfusion in Trauma (November 2026) replaces the 2014 massive transfusion guideline and is the current US reference, written as key points without letter grades \[2\]. Where the two differ, both are shown.

**When to activate massive hemorrhage protocol** \[2\], \[21\]

| Trigger | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Shock index | Above 1.0 with SBP below 110 mm Hg |
| ABC score 2 or more | One point each: penetrating mechanism, positive FAST, SBP 90 or less, HR 120 or more. Sensitivity 75%, specificity 86% |
| Ongoing need | More than 3 units of blood products in 1 hour |
| Other | EtCO2 below 25 mm Hg on controlled ventilation, high lactate or base deficit |

"Available data do not clearly support one MHP activation criterion over the others" \[2\].

**Drugs and products**

| Drug or product | Dose | When | Source and grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tranexamic acid | 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g over 8 h. Or 2 g IV bolus | Within 3 h of injury (later only with viscoelastic evidence of fibrinolysis). Never more than 2 g total | European 1A for 1 g plus 1 g \[1\]. ACS key point for the 2 g bolus \[2\]. EAST 2025 "cannot recommend for or against" the higher bolus \[10\] |
| Blood | Low-titer O whole blood, or components in a 1:1:1 ratio. Platelets and plasma in the first round | From the first round | ACS key point \[2\]. European: plasma to red cells at least 1:2, or fibrinogen plus red cells (1C) \[1\] |
| Calcium chloride 10% | 20 mg/kg IV (maximum 1 g) | After every 2 or more units of blood. Keep ionized calcium 1.0 to 1.3 mmol/L | ACS \[2\]. European: keep within the normal range and prefer the chloride salt (1C) \[1\] |
| Calcium gluconate 10% | 40 mg/kg IV (maximum 2 g) | Same | ACS \[2\] |
| Fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate | 3 to 4 g fibrinogen concentrate, or 15 to 20 single-donor units of cryoprecipitate (about 3 to 4 pools of 5†, not 15 to 20 pools) | Clauss fibrinogen 1.5 g/L or less, or viscoelastic deficit. Not empiric | European 1C trigger, 2C dose \[1\]. ACS: "Empiric use of fibrinogen has not been shown to improve outcomes" \[2\] |
| Platelets | 1 apheresis unit (about 5 to 6 whole-blood units) | Keep above 50 x 10⁹/L, or above 100 x 10⁹/L with TBI (European. TQIP 2024 uses 75 x 10⁹/L for TBI) | European 2C \[1\], \[2\] |
| 4F-PCC for trauma coagulopathy | Do not give empirically |  | ACS \[2\]. PROCOAG: more thromboembolism \[17\] |
| Recombinant factor VIIa | Not first line. Last resort only |  | European 1B against first-line use \[1\] |
| Norepinephrine | Titrate to the permissive target | Only if restricted volume fails to reach it | European 1C \[1\] |
| Red cells after hemostasis | Hemoglobin target 7 to 9 g/dL |  | European 1C \[1\]. ACS 7 to 8 g/dL \[2\] |

A great way to keep calcium straight is the elemental math: 10 mL of 10% calcium chloride carries 270 mg of elemental calcium and 10 mL of 10% gluconate carries 90 mg \[1\]. So the two ACS maximums (1 g chloride, 2 g gluconate) are not equivalent. Hypocalcemia is present on arrival in about 56% of trauma patients before any transfusion, and it carries roughly 2.4 times the odds of death \[22\]. No trial yet shows that giving calcium improves survival, and CAVALIER (calcium plus vasopressin) is still enrolling \[24\].

**Blood pressure targets until bleeding is controlled**

| Patient | European 2023 \[1\] | ACS 2026 \[2\] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No brain injury | SBP 80 to 90 mm Hg, MAP 50 to 60 (1B) | SBP 90 mm Hg |
| Severe TBI | MAP 80 mm Hg or more (1C) | SBP 110 mm Hg |
| Children |  | Permissive hypotension "is not a recommended strategy" |

The European guideline also recommends avoiding hypotonic fluids such as Ringer's lactate in severe head trauma (1B) \[1\].

**Anticoagulant and antiplatelet reversal**

Andexanet alfa left the US market on December 22, 2025, after FDA concluded its risks outweigh its benefits (thrombosis 14.6% vs 6.9% in ANNEXA-I, per FDA) \[25\]. 4F-PCC is now first line for factor Xa inhibitors \[2\].

Weight capped at 100 kg \[27\]. Fixed option 1,500 units, or 2,000 if INR above 10 or weight above 100 kg \[2\]. Vitamin K 5 to 10 mg IV | European 1A \[1\]. Plasma is second line \[2\] |
| Apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban | 4F-PCC | 25 units/kg minor bleeding, 50 units/kg major, or fixed 3,000 units \[2\]. European suggests starting at 25 units/kg (2C) \[1\] | ACS \[2\]. Plasma "not recommended" |

**Key trials**

| Trial | Result |
| --- | --- |
| CRASH-2, Lancet 2010 (n=20,211) \[5\] | TXA: all-cause death 14.5% vs 16.0% (RR 0.91) |
| CRASH-2 timing analysis, 2011 \[6\] | Bleeding death RR 0.68 within 1 h, 0.79 at 1 to 3 h, and 1.44 (harm) after 3 h |
| PATCH-Trauma, NEJM 2023 (n=1,310) \[7\] | Prehospital TXA: good 6-month outcome 53.7% vs 53.5%. 28-day death 17.3% vs 21.8% (RR 0.79, secondary) |
| STAAMP, JAMA Surg 2021 (n=903) \[8\] | Prehospital TXA: 30-day death 8.1% vs 9.9% (P=.17) |
| EAST 2025 meta-analysis \[10\] | TXA lowered 24-hour and 30-day mortality, prehospital and in hospital, with no rise in clotting events |
| PROPPR, JAMA 2015 (n=680) \[12\] | 1:1:1 vs 1:1:2: 24-hour death 12.7% vs 17.0% (P=.12). Exsanguination death 9.2% vs 14.6% |
| PAMPer, NEJM 2018 (n=501) \[13\] | Prehospital plasma by air: 30-day death 23.2% vs 33.0% |
| SWiFT, NEJM 2026 (n=616) \[14\] | Prehospital whole blood vs components: death or massive transfusion 48.7% vs 47.7% |
| TOWAR, NEJM 2026 (n=993) \[15\] | Prehospital whole blood vs components: 30-day death 25.9% vs 20.5% (aOR 1.24, not significant) |
| CRYOSTAT-2, JAMA 2023 (n=1,604) \[16\] | Empiric early cryoprecipitate: 28-day death 25.3% vs 26.1% |
| PROCOAG, JAMA 2023 (n=324) \[17\] | Empiric 4F-PCC: no fewer products, thromboembolism 35% vs 24% |
| UK-REBOA, JAMA 2023 (n=90) \[18\] | ER balloon occlusion: 90-day death 54% vs 42%, 86.9% probability of harm |
| Sims, JAMA Surg 2019 (n=100) \[20\] | Vasopressin: less blood (1.4 vs 2.9 L), mortality 12% vs 12% |

Overall, since PROPPR no in-hospital product or adjunct has improved survival, and the two empiric add-ons tested (cryoprecipitate, PCC) either did nothing or caused clots. Early TXA, balanced blood and fast surgical hemostasis are essentially the core, even though PROPPR itself did not lower 24-hour or 30-day mortality.

## Blunt and penetrating trauma guidelines

The Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma (EAST) writes most of the injury-specific guidelines, and many of the ones that matter to pharmacy are from 2010 to 2012 yet are still the newest available. The newer EAST documents use GRADE wording ("we recommend", "we conditionally recommend"). The Western Trauma Association (WTA) publishes algorithms, and ACS TQIP publishes ungraded best practices.

| Injury | What the guideline says | Pharmacy angle | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Penetrating abdominal injury | Stable stab wounds and tangential gunshot wounds without peritonitis do not need routine laparotomy | Antibiotics only for 24 h with a hollow viscus injury (Level I). No data for longer courses after damage control | EAST 2010 \[92\]. EAST 2012 \[84\] |
| Tube thoracostomy for traumatic hemothorax or pneumothorax | "We conditionally recommend that antibiotic prophylaxis be given at the time of insertion to reduce empyema" | Empyema odds 0.47 overall, 0.25 in penetrating trauma. No effect on pneumonia or death. No drug named | EAST 2022 \[85\] |
| Open fractures | Antibiotics within 1 hour of arrival, scaled to the Gustilo type | See the prophylaxis chart below | EAST 2011† \[86\]. TQIP 2015 \[87\] |
| Blunt splenic and hepatic injury | Nonoperative management is "the treatment modality of choice" in stable patients regardless of grade | Vaccines only after splenectomy. EAST "conditionally recommend\[s\] against routine post-splenectomy vaccinations" after embolization alone | EAST 2012 \[90\]. EAST 2022 \[96\] |
| Blunt cerebrovascular injury | "We recommend the use of ATT" (antithrombotic therapy). Stroke odds 0.20, mortality odds 0.17 | Aspirin or heparin, started once bleeding risk allows†. EAST 2010 called them "seemingly equivalent"† | EAST 2020 \[88\] |
| Blunt cardiac injury | ECG and troponin I for everyone suspected. Normal both rules it out | ECG and troponin I on arrival | EAST 2012 \[89\] |
| Pelvic fracture hemorrhage | Binder, angiography, preperitoneal packing | TXA and massive transfusion as in the hemorrhage section | EAST 2011 \[104\] |
| Rhabdomyolysis and crush | "Conditionally recommend for aggressive" IV fluid. "Conditionally recommend against treatment with bicarbonate or mannitol" | No bicarbonate or mannitol drips. Avoid succinylcholine† | EAST 2022 \[93\] |
| Older adults on anticoagulants | Coagulation profile and brain imaging "as soon as possible after admission" | Reversal protocol ready before the CT reads | EAST 2012 \[95\] |
| Trauma in pregnancy | Kleihauer-Betke testing in Rh-negative patients to dose Rh(D) immune globulin | Rh(D) immune globulin dose from the test | EAST 2025 \[94\] |
| Rib fractures | See the analgesia section | Multimodal analgesia, blocks | EAST 2016, 2023 \[70\], \[71\] |

The WTA has newer algorithms for penetrating chest injury, blunt cerebrovascular injury and the mangled extremity (2026), and for blunt splenic injury (2023). We could not read them, so they are not summarized here.

## Infection prophylaxis, tetanus and vaccines

The trauma guidelines name the timing and the spectrum but almost never the dose, so the doses below are standard surgical prophylaxis values† and our institutional protocol wins.

**Antibiotic prophylaxis**

| Indication | Drug | Adult dose | Timing and duration | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open fracture, Gustilo I or II | Cefazolin. Clindamycin for severe beta-lactam allergy | Cefazolin 2 g IV (3 g if 120 kg or more)†. Clindamycin 900 mg IV† | Within 1 hour of arrival, then scheduled until surgery. Stop 24 h after the procedure | TQIP 2015 \[87\]. EAST 2011 Level 1 for gram-positive coverage† \[86\] |
| Open fracture, Gustilo III | Cefazolin plus a gram-negative agent (gentamicin is the example named) | Gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV once daily† | Up to 72 h if severely contaminated, not beyond 72 h unless re-operated | TQIP 2015 \[87\]. EAST 2011 Level 2† \[86\] |
| Soil, farm or fecal contamination | Add high-dose penicillin G | 4 million units IV every 4 h† | With the first dose | TQIP 2015 \[87\]. EAST 2011† \[86\] |
| Open fracture: avoid | Fluoroquinolones |  |  | EAST 2011 Level 2: "no advantage"† \[86\] |
| Penetrating abdominal injury | One aerobic plus anaerobic agent†: cefoxitin, ertapenem, or cefazolin plus metronidazole | Cefoxitin 2 g. Ertapenem 1 g. Cefazolin 2 g plus metronidazole 500 mg† | Single preoperative dose†. Stop at 24 h with a hollow viscus injury | EAST 2012 Level I for 24 h \[84\] |
| Chest tube for traumatic hemothorax or pneumothorax | Cefazolin was the agent most studied | 2 g IV once† | "At the time of insertion" | EAST 2022, conditional \[85\] |

The gram-negative add-on for type III fractures is being questioned: a 1,041-patient Japanese cohort found no fewer deep infections with broad-spectrum coverage \[101\]. In hemorrhagic shock, the penetrating abdominal dose may need to be repeated after large-volume transfusion†.

**Tetanus** (CDC, updated September 2026) \[97\]

| Vaccination history | Clean, minor wound | Dirty or major wound (open fracture, gunshot, stab, crush, bite, burn) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown, or fewer than 3 doses | Tdap or Td | Tdap or Td **plus** tetanus immune globulin 250 units IM |
| 3 or more doses | Tdap or Td if the last dose was 10 or more years ago | Tdap or Td if the last dose was **5** or more years ago. Immune globulin only for HIV or severe immunodeficiency |

Either Td or Tdap can be used since the 2019 ACIP update, and Tdap is the one to choose if the patient has never had it† \[98\]. The TQIP orthopedic guideline (2015) prints a 10-year trigger for open fractures, which conflicts with CDC, as an open fracture is a dirty wound \[87\], \[97\]. Antibiotics do not prevent tetanus \[97\].

**After splenectomy** (CDC adult schedule, 2025 version, the latest found) \[99\]

| Vaccine | Schedule |
| --- | --- |
| Pneumococcal | One dose of PCV20 or PCV21 |
| Meningococcal ACWY | 2 doses at least 8 weeks apart, then every 5 years |
| Meningococcal B | Full series per brand, booster at 1 year, then every 2 to 3 years |
| Haemophilus influenzae type b | One dose if not previously vaccinated |

Give them about 14 days after surgery, as functional antibody response was better at day 14 than at day 1 or 7 in a trauma trial, or at discharge if the patient may not come back† \[100\]. That trial used the older polysaccharide vaccine, so applying day 14 to conjugate vaccines is an extrapolation.

## Analgesia, sedation and RSI

No US trauma society gives a strong analgesia recommendation, so the practical US source is the ACS TQIP Acute Pain guideline (2020, consensus, ungraded): a scheduled acetaminophen and NSAID backbone, opioids for breakthrough, and analgesic-dose ketamine or fentanyl first in the unstable ER patient \[72\].

**Analgesia chart** (TQIP Table 7 \[72\] unless marked)

| Drug | Dose | Maximum | Cautions in trauma |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acetaminophen | 1,000 mg every 6 h, PO or IV. "Scheduled dosing is ideal" | 4 g/day (2 to 3 g/day in older adults) | Liver disease |
| Ibuprofen | 400 mg every 6 h | 2,400 mg/day | Active bleeding, AKI. "Most practitioners avoid" NSAIDs with TBI or intracranial hemorrhage |
| Ketorolac | 10 mg every 6 h | 40 mg/day, 5 days | Same. Doses above 10 mg add no analgesia (ceiling effect) |
| Ketamine, analgesic dose | 0.3 mg/kg bolus, then 0.1 mg/kg/h. Dose on ideal body weight if obese | 0.5 mg/kg bolus, 1 mg/kg/h | Emergence reactions. Safe in TBI per recent reviews |
| Fentanyl | 25 to 50 mcg IV every 30 to 60 min |  | Minimal hemodynamic effect, which is why it leads in the unstable patient |
| Lidocaine IV | 1.5 mg/kg bolus | 200 mg/dose | Never with a running amide regional block (systemic toxicity) |
| Methocarbamol | 1,000 mg every 8 h | 4 g/day | Shorter stay in closed rib fractures |
| Gabapentin | 300 mg every 8 h | 1,800 mg/day | "Limited evidence" for broad benefit. Adds respiratory depression with opioids |

Short, early NSAID courses do not increase fracture nonunion, but courses of 2 weeks or longer should be avoided \[72\]. Extended-release opioids, meperidine and codeine should not be used for acute trauma pain, and every patient on an opioid gets a stimulant laxative \[72\].

**Rib fractures**

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| EAST 2016 \[70\] | "We conditionally recommend epidural analgesia and multimodal analgesia" (low quality). Replaces the 2005 guideline that favored epidurals |
| EAST and Chest Wall Injury Society 2023, age 65 or older \[71\] | "We offer no recommendation for or against ketamine, epidural, or other locoregional analgesia." Conditionally recommends noninvasive ventilation (pneumonia odds down 85%) |
| Carver, J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2019 (n=91) \[73\] | Ketamine infusion: no better 24-hour pain score. Less opioid only with ISS above 15 (35.7 vs 68 OME in the first 24 h) |
| SABRE, JAMA Surg 2024 (n=210) \[74\] | ER serratus anterior block: pain goal met in 41% vs 19.6%. 24-hour opioid 45 vs 91 MME. No change in pneumonia |

A great way to say it is that fascial plane blocks cut early pain and usually opioid use, but no block has yet changed pneumonia or survival.

**RSI in trauma**

The only trauma airway guideline is EAST 2012, which names "no pharmacologic induction agent of choice" and still recommends succinylcholine \[75\]. The 2026 RSI trial **excluded patients with a primary diagnosis of trauma**, so its results (equal mortality, more cardiovascular collapse with ketamine: 22.1% vs 17.0%) are an extrapolation for us \[47\].

| Drug | Usual RSI dose (etomidate and ketamine from the RSI trial, the rest standard values†) | In hemorrhagic shock† | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Etomidate | 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg \[47\] | 0.1 to 0.2 mg/kg | Less peri-intubation collapse than ketamine in critically ill adults \[47\] |
| Ketamine | 1 to 2 mg/kg \[47\] | 0.5 to 1 mg/kg | Not the automatic "safe shock drug" anymore |
| Propofol | 1 to 2 mg/kg | Avoid, or 0.5 mg/kg or less | Vasodilation and myocardial depression |
| Rocuronium | 1.2 mg/kg | 1.2 to 1.6 mg/kg (slow circulation delays onset) | Preferred whenever succinylcholine is risky. Sugammadex 16 mg/kg reverses† |
| Succinylcholine | 1.5 mg/kg | 1.5 to 2 mg/kg | Avoid after about 24 to 72 h† in burns, crush injury, spinal cord injury and denervation, and with hyperkalemia |

Resuscitate before we intubate: blood running, a pressor drawn up, and analgesia started promptly after rocuronium so the patient is never paralyzed and awake†. Typical starts are fentanyl 25 to 100 mcg/h and propofol 5 to 50 mcg/kg/min, at the low end in shock†.

## Traumatic brain injury: ER algorithm

&#91;embedded content: traumatic brain injury · ER algorithm, 7 steps\]

The sequence is a synthesis of BTF \[31\], BTF Prehospital \[33\], ACS TQIP \[34\] and SIBICC Tier 0 to 1 \[35\], not a published algorithm. Steps 1 and 2 are highlighted because a single hypoxic or hypotensive episode is the injury we can actually prevent, and steps 3 to 6 essentially all run through pharmacy.

## TBI targets and drug chart

In TBI the second hit (one episode of hypotension or hypoxia) is linked to worse outcomes, so our job is to protect pressure, oxygen, CO2 and sodium while the neurosurgeons decide. The Brain Trauma Foundation 4th edition (2016) is still the current adult guideline, and BTF has said it will not write a 5th edition \[31\]. The ACS TQIP TBI guideline (2024) is the most pharmacy-dense US document \[34\].

**Targets in the first hours**

| Parameter | Target | Source and grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systolic BP | 110 mm Hg or more | TQIP \[34\]. BTF Prehospital: weak, "optimal targets may be higher" \[33\]. BTF age bands (100 or more at ages 50 to 69, 110 or more otherwise): Level III \[31\] |
| Oxygen saturation | 94% or more (above 90% at the least) | TQIP \[34\]. BTF Prehospital: strong for above 90% \[33\] |
| CO2 | PaCO2 35 to 45, EtCO2 35 to 45 mm Hg | TQIP \[34\]. BTF Prehospital: strong \[33\] |
| Hyperventilation | Only for signs of herniation, to EtCO2 30 to 35. No prophylactic hyperventilation, and prolonged PaCO2 of 25 or less is specifically not recommended | BTF Prehospital: strong \[33\]. BTF: Level IIB against prophylaxis \[31\] |
| ICP and CPP (once monitored) | ICP below 22. CPP 60 to 70 mm Hg | BTF: Level IIB \[31\] |
| Sodium and osmolality | Sodium 135 to 145. Stop hyperosmolar therapy at sodium 155 or osmolality 320 | TQIP and SIBICC \[34\], \[35\] |
| Glucose, temperature | 100 to 180 mg/dL. 36.0 to 37.9 °C, "treat fever aggressively" | TQIP \[34\] |
| Coagulation | INR 1.4 or less. Platelets 75,000 or more | TQIP \[34\] |
| Hemoglobin | TQIP says 7 g/dL or more. TRAIN (2024) favored transfusing below 9 | \[34\], \[44\] |

The pressure target is not arbitrary: in 3,844 patients with major TBI, every 10 mm Hg rise in prehospital SBP (from 40 up to 119) lowered the adjusted odds of death (OR 0.81 per 10 mm Hg) with no safe threshold \[55\].

| Inaba, J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2013 (n=813, nonrandomized) \[41\] | Levetiracetam vs phenytoin: early seizures 1.5% vs 1.5% |
| Bulger, JAMA 2010 (n=1,331) \[38\] | Prehospital 7.5% saline: no better 6-month outcome (P=.67) |
| COBI, JAMA 2021 (n=370) \[37\] | Continuous 20% saline: no better 6-month outcome (OR 1.02) |
| CRASH-3, Lancet 2019 (n=12,737) \[36\] | TXA within 3 h: head-injury death 18.5% vs 19.8% (RR 0.94). Mild to moderate RR 0.78 |

CRASH-4 (500 mg IM TXA in older adults with mild TBI) and BOOST-3 (brain oxygen monitoring) have not reported yet \[50\]. Antibiotics are not indicated for an EVD or ICP monitor, a skull-base fracture, a CSF leak or pneumocephalus, and an open skull fracture gets at most 3 days \[34\].

## Spinal cord injury

The MAP target for acute spinal cord injury came down in 2024, and the first randomized trial (2025) suggests higher targets mostly add harm. Steroids remain "not recommended" by the US neurosurgical societies \[57\], \[61\], \[64\].

**What the guidelines say**

| Topic | AANS/CNS 2013 \[56\], \[57\], \[58\] | AO Spine 2017 and AO Spine/Praxis 2024 \[59\], \[60\], \[61\], \[62\] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MAP target | 85 to 90 mm Hg for the first 7 days (Level III). Now superseded | "At least 75-80 mmHg but not higher than 90-95 mmHg" for 3 to 7 days (2024: weak, very low quality). AO Spine 2026 restates it as conditional \[63\] |
| Hypotension | Correct SBP below 90 "as soon as possible" (Level III) | Re-endorsed by 84% of the 2024 panel |
| Pressor choice | None named | No recommendation. Left to the treating physician |
| Methylprednisolone | "Not recommended" (Level I). Not FDA approved for this use, with Class I to III evidence of harm | 2017: 24-h infusion within 8 h may be offered "as a treatment option" (weak, moderate). Not after 8 h, and not the 48-h regimen (weak) |
| GM-1 ganglioside | "Not recommended" (Level I) |  |
| Surgery timing |  | Within 24 h, now "recommended" (2024: strong, moderate) |
| VTE prophylaxis | LMWH, rotating beds or both (Level I). Start within 72 h and continue 3 months (Level II). No routine IVC filter (Level III) | LMWH or fixed low-dose UFH, started within 72 h (2017: weak) |

The 2024 upper limit means "avoiding medical augmentation of MAP above 90-95", not treating a spontaneous MAP of 95 with antihypertensives \[61\].

**Neurogenic shock and bradycardia**

Neurogenic shock (SBP below 100 with HR below 80 in one series) occurred in 19.3% of 490 acute SCI patients, and every patient with a severe cervical injury in the Yale series had prolonged bradycardia \[56\]. Hemorrhage has to be ruled out first, as the patient with a broken neck can also have a broken spleen†.

| Problem | Drug | Dose | Source and grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hypotension with bradycardia (cervical or high thoracic) | Norepinephrine (alpha plus beta-1)† | Titrate to MAP at least 75 to 80, not above 90 to 95 | No human SCI data on norepinephrine. Dopamine raised intrathecal pressure and caused more cardiac complications than phenylephrine \[61\] |
| Hypotension with normal heart rate (lower thoracic) | Phenylephrine | Titrate | Pure alpha-1, so reflex bradycardia can worsen a cervical injury† |
| Symptomatic bradycardia | Atropine | 0.5 to 1 mg IV every 3 to 5 min, maximum 3 mg | ACC/AHA/HRS 2018: Class IIa \[65\] |
| Recurrent bradycardia after SCI | Aminophylline | 6 mg/kg in 100 to 200 mL over 20 to 30 min | ACC/AHA/HRS 2018: Class IIa, C-LD, written specifically for acute SCI \[65\] |
| Same, enteral | Theophylline | 5 to 10 mg/kg/day orally, titrated | Same. Works at levels below the usual 10 to 20 mcg/mL \[65\] |
| Refractory bradycardia | Isoproterenol, epinephrine, or temporary pacing | Isoproterenol 1 to 20 mcg/min. Epinephrine 2 to 10 mcg/min | Pacing Class IIa \[65\] |
| Suctioning or intubation | Atropine or glycopyrrolate at the bedside |  | Vagal bradycardia and asystole with suctioning† |

**Steroid dosing, if a team chooses it** \[59\], \[66\]

Methylprednisolone 30 mg/kg IV bolus (over 15 min, then a 45-min pause†), then 5.4 mg/kg/h for 23 h, started within 8 hours of injury. If used, we own the insulin, GI prophylaxis and infection watch that come with it, and we should flag any patient with a moderate or severe TBI, as steroids raised death in CRASH \[46\], \[57\].

**Pharmacy pearls**

| Pearl | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Succinylcholine | Low risk in the first day or two, then avoid for months because of hyperkalemia from receptor upregulation (peak at 7 to 10 days)†. Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg is the alternative |
| Peripheral pressor | Norepinephrine can run short term through a good proximal IV while central access is placed, per institutional policy† |
| Stress ulcer prophylaxis | While ventilated, in shock, or on high-dose steroids† |
| Bowel program | Start early. Opioids worsen the ileus of a denervated gut† |
| Riluzole | Investigational for SCI (100 mg twice daily for 24 h, then 50 mg twice daily for 13 days in RISCIS). AO Spine 2026 makes no recommendation \[63\], \[68\] |

**Key trials**

| Trial | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Sajdeya, JAMA Netw Open 2025 (n=92) \[64\] | MAP above 85 to 90 vs above 65 to 70 for 7 days: no better motor or sensory recovery. Ventilator days 9.44 vs 3.78. Respiratory complications 78% vs 39%. Underpowered, only 38 completed follow-up |
| NASCIS II, NEJM 1990 (n=487) \[66\] | Methylprednisolone within 8 h: 6-month motor change 16.0 vs 11.2, a post hoc subgroup. No benefit after 8 h |
| NASCIS III, JAMA 1997 (n=499) \[67\] | 48 h vs 24 h: more severe sepsis and pneumonia, borderline motor benefit only in the 3 to 8 h subgroup |
| AO Spine 2017 guideline evidence review \[59\] | Within 8 h: about 3 to 4 extra motor points. The 8-hour cut point was "chosen after data collection" |
| RISCIS, J Neurotrauma 2023 (n=193) \[68\] | Riluzole: missed its primary end point after stopping at 55% of planned enrollment |

A 3-arm US trial (MAP 65 or more vs 85 to 90 vs spinal cord perfusion pressure 65 or more) is now recruiting (NCT06451133).

## VTE prophylaxis across trauma, TBI and SCI

Trauma flips from bleeding to clotting within about 24 hours, so the default is enoxaparin started within 24 hours for most patients and within 72 hours for nearly all of them \[77\]. The two US documents (WTA 2020 and AAST/ACS 2022) are consensus without letter grades \[77\], \[78\].

**When to start**

| Injury | Start | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General trauma | As soon as possible, most within 24 h, almost all within 72 h. Hold for a hemoglobin drop above 2 g/dL in under 12 h or ongoing transfusion | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| TBI, low risk, nonoperative | Within 24 h, once the follow-up CT shows no progression | TQIP 2024 \[34\] |
| TBI, moderate or high risk | 24 to 48 h after injury with a stable CT. After craniotomy, 24 to 48 h with a stable postoperative CT | TQIP 2024 \[34\] |
| TBI with an ICP monitor | Do not hold for the monitor | TQIP 2024 \[34\] |
| Spinal cord injury | Within 72 h (AANS/CNS Level II, AO Spine weak). LMWH preferred over UFH (CSCM). Continue at least 8 weeks to 3 months | \[58\], \[60\], \[69\] |
| Solid organ injury, nonoperative | Within 24 h for most. Caution with grade IV to V injuries | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Before most surgery | Do not hold reflexively. Holding is "only indicated, with few exceptions, for brain or spine surgery" | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Epidural catheter | Prophylactic enoxaparin 12 h before placement or removal, resume 4 to 12 h after | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Cannot anticoagulate at all | Pneumatic compression. No prophylactic IVC filter | WTA 2020 \[77\]. Ho 2019 \[81\] |

Pneumatic compression goes on everyone at moderate or high risk from admission, whatever the drug plan \[77\].

**Dosing**

| Regimen | Dose | Who | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enoxaparin | 40 mg SC every 12 h | Age 18 to 65, weight above 50 kg, CrCl above 60. WTA calls it "the standard for most trauma patients" | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Enoxaparin | 30 mg SC every 12 h | Age above 65, weight below 50 kg, CrCl 30 to 60, and the starting dose in TBI and spine injury | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Enoxaparin, weight based | 0.5 mg/kg every 12 h | Obesity, with anti-Xa monitoring | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Anti-Xa target | Peak 0.2 to 0.4 IU/mL, or trough 0.1 to 0.2 IU/mL | Titrate | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Unfractionated heparin | 5,000 units SC every 8 h | CrCl below 30 or dialysis | WTA 2020 \[77\] |
| Aspirin | 81 mg PO twice daily | Operative extremity or pelvic fracture without other high-risk features | PREVENT CLOT \[79\] |

**Key trials**

| Trial | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Geerts, NEJM 1996 (n=344) \[80\] | Enoxaparin 30 mg vs UFH 5,000 units every 12 h: DVT 31% vs 44%, proximal DVT 6% vs 15% |
| PREVENT CLOT, NEJM 2023 (n=12,211) \[79\] | Aspirin 81 mg twice daily vs enoxaparin 30 mg twice daily after fracture surgery: 90-day death 0.78% vs 0.73% (noninferior). DVT 2.51% vs 1.71%. PE 1.49% in both |
| Ho, NEJM 2019 (n=240) \[81\] | Prophylactic IVC filter in severe trauma: PE or death 13.9% vs 14.4% (HR 0.99) |
| Niziolek, Trauma Surg Acute Care Open 2024 (n=282) \[82\] | Only 42.7% of trauma ICU patients reached target anti-Xa on 30 mg twice daily |
| AAST survey, 2023 \[83\] | Guideline-directed dosing 88.2% with a clinical pharmacist on the team vs 69.0% without |

That last row is essentially our job description: most trauma ICU patients in one cohort were underdosed on 30 mg twice daily, and teams with a pharmacist dose closer to the guideline. PREVENT CLOT enrolled a young population (mean age 44.6), so aspirin should not be stretched to TBI, spinal cord injury or older polytrauma patients \[79\].

## Special populations

**Older adults on antithrombotics**

Older adults are under-triaged because their vital signs mislead: beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers blunt the tachycardia, and a usual SBP of 130 to 150 makes a "normal" reading hide hypoperfusion \[105\].

| Point | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activate the trauma team for | Ground-level fall on any antithrombotic. SBP below 110. HR above 90. Shock index above 1 | ACS TQIP Geriatric 2023 \[105\] |
| Shock index | 1 or more was the strongest predictor of death in 217,190 patients aged 65 or older (OR 3.1). SBP and HR alone did not predict it | \[106\] |
| Reversal | Take an anticoagulant history from every older patient. Reverse for any intracranial bleed, and repeat the CT at 6 to 8 h | \[105\] |
| DOAC vs warfarin | DOACs carried less immediate intracranial bleeding (OR 0.58) and fewer neurosurgical procedures than warfarin | \[126\] |
| Delayed bleed after a negative CT | 2.43% on DOACs vs 2.31% on warfarin. Death from it 0.16% vs 0.48% | \[49\] |
| Medication review | Beers criteria. Continue drugs with withdrawal risk (SSRIs, chronic benzodiazepines, opioids, clonidine, antiepileptics). Avoid starting new benzodiazepines. "Continue a beta blocker or start one, if indicated" | \[105\] |
| Doses | Lower and slower: acetaminophen 2 to 3 g/day, enoxaparin 30 mg twice daily if over 65 | \[72\], \[77\] |

## The ER pharmacist's role in trauma

The ACS trauma center standards (2022, revised July 2025) never use the word pharmacist, yet every designated center must have a massive transfusion protocol and a rapid reversal protocol listing "therapeutic options and indications for the use of each reversal agent" \[113\]. Those protocols are ours to write and to run at the bedside.

**What we own and what we catch**

| Moment | What the pharmacist owns | Common error to catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | Time of injury, weight, anticoagulant history, prehospital drugs | TXA given twice (prehospital and ER). The 2025 joint statement asks that prehospital TXA be clearly communicated \[11\] |
| TXA | Premixed 1 g/100 mL bags. Stored away from local anesthetics | Wrong-route TXA: FDA now requires a boxed warning on inadvertent spinal injection \[120\]. TXA after 3 h |
| Massive transfusion | Calcium after every 2 units, on its own line | Calcium in the same line as blood. Chloride and gluconate treated as equal doses |
| Reversal | 4F-PCC dosed, reconstituted and running, plus IV vitamin K for warfarin | Andexanet still on an order set. Shaking the PCC vial. Infusing faster than 0.12 mL/kg/min (maximum 8.4 mL/min) \[27\] |
| Brain injury | Mannitol with an in-line filter, hypertonic saline by concentration and line | 23.4% saline through a peripheral IV outside policy. Peripheral boluses extravasated in 6.4% of 863 doses, without necrosis \[121\]. Steroids. Valproate |
| RSI | Shock-reduced induction doses drawn up, rocuronium, a pressor, post-intubation analgesia | Full induction dose in a bleeding patient. Succinylcholine more than 24 to 72 h after burns, crush or cord injury |
| Open wounds | Cefazolin within the first hour, tetanus status, immune globulin when needed | Tetanus at 10 years for a dirty wound. Fluoroquinolones for open fractures |
| First 24 to 72 h | Enoxaparin start time and dose, anti-Xa follow-up | Reflexive holds. 30 mg twice daily never rechecked |

## Notes on marked items

- **†** means the value came from a guideline summary, a review, standard teaching or recall of the source and was not confirmed in the primary document for this handout.
- **ATLS 11 \[3\], \[4\].** Only xABCDE and "exsanguinating hemorrhage first" were confirmed on ACS pages. Its fluid, TXA and whole-blood details are from a secondary review, and whether it kept the class I to IV hemorrhage table is unknown, as the manual is paywalled.
- **Blunt vs penetrating table.** A teaching synthesis, not a published table.
- **Protamine and desmopressin doses.** Protamine doses and the 2016 NCS/SCCM desmopressin dose (0.4 mcg/kg) are recalled, as that guideline was not read in full. The 0.3 mcg/kg figure is the older European and label-based dose. TQIP's 0.4 mcg/kg was confirmed \[34\].
- **Hypertonic saline and fosphenytoin.** The 3% 250 mL volume, central-line-only route for 23.4%, levetiracetam loading and fosphenytoin doses are standard practice values. No adult guideline prints them.
- **Spinal cord injury pearls.** The 15-minute bolus and 45-minute pause in the steroid regimen, phenylephrine reflex bradycardia, the succinylcholine window, peripheral norepinephrine, stress ulcer prophylaxis and the bowel program are standard teaching, not confirmed in a primary source.
- **RSI shock doses.** No trauma society prints reduced induction doses. The RSI trial excluded trauma \[47\].
- **Open fracture and penetrating abdominal doses.** EAST and TQIP name the timing and the spectrum but not the doses. EAST 2011 and AAOS 2022 wording was read through secondary sources only \[86\].
- **WTA algorithms 2023 to 2026.** Citations confirmed, content not read.
- **Uterine displacement timing.** The SOGC abstract confirms displacement, and the 20-week threshold is common teaching.

* **Checked against primary documents.** The European guideline (full text), the ACS Transfusion in Trauma guideline (November 2026, full PDF), ACS TQIP TBI (2024), Acute Pain (2020), Orthopaedic (2015) and Geriatric (2023) PDFs, the BTF 4th edition summary, the AO Spine/Praxis 2024 hemodynamic guideline, EAST 2022 tube thoracostomy, WTA 2020 VTE, the RSI trial and the CDC tetanus page were read in full. Every trial number in the key trials tables matches its PubMed abstract. Every journal reference was checked against PubMed on November 20.
* **New since our shock handout.** The ACS transfusion guideline (November 2026), the NCS/SCCM reversal update (September 2026), the 2026 whole-blood trials (SWiFT, TOWAR), EZPLAZ freeze-dried plasma (July 2026) and the FDA boxed warning for wrong-route TXA (2025).
* **Not yet reported.** CRASH-4, BOOST-3, CAVALIER, MATIC-2 and the 3-arm spinal cord MAP trial.
* **Site-specific items to confirm locally.** Whether TXA is stocked as premixed bags only, the 4F-PCC fixed-dose protocol, the massive transfusion protocol's calcium step, whether low-titer O whole blood is available, the levetiracetam order set, the enoxaparin default dose and anti-Xa workflow, and the open fracture antibiotic order set.
