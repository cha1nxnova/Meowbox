## 1. Drug chart: adult ED dosing by route, onset, duration, repeat dosing, maximum, cautions

### Takeaway
Only four products carry a US label for agitation (olanzapine IM, ziprasidone IM, inhaled loxapine, sublingual dexmedetomidine film), and all four are labeled only for agitation with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder; droperidol, midazolam, lorazepam, ketamine, IV olanzapine and haloperidol for undifferentiated agitation are off-label, with doses that come from trials rather than labels. The label maximum for droperidol (2.5 mg) and the label IM range for ketamine (6.5 to 13 mg/kg) do not match ED practice (droperidol 5 to 10 mg; ketamine 4 to 5 mg/kg IM), so the handout should show both.

### Inferences
- The film's evidence base (cooperative, mild-to-moderate, self-administering patients, no recent sedatives, normal hemodynamics) plus very low sales suggests it is a niche ED option, not a tool for the combative or intoxicated patient.
- Lorazepam IM is the slowest and longest-acting of the common options (32 min mean onset, 217 min to arousal in Nobay; 29% sedated at 15 min in Martel 2021), which argues for midazolam when a benzodiazepine is wanted IM.
- Ziprasidone IM is consistently slower than droperidol, olanzapine and midazolam across three Hennepin studies.

## 2. Key trials: exact headline numbers (all checked against PubMed abstracts)

### Takeaway
Across RCTs, IM midazolam and IM droperidol act fastest among conventional agents, IM olanzapine is close behind, and haloperidol, ziprasidone and lorazepam are slowest; adding midazolam to droperidol speeds sedation further, and ketamine is fastest of all at the cost of more airway events when used prehospital. Several abstracts (Klein 2018, Riddell 2017, Chan 2013, Taylor 2017) print differences rather than per-arm values, and two abstracts contain internal arithmetic inconsistencies that are flagged below.

### Cited Findings

### Hennepin (Minneapolis) program: IM comparative studies

- **Klein 2018 (IM midazolam, olanzapine, ziprasidone, haloperidol).** Prospective observational, consecutive ED patients, medication fixed in predetermined 3-week blocks: haloperidol 5 mg, ziprasidone 20 mg, olanzapine 10 mg, midazolam 5 mg, haloperidol 10 mg (all IM). n=737 (median age 40; 72% men). Primary outcome: proportion adequately sedated (Altered Mental Status Scale, AMSS <1) at 15 min. Abstract prints differences only, not the per-arm proportions: midazolam vs ziprasidone +18% (95% CI 6 to 29); vs haloperidol 5 mg +30% (19 to 41); vs haloperidol 10 mg +28% (17 to 39); vs olanzapine +9% (-1 to 20). Olanzapine vs haloperidol 5 mg +20% (10 to 31); vs haloperidol 10 mg +18% (7 to 29); vs ziprasidone +8% (-3 to 19). Adverse events (abstract, as printed): cardiac arrest 0; extrapyramidal effects 2 (0.3%); hypotension 5 (0.5%); hypoxemia 10 (1%); intubation 4 (0.5%); similar across groups. [verified: PubMed abstract] Klein LR, Driver BE, Miner JR, Martel ML, Hessel M, Collins JD, Horton GB, Fagerstrom E, Satpathy R, Cole JB. Ann Emerg Med. 2018;72(4):374-385. PMID 29885904. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2018.04.027)
  - NOTE: the abstract's AE percentages (5 = 0.5%, 4 = 0.5%) do not match n=737 arithmetically (5/737 = 0.7%); quote the counts. [verified: abstract as printed; arithmetic is mine]
  - Per-arm 15-min proportions and median times to sedation are NOT in the abstract (see Gaps).
- **Klein 2019 (rescue sedation, IM antipsychotics).** Retrospective, n=15,918 (median age 37; 75% male). Rescue sedation within 1 h: droperidol 547/4,947 (11%, 95% CI 10-12); olanzapine 988/8,825 (11%, 10-12); haloperidol 390/2,146 (18%, 17-20). Whole encounter: droperidol 17%, olanzapine 19%, haloperidol 26%. AEs: intubation 49 (0.3%), akathisia 7 (0.04%), dystonia 5 (0.03%), respiratory arrest 1 (0.006%), torsades 0. [verified: PubMed abstract] Klein LR, Driver BE, Horton G, Scharber S, Martel ML, Cole JB. J Emerg Med. 2019;56(5):484-490. PMID 30745194. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2018.12.036)
- **Martel 2021 (RCT; droperidol vs ziprasidone vs lorazepam IM).** Randomized double-blind, exception from informed consent, enrolled July 2004 to March 2005 (published 2021; e-pub Oct 2020). n=115. Arms IM: droperidol 5 mg, ziprasidone 10 mg, ziprasidone 20 mg, lorazepam 2 mg. Primary: adequately sedated (AMSS ≤0) at 15 min: droperidol 16/25 (64%); ziprasidone 10 mg 7/28 (25%); ziprasidone 20 mg 11/31 (35%); lorazepam 9/31 (29%). Abstract's pairwise differences: droperidol vs ziprasidone 20 mg "39% (95% CI 3% to 54%)"; vs lorazepam 33% (8% to 58%). No significant difference in rescue sedation. Respiratory depression (ETCO2 change or SpO2 <90%): droperidol 3/25 (12%); ziprasidone 10 mg 10/28 (36%); ziprasidone 20 mg 12/31 (39%); lorazepam 15/31 (48%). One intubation (ziprasidone 20 mg; acute subdural hematoma). No ventricular dysrhythmias; QTc similar. [verified: PubMed abstract] Martel ML, Driver BE, Miner JR, Biros MH, Cole JB. Acad Emerg Med. 2021;28(4):421-434. PMID 32888340. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.14124)

[verified: PubMed abstract] Cole JB, Stang JL, DeVries PA, Martel ML, Miner JR, Driver BE. Ann Emerg Med. 2021;78(2):274-286. PMID 33846015. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2021.01.005)

## 3. Droperidol: boxed warning, QT data, positions, availability

### Takeaway
The 2001 boxed warning remains on the label and restricts the labeled use to perioperative nausea at 2.5 mg, but ED cohorts totalling tens of thousands of patients at 5 to 10 mg show QT prolongation and torsades to be rare (1 case in 16,546; 0 in 1,009 with ECGs at 10 mg). ACEP's 2023 policy and a 2026 toxicology review both place droperidol first line.

### Cited Findings
- FDA required the black box warning in 2001 "in response to case reports of QT prolongation and potential fatal arrhythmias". [verified: abstract] — [Gaw 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2019.09.007)
- Current label wording: droperidol "should be reserved for use in the treatment of patients who fail to show an acceptable response to other adequate treatments"; "all patients should undergo a 12-lead ECG prior to administration"; do not give if QTc >440 ms (males) or >450 ms (females); if given despite risk, ECG monitoring before and for 2 to 3 h after; extra caution with age over 65, alcohol abuse, benzodiazepines, IV opiates. [verified: label] — [droperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=147e033d-d997-4ef6-8bb5-a9ba372590b2)
- Label dose-response QT data: 0.1, 0.175 and 0.25 mg/kg prolonged median QTc by 37, 44 and 59 ms in 40 surgical patients. [verified: label] — [droperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=147e033d-d997-4ef6-8bb5-a9ba372590b2)
- Label maximum initial adult dose 2.5 mg IM or slow IV with 1.25 mg increments; children 2 to 12 y, 0.1 mg/kg. [verified: label] — [droperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=147e033d-d997-4ef6-8bb5-a9ba372590b2)
- Calver 2015: abnormal QT 13/1,009 (1.3%), 7 with another cause; no torsades; median dose 10 mg. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2015.03.016)
- Cole 2020: 1 torsades case in 16,546 patients (0.006%); mean QTc 424.3 to 427.6 ms in noncritical paired ECGs. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2020.4.47036)
- Gaw 2020: 6,353 visits, no deaths within 24 h attributable to droperidol, no fatal arrhythmias, akathisia 2.9%. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2019.09.007)
- Klein 2019: 0 torsades among 15,918 patients given IM droperidol, olanzapine or haloperidol. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2018.12.036)
- Availability: droperidol "again became widely available in the US in early 2019" after "a six-year hiatus"; it was unavailable again at Hennepin Nov 2019 to Mar 2020. [verified: abstracts] — [Cole 2020](https://doi.org/10.5811/westjem.2020.4.47036); [Cole 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2021.01.005)
- Two US droperidol injection labels are currently listed (American Regent, version Mar 2023; Hikma, Jun 2022), 2.5 mg/mL. [verified: DailyMed listing] — [droperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=147e033d-d997-4ef6-8bb5-a9ba372590b2)
- Dose: 5 mg vs 10 mg initial dose, rescue at 1 h 9.6% vs 14.8% (confounded by indication). Older adults: time to sedation 30 min (10 mg) vs 21 min (5 mg), P=.55; authors advise a 5 mg start. [verified: abstracts] — [Cole 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2024.07.005); [Calver 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2013.03.026)
- ACEP 2023 clinical policy, Level B: use droperidol plus midazolam, or an atypical antipsychotic plus midazolam, for more rapid and efficacious treatment of severe agitation; if a single agent, droperidol or an atypical antipsychotic. [secondary: digests of the policy; the ACEP page returned HTTP 403] — [JournalFeed digest](https://journalfeed.org/article-a-day/2024/when-calm-down-doesnt-worknew-acep-severe-agitation-policy/); [ACEP Now](https://acepnow.com/article/aceps-new-clinical-policy-on-severe-agitation); policy citation [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2023.09.010)
- Isoardi 2026: IM droperidol first line, repeat at 15 min. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2591356)
- Project BETA 2012 took the opposite view ("it is prudent for clinicians to avoid using droperidol for agitation"), written before the ED safety cohorts. [secondary: machine summary of full text] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/)

### Inferences
- A pharmacist-facing position: the label requirement for a pre-dose ECG is usually impossible in a combative patient; the cohort data support giving the drug and obtaining an ECG once the patient is safe, with closer monitoring in those with known QT risk. This is my synthesis; Cole 2020 itself concludes ECG monitoring "is useful only in high-risk patients".

### Gaps
- [recalled, not confirmed]: the AAEM position statement on droperidol (Perkins et al., J Emerg Med 2015) supporting doses up to 10 mg IM without mandatory ECG; the 2013 to 2019 US supply gap was a manufacturer discontinuation/shortage rather than an FDA withdrawal. Neither was read this session.
- Exact ACEP recommendation wording and evidence grades were not read in the primary document.

## 4. Olanzapine IM plus parenteral benzodiazepine

### Takeaway
The US label says the combination "is not recommended" because of excessive sedation and cardiorespiratory depression, but the supporting data are post-marketing reports, and the largest ED comparison (n=693) found no difference in intubation, hypoxemia or hypotension; ACEP's 2023 policy recommends an atypical antipsychotic with midazolam.

### Cited Findings
- Label: "Concomitant administration of intramuscular olanzapine and parenteral benzodiazepine is not recommended due to the potential for excessive sedation and cardiorespiratory depression." [verified: label] — [Zyprexa label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b418946a-1ab4-4d89-a012-d94fc361a3c4)
- Label interaction study: IM lorazepam 2 mg given 1 h after IM olanzapine 5 mg did not change the pharmacokinetics of either drug but "added to the somnolence observed with either drug alone". [verified: label] — [Zyprexa label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b418946a-1ab4-4d89-a012-d94fc361a3c4)
- Cole 2024: intubation 21/549 (3.8%) olanzapine-only vs 5/144 (3.5%) olanzapine + benzodiazepine; difference 0.3% (95% CI -3.0 to 3.7); hypoxemia 2% vs 3%; hypotension 9% vs 9%. The abstract notes the European label says to separate the drugs by 60 min and that ACEP recommends co-administration. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.05.006)
- Williams 2018: 91 co-administrations (41 within 60 min), no hypotension, bradycardia, bradypnea or desaturation; warning dates from 2005 post-marketing fatality reports. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.9740/mhc.2018.09.208)
- Chan 2013: IV olanzapine 5 mg before IV midazolam shortened time to sedation with adverse events no different from midazolam alone (n=336). [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2012.07.118)
- Dissent: Isoardi 2026 advises against antipsychotic + benzodiazepine combinations in general; BETA 2012 said the olanzapine + benzodiazepine combination "should be avoided", citing desaturation in alcohol-intoxicated patients; pediatric BETA says no benzodiazepine within 1 h of olanzapine. [verified: abstract for Isoardi; secondary for the two BETA papers] — [Isoardi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2591356); [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/); [Gerson 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6404720/)

### Inferences
- Cole 2024 is retrospective, single-center, and the confidence interval allows up to a 3.7-point excess in intubation, so it reduces but does not remove concern; caution is most defensible in heavily alcohol-intoxicated or older patients.

### Gaps
- No "Cole 2023" paper on this question was found; the relevant paper is Cole 2024 (Ann Emerg Med 84:658).

## 5. Haloperidol: IV QT warning, EPS/dystonia, akathisia, NMS

### Takeaway
Haloperidol is the slowest common IM option and the one with a label contraindication in Parkinson disease and Lewy body dementia; IV use is unapproved and needs ECG monitoring. Measured EPS and dystonia rates in ED agitation cohorts are low (well under 1%), which weakens the case for routine diphenhydramine or benztropine prophylaxis.

### Cited Findings
- Label: "Higher than recommended doses of any formulation and intravenous administration of haloperidol injection appear to be associated with a higher risk of QTc interval-prolongation and Torsades de Pointes... HALOPERIDOL INJECTION IS NOT APPROVED FOR INTRAVENOUS ADMINISTRATION. If haloperidol injection is administered intravenously, the ECG should be monitored". [verified: label] — [haloperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1ad2abd-a9d6-44e3-957b-36a73260f4f5)
- Label: contraindicated in Parkinson disease and dementia with Lewy bodies (severe EPS, confusion, sedation, falls). [verified: label] — [haloperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1ad2abd-a9d6-44e3-957b-36a73260f4f5)
- BETA 2012: avoid IV haloperidol where possible; if used IV, limit to 5 to 10 mg/day with continuous ECG monitoring. [secondary: machine summary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/)
- EPS rates: 2/737 (0.3%) across all IM arms (Klein 2018); akathisia 7 (0.04%) and dystonia 5 (0.03%) among 15,918 (Klein 2019); droperidol EPS 6/538 (1%) vs olanzapine 1/719 (0.1%) (Cole 2021); droperidol akathisia 2.9% (Gaw 2020); 1 dystonia in the haloperidol arm of Chan 2021. [verified: abstracts] — [Klein 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2018.04.027); [Klein 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2018.12.036); [Cole 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2021.01.005); [Gaw 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2019.09.007); [Chan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2021.100751)
- Prophylactic diphenhydramine: no reduction in rescue dosing and more hypotension and desaturation (Jeffers 2022); patients without diphenhydramine got an antimuscarinic within 2 days more often (15 vs 6) but none had documented EPS. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2022.01.009)
- Dystonia treatment: benztropine 1 to 2 mL IM or IV "usually relieves the condition quickly". [verified: label] — [benztropine label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f92fbafa-314d-4449-831f-26108339e6b2)
- NMS features as listed in antipsychotic labeling: hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability, with possible raised creatine kinase, myoglobinuria and acute renal failure. [verified: label] — [Geodon label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8326928a-2cb6-4f7f-9712-03a425a14c37)
- IM QTc comparison in the Geodon label: haloperidol 7.5 mg then 10 mg IM raised QTc by 6.0 then 14.7 ms; ziprasidone 20 mg then 30 mg by 4.6 then 12.8 ms. [verified: label] — [Geodon label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8326928a-2cb6-4f7f-9712-03a425a14c37)

### Inferences
- ED cohort EPS rates rely on chart documentation during short stays and will undercount delayed dystonia (which can appear after discharge) and akathisia.

### Gaps
- The 2007 FDA alert on IV haloperidol was not retrieved; the current label text above is what I verified. [recalled]: the alert was issued September 2007.
- [recalled, not confirmed]: diphenhydramine 25 to 50 mg IV/IM for acute dystonia with 2 to 3 days of oral follow-on; akathisia treatment with dose reduction, a benzodiazepine, propranolol, or diphenhydramine; NMS management (stop the drug, cooling, benzodiazepines, dantrolene or bromocriptine in severe cases).

## 6. Ketamine for severe agitation

### Takeaway
Ketamine 4 to 5 mg/kg IM sedates in about 5 minutes, faster than any comparator, but intubation followed in 39 to 63% of prehospital cohorts versus about 2% when it was given in the ED, with marked clinician-to-clinician variation. Guidance places it as rescue or for immediate danger, not routine first line.

### Cited Findings
- Speed: median 5.8 vs 14.7 min (Barbic 2021, ED RCT); 5 vs 17 min (Cole 2016); 4.2 min (Cole 2018); mean 7.73 vs 11.42 min (Heydari 2018); 15 vs 36 min (Lin 2021). [verified: abstracts] — [Barbic](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2021.05.023); [Cole 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2016.1177652); [Cole 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2017.10.022); [Heydari](https://doi.org/10.29252/beat-060404); [Lin](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2020.04.013)
- Intubation: prehospital 39% (Cole 2016), 57% (Cole 2018), 63% (Olives 2016); pooled 30.5% overall but 1.8% (95% CI 0.0-4.4) when given in the ED and 4.9% in air transport (Mankowitz 2018). Dose was not associated with intubation; one physician performed 36% of intubations in Cole 2018. [verified: abstracts] — [Olives](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1049023X16000819); [Mankowitz](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2018.07.017)
- Other adverse effects: hypersalivation 38% and 18%; laryngospasm 5% (3/55); emergence 10% and 4%; vomiting 9% and 6% (Cole 2016, 2018). Pooled: vomiting 5.2%, hypertension 12.1%, emergence 3.5%, transient hypoxia 1.8%, laryngospasm 1.3% (Mankowitz). Serious adverse events 12.5% vs 5.0% in Barbic (CI crosses 0). [verified: abstracts] — same DOIs as above
- Rescue use: after failed droperidol, median 300 mg IM sedated 90%; 4 of 5 failures got ≤200 mg; authors suggest 4 to 5 mg/kg. [verified: abstract] — [Isbister 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2015.11.028)
- Label: emergence delirium in about 12%, lower with IM; laryngospasm and airway obstruction listed; respiratory depression with overdose or rapid IV push (give over 60 s); salivation, consider antisialagogue; label IM range 6.5 to 13 mg/kg and IV 1 to 4.5 mg/kg are anesthetic doses. [verified: label] — [ketamine label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bb912318-2e22-4469-b0a2-774803ee1bb8)
- ACEP 2023 policy: ketamine IV or IM "may be considered" for rapid control when safety is a concern (Level C, consensus); no recommendation for or against specific agents out of hospital, and none for patients over 65. [secondary] — [ACEP Now](https://acepnow.com/article/which-sedatives-are-best-for-managing-severe-agitation-in-the-emergency-department); [JournalFeed](https://journalfeed.org/article-a-day/2024/when-calm-down-doesnt-worknew-acep-severe-agitation-policy/)
- Joint ASA/ACEP statement, 26 Aug 2020: the societies "firmly oppose the use of ketamine or any other sedative/hypnotic agent to chemically incapacitate someone solely for a law enforcement purpose and not for a legitimate medical reason". [secondary: search rendering of the ASA news release] — [ASA newsroom](https://asahq.org/about-asa/newsroom/news-releases/2020/08/american-college-of-emergency-physicians-and-american-society-of-anesthesiologists-issue-joint-statement-on-ketamine-use)
- ACEP withdrew its 2009 "excited delirium" white paper in October 2023; its 2021 report used "hyperactive delirium with severe agitation"; the AMA and APA also oppose the term as a diagnosis. [secondary] — [ACEP Now](https://acepnow.com/article/acep-rejects-excited-delirium/2); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/new-ama-policy-opposes-excited-delirium-diagnosis)
- Isoardi 2026: ketamine for rescue or "extremely dangerous scenarios when immediate control is required"; monitor with continuous pulse oximetry and capnography where available. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2591356)

### Inferences
- The prehospital-to-ED gap in intubation likely reflects receiving-clinician practice and patient mix as much as pharmacology (dose did not predict intubation; one clinician accounted for a third of intubations).
- Weight-based IM dosing with estimated weights and two vial concentrations is an obvious error risk; a pharmacist-built fixed-volume table is a reasonable handout point (my suggestion, not sourced).

### Gaps
- Dosing-error data: nothing retrieved. [recalled, not confirmed]: US ketamine vials come as 10, 50 and 100 mg/mL, and IM dosing needs the concentrated vials; reports of overdoses from weight overestimation in EMS reviews.
- No study comparing 2 mg/kg vs 4 to 5 mg/kg IM for agitation was found; Türkücü 2025 (IV 0.5 vs 1 mg/kg) was procedural sedation, not agitation. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.15116)
- The 2021 ACEP hyperactive delirium task force report was not read.

## 7. Selection by cause and special populations

### Takeaway
Guidance agrees on treating the cause: benzodiazepines for withdrawal and (traditionally) stimulant toxicity, antipsychotics for psychosis and alcohol intoxication, low-dose antipsychotic and no benzodiazepine for delirium in older adults, and no haloperidol in Parkinson disease or Lewy body dementia. Newer data blur the stimulant rule: antipsychotics performed as well as midazolam and better than lorazepam in methamphetamine agitation.

### Cited Findings
- **Alcohol intoxication.** BETA: use medication sparingly; avoid benzodiazepines (respiratory depression); antipsychotic preferred, haloperidol having the longest record. [secondary: machine summary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/). Droperidol gave the shortest ED stay (499 vs 524 haloperidol vs 533 min olanzapine) in 11,787 intoxicated patients. [verified: abstract] — [Cole 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.13486). Oral olanzapine or lorazepam worked as fast as IM in a 73% alcohol-intoxicated cohort. [verified: abstract] — [Cole 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.51683)
- **Stimulants (methamphetamine, cocaine).** BETA: benzodiazepines first line; add a second-generation antipsychotic for psychosis in chronic amphetamine users. [secondary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/). Connors 2019: among 96 cocaine and 330 amphetamine cases given antipsychotics, "neither a clear benefit of antipsychotics over benzodiazepines nor a definitive signal of harm". Richards 2015 recommends butyrophenones, later-generation antipsychotics and benzodiazepines. [verified: abstracts] — [Connors](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2019.01.001); [Richards](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2015.01.040). Methamphetamine data: IV midazolam-droperidol sedated 85.3% at 10 min vs 46.7% droperidol vs 50.0% olanzapine (Yap 2017); IM droperidol, olanzapine and midazolam similar (16, 16, 13 min), lorazepam slower (29 min, 46% rescue) (Cole 2026). [verified: abstracts] — [Yap](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13780); [Cole 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2026.02.027)
- **Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal.** "A benzodiazepine is the agent of choice". [secondary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/). Isoardi 2026 also reserves benzodiazepines for withdrawal and stimulant intoxication. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2591356)
- **Anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) delirium.** Akorn, the sole US physostigmine maker, ceased operations in February 2023; FDA has allowed temporary import of Anticholium (2 mg/5 mL, 0.4 mg/mL, versus the former US 1 mg/mL 2 mL ampule), distributed only by Provepharm/Direct Success; the product is not FDA approved. [verified: imported-product label for strength, importer and unapproved status; secondary for the Akorn date] — [Anticholium label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b90b50e8-c8f1-4f2d-be6e-4d269b8c362c); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/pharmacy/4-drugs-are-discontinued-after-us-drugmaker-closed.html). An ASHP bulletin dated 3 Mar 2026 reportedly said remaining import stock was one lot expiring May 2026 with no further imports expected; I could not open ASHP to confirm, and the DailyMed import label was last updated 17 Nov 2025. [secondary] — [ASHP shortage detail](https://www.ashp.org/drug-shortages/current-shortages/drug-shortage-detail.aspx?id=721). Anticholium dosing: 0.04 mg/kg slow IV at about 1 mg/min, maximum single dose 2 mg, repeat no sooner than 5 to 20 min. [verified: label] — [Anticholium label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b90b50e8-c8f1-4f2d-be6e-4d269b8c362c). Rivastigmine alternative: 3 mg PO every 2 h until controlled, maximum 12 mg, then 3 to 6 mg every 12 h for 24 h, or a 9.5 or 13.3 mg/24 h patch; delay physostigmine until a wide QRS is narrowed with bicarbonate; keep atropine at the bedside. [secondary: poison center guidance, Dec 2023] — [Missouri Poison Center](https://missouripoisoncenter.org/updated-treatment-for-anticholinergic-delirium-physostigmine-rivastigmine/). A 2026 case report calls symptom control with standard-dose patches "equivocal" and used 26.6 mg/24 h. [verified: abstract] — [Watson 2026](https://doi.org/10.5811/cpcem.49089)
- **Psychosis / known psychiatric illness.** Antipsychotics preferred over benzodiazepines; oral first if cooperative; IM olanzapine or ziprasidone if not; if one antipsychotic dose is insufficient, add lorazepam rather than more antipsychotic (except with olanzapine). [secondary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/). Haloperidol 5 mg + lorazepam 2 mg was non-inferior to olanzapine 10 mg IM (PANSS-EC -9.9 vs -10.2 at 2 h). [verified: abstract] — [Huang 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfma.2015.01.018). Labeled noninjectable options: inhaled loxapine and sublingual dexmedetomidine (section 1).
- **Delirium and older adults.** BETA: benzodiazepines "should be generally avoided because they can exacerbate the delirium"; second-generation antipsychotic preferred, low-dose haloperidol acceptable; fix the medical cause first. [secondary] — [Wilson 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298219/). Older-adult safety review: any adverse event 16.8%; midazolam 53% (OR 5.25 vs haloperidol); quetiapine lowest (OR 0.27). [verified: abstract] — [Casey 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jgs.19485). Label dose reductions: olanzapine IM 5 mg geriatric, 2.5 mg debilitated; Igalmi 120 mcg at ≥65; droperidol "appropriately reduced in elderly". [verified: labels] — [Zyprexa](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b418946a-1ab4-4d89-a012-d94fc361a3c4). Droperidol in patients over 65: 5 mg start (Calver 2013). [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2013.03.026). ACEP 2023 made no recommendation for patients over 65 for lack of data. [secondary] — [JournalFeed](https://journalfeed.org/article-a-day/2024/when-calm-down-doesnt-worknew-acep-severe-agitation-policy/)
- **Dementia.** All antipsychotics carry the boxed warning for increased mortality in dementia-related psychosis (1.6 to 1.7 times placebo; about 4.5% vs 2.6% over 10 weeks per class labeling). [verified: label for the 1.6 to 1.7 and 4.5% figures; the 2.6% comparator was cut off in my extract, so treat it as recalled] — [haloperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1ad2abd-a9d6-44e3-957b-36a73260f4f5)
- **Parkinson disease / Lewy body dementia.** Haloperidol injection is contraindicated. [verified: label] — [haloperidol label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1ad2abd-a9d6-44e3-957b-36a73260f4f5)
- **QT prolongation.** Droperidol contraindicated by label with QTc >440/450 ms; Igalmi "prolongs the QT interval; avoid use in patients with risk factors"; ziprasidone and haloperidol both lengthen QTc after IM dosing; IV olanzapine cohort median QTc 404 ms before and 415 ms after (unpaired). [verified: labels and abstract] — [droperidol](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=147e033d-d997-4ef6-8bb5-a9ba372590b2); [Igalmi](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=99476e8c-2527-4cb0-9d67-9f9cd91343c6); [Martel 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.12842)
- **Pediatrics (2019 AAEP consensus, Gerson et al).** Doses as returned by a machine summary of Table 2, so [secondary] and to be checked against the PDF before printing: diphenhydramine 12.5 to 50 mg or 1 mg/kg PO/IM; lorazepam 0.5 to 2 mg or 0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg PO/IM/IV; clonidine 0.05 to 0.1 mg PO; chlorpromazine 12.5 to 60 mg or 0.55 mg/kg PO/IM (IM half the PO dose); haloperidol 0.5 to 5 mg PO/IM (IM half the PO dose; max 6 mg/day at 15 to 40 kg, 15 mg/day above 40 kg); olanzapine 2.5 to 10 mg PO/ODT/IM (IM half or a quarter of PO); risperidone 0.25 to 1 mg or 0.005 to 0.01 mg/kg PO/ODT; quetiapine 25 to 50 mg or 1 to 1.5 mg/kg PO. Severe agitation of unknown cause: lorazepam with haloperidol or chlorpromazine, or olanzapine alone; no benzodiazepine within 1 h of olanzapine; avoid diphenhydramine and benzodiazepines in delirium; "ketamine and barbiturates are not recommended"; ziprasidone not recommended; avoid IM in autism/developmental disability unless essential. — [Gerson 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6404720/)
  - The summary returned "0.55 mg/kg per dose" for haloperidol as well as chlorpromazine. That would be 22 mg for a 40 kg child and exceeds the table's own 0.5 to 5 mg range, so do not print a mg/kg haloperidol dose without reading the original table.
- Pediatric trial data: oral olanzapine vs oral diazepam, 61% vs 57% sedated at 1 h (PEAChY-O); Hennepin pediatric olanzapine cohort, 17% needed another sedative, intubation 0.7%. [verified: abstracts] — [Bourke 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2025.01.007); [Cole 2020](https://doi.org/10.1097/PEC.0000000000001231)
- **Head injury.** Only incidental data: the single intubation in Martel 2021 was for an acute subdural hematoma in a patient treated as agitated. [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.14124)

### Inferences
- The stimulant "benzodiazepines first" rule rests on mechanism and tradition; the two systematic reviews and the 2017 and 2026 methamphetamine analyses support droperidol or olanzapine as acceptable, and lorazepam IM as a poor choice on speed.
- With physostigmine supply apparently exhausted or nearly so in 2026, rivastigmine (oral or patch) plus benzodiazepines is the practical plan; pharmacists should check local stock before listing physostigmine on a handout.

### Gaps
- [recalled, not confirmed]: 2023 AGS Beers Criteria advise avoiding benzodiazepines in older adults and in delirium (except withdrawal states), and avoiding antipsychotics for behavioral problems of dementia or delirium unless nonpharmacologic options have failed and there is threat of harm; low-dose haloperidol 0.5 to 1 mg; quetiapine 12.5 to 25 mg as the usual choice in Parkinson disease or Lewy body dementia. The Beers document was not read.
- Pregnancy: no source read. [recalled]: haloperidol has the largest pregnancy experience among antipsychotics; avoid supine positioning in late pregnancy.
- PCP and synthetic cannabinoids: no specific trial retrieved; pediatric BETA only says to consider synthetics when the screen is negative. [recalled]: benzodiazepines first line by convention.
- Head injury: no guidance source read.
- Current physostigmine stock after May 2026: unconfirmed.
