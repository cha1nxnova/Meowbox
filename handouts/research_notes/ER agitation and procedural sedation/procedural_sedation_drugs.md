## 1. Drug chart: adult and pediatric PSA dosing, onset, duration, adverse-effect profile

### Takeaway
Label-verified doses exist for propofol (MAC sedation), ketamine (anesthesia doses only), remimazolam, midazolam, dexmedetomidine IV, flumazenil and naloxone; the customary ER PSA doses (propofol 0.5 to 1 mg/kg, ketamine 1 to 1.5 mg/kg IV or 4 to 5 mg/kg IM, etomidate 0.1 to 0.15 mg/kg, ketofol 0.5 mg/kg each) come from trial protocols and guidelines, not labels, and several are off-label (all pediatric propofol sedation, all intranasal routes, etomidate for sedation). Onset and duration figures are mostly [recalled] and should be checked against a tertiary reference before the handout is printed.

### Cited Findings

**Propofol (no analgesia)**
- Trial protocol dose used in the Hennepin RCTs: 1 mg/kg IV then 0.5 mg/kg every 3 min as needed (adults) [verified] — [Miner 2010, PMID 20624140](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2010.00776.x); 1 mg/kg then 0.5 mg/kg every 2 min in the methohexital comparison [verified] — [Miner 2003, PMID 12957974](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2003.tb00646.x).
- Pediatric ER protocol: 1 mg/kg bolus then 0.5 mg/kg doses, opioid 1 minute before; median total 2.7 mg/kg; n=393 [verified] — [Bassett 2003, PMID 14634602](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0196-0644(03)00619-x).
- Label (Diprivan, DailyMed version published 22 Sep 2025, PLR format): MAC sedation initiation by slow injection "approximately 0.5 mg/kg administered over 3 minutes to 5 minutes"; or infusion 100 to 150 mcg/kg/min for 3 to 5 min; maintenance 25 to 75 mcg/kg/min; "slow infusion or slow injection techniques are preferable over rapid bolus" [verified] — [Diprivan label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e3105799-a4b6-f3f4-e6e2-b2b29bbdb538).
- Label, elderly/debilitated/ASA III to IV: "rapid (single or repeated) bolus dose administration should not be used for MAC sedation"; give over 3 to 5 min and reduce dosage "to approximately 80% of the usual adult dosage" [verified] — same label. NOTE: the label figure is a reduction TO about 80% (a 20% reduction), not the 50% reduction in the brief; the 50% figure is ER custom [recalled] and I found no primary source for it.
- Label: "DIPRIVAN is not indicated for use in pediatric patients for ICU sedation or for MAC sedation for surgical, nonsurgical or diagnostic procedures as safety and effectiveness have not been established" (pediatric PSA is off-label); fentanyl with propofol in children "may result in serious bradycardia" [verified] — same label.
- Label: pain on injection in 45% of pediatric patients when a small hand vein was used without lidocaine; under 10% with lidocaine pretreatment or antecubital veins; lidocaine 1 mL of 1% before injection; do not mix more than 20 mg lidocaine per 200 mg propofol [verified] — same label.
- Repeat dose 0.25 to 0.5 mg/kg every 1 to 3 min; onset about 30 to 60 s; duration about 5 to 10 min [recalled].
- Hemodynamic/respiratory effect sizes: see trial table (hypotension 3.5% Burton 2006; 8% POKER; oxygen desaturation 7.7%, BVM 3.9% Burton 2006) [verified].

**Ketamine (dissociative; analgesic)**
- Label (Ketalar, Par, published 26 Mar 2026): IV induction 1 to 4.5 mg/kg, average 2 mg/kg gives 5 to 10 min surgical anesthesia within 30 s; "Administer KETALAR slowly (i.e., over a period of 60 seconds). Rapid administration may result in respiratory depression and enhanced vasopressor response"; IM 6.5 to 13 mg/kg (9 to 13 mg/kg gives anesthesia in 3 to 4 min lasting 12 to 25 min) [verified] — [Ketalar label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=14e8f864-8b8a-4e7e-8439-e510d3107063). These are anesthesia doses; ER PSA doses are lower.
- Label strengths: 10 mg/mL (200 mg/20 mL), 50 mg/mL (500 mg/10 mL), 100 mg/mL (500 mg/5 mL) multiple-dose vials; "Do not administer the 100 mg/mL concentration of KETALAR intravenously without proper dilution" [verified] — same label.
- Label contraindications: patients "for whom a significant elevation of blood pressure would constitute a serious hazard"; hypersensitivity. Label says emergence delirium "in approximately 12% of patients"; "Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients below the age of 16 have not been established"; "not recommended for use in patients who have not followed nil per os guidelines"; "administer an antisialagogue prior to induction of anesthesia" (label language conflicts with ER evidence, see section 5) [verified] — same label.
- 2004 guideline (pediatric; read in full PDF): IV loading dose 1.5 mg/kg over 60 seconds, incremental doses 0.5 to 1.0 mg/kg; IM 4 to 5 mg/kg, repeat full or half dose; absolute contraindication age younger than 3 months; if atropine used 0.01 mg/kg; quoted rates transient laryngospasm 0.4%, transient apnea/respiratory depression 0.3%, recovery agitation mild 17.6%, moderate or severe 1.6% [verified, 2004 version only] — [Green and Krauss, Ann Emerg Med 2004;44:460-471, doi 10.1016/j.annemergmed.2004.06.006](https://rsds.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/clinical-practice-guideline-emergency-dept-ketamine.pdf).
- 2011 update (Green SM, Roback MG, Kennedy RM, Krauss B. Ann Emerg Med 2011;57(5):449-461; PMID 21256625): abstract confirms revision "particularly with respect to contraindications, age recommendations, potential neurotoxicity, and the role of coadministered anticholinergics and benzodiazepines" [verified, abstract only] — [PMID 21256625](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2010.11.030). Absolute contraindications age under 3 months and known/suspected schizophrenia (even if stable on medication); head trauma removed as a relative contraindication [secondary] — [WikEM ketamine](https://wikem.org/wiki/Ketamine). 2011 doses of 1.5 to 2 mg/kg IV in children, 1 mg/kg IV in adults, 4 to 5 mg/kg IM, given over 30 to 60 s [secondary/recalled; secondary sources disagree on 1 vs 1.5 to 2 mg/kg, which reflects the adult vs child split].
- Pediatric dose-finding RCT: 1 vs 1.5 vs 2 mg/kg IV, n=125 analysed (50/35/40); redosing 16.0% vs 2.9% vs 5.0%; adverse events 10.0% vs 14.3% vs 10.0%; sedation duration 23 vs 24.5 vs 23 min [verified] — [Kannikeswaran 2016, PMID 27216835](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2016.03.064).
- Adult dose RCT: 0.5 vs 1 mg/kg IV, n=108 (54 each); recovery agitation similar (difference 1.9%, 95% CI -14.8% to 18.4%); 4 patients (7.4%) in the 0.5 mg/kg arm needed an extra half-dose vs none [verified] — [Türkücü 2025, Acad Emerg Med 32(8):857-862, PMID 39878430](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.15116).
- IV vs IM in children (1 mg/kg IV vs 4 mg/kg IM, n=208 completed): vomiting 11.9% IV vs 26.3% IM (OR 2.60, 95% CI 1.2 to 5.9); respiratory events 8.3% vs 4.0%; length of sedation median 80 vs 129 min [verified] — [Roback 2006, PMID 17052563](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2006.06.001).
- Onset IV about 1 min, IM about 5 min; dissociation lasts about 10 to 15 min IV and 15 to 30 min IM [recalled; the 2004 guideline table lists peak concentrations at 5 min IM and 1 min IV, verified].

**Ketofol (ketamine + propofol; off-label mixture)**
- 1:1 single-syringe mixture used in the two large double-blind RCTs (Andolfatto 2012; POKER 2016), titrated by weight-based schedule [verified that both were 1:1; the mL/kg aliquots are not printed in the abstracts] — [PMID 22401952](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2012.01.017), [PMID 27460905](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2016.05.024).
- Sequential regimen: ketamine 0.5 mg/kg then propofol 1 mg/kg, repeat propofol 0.5 mg/kg (David and Shipp) [verified] — [PMID 21256626](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2010.11.025). Pediatric: ketamine 0.5 mg/kg + propofol 0.5 mg/kg, then propofol 0.5 mg/kg every 2 min (Shah) [verified] — [PMID 20947210](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2010.08.032).
- 1:1 vs 4:1 (propofol:ketamine): recovery agitation 21% with 1:1 vs 10% with 4:1 vs 8% propofol [verified] — [Miner 2015, PMID 25441247](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2014.08.046).
- Customary starting aliquot 0.5 mg/kg of each drug (0.1 mL/kg of a 5 mg/mL + 5 mg/mL mix) [recalled].

**Etomidate (no analgesia; sedation use is off-label)**
- Label (Amidate, Hospira, published 25 May 2026): induction 0.2 to 0.6 mg/kg (usual 0.3 mg/kg) over 30 to 60 s; not recommended under age 10 years; "Geriatric patients may require reduced doses"; venous pain "in about 20% of the patients" (range 1.2% to 42%); transient skeletal muscle movements "in about 32%"; boxed-style warning against prolonged infusion because of cortisol and aldosterone suppression; induction doses reduce cortisol and aldosterone "not associated with changes in vital signs or evidence of increased mortality" [verified] — [Amidate label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b7ed5bf8-ba75-44dc-8f81-96b4ad5766be).
- ER PSA doses reported: review range 0.15 to 0.22 mg/kg; myoclonus 20 to 45%; desaturation below 90% or apnea about 10% [verified] — [Falk and Zed 2004, PMID 15173551](https://doi.org/10.1345/aph.1E008). Mean cumulative dose 0.20 mg/kg [verified] — [Vinson and Bradbury 2002, PMID 12023700](https://doi.org/10.1067/mem.2002.123695).
- Customary PSA starting dose 0.1 to 0.15 mg/kg, repeat 0.05 mg/kg; onset under 1 min; duration 5 to 10 min [recalled].

**Midazolam (no analgesia) with or without fentanyl**
- Label (midazolam injection, Hikma, published 19 Dec 2024), sedation, healthy adults under 60: titrate slowly; "No more than 2.5 mg should be given over a period of at least 2 minutes"; wait 2 or more minutes; total over 5 mg not usually necessary; about 30% less if opioid premedication. Age 60 or older, debilitated or chronically ill: "No more than 1.5 mg should be given over a period of no less than 2 minutes"; further increments no more than 1 mg over 2 min; total over 3.5 mg not usually necessary [verified] — [midazolam label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f8bb3427-c887-47b6-8042-8a21dc161c79).
- Label pediatric IV: 6 months to 5 years initial 0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg (total up to 0.6 mg/kg, usually not over 6 mg); 6 to 12 years initial 0.025 to 0.05 mg/kg (total up to 0.4 mg/kg, usually not over 10 mg); 12 to 16 years dose as adults [verified] — same label.
- Intranasal midazolam (off-label route): adaptive double-blind RCT of 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 mg/kg for laceration repair, age 6 months to 7 years, n=101; 0.2 and 0.3 mg/kg eliminated; "optimal doses ... were 0.4 and 0.5 mg/kg"; no serious adverse events [verified] — [Tsze 2025, JAMA Pediatr 179(9):979-986, PMID 40720114](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2025.2181).
- Fentanyl IV 0.5 to 1 mcg/kg titrated; onset 2 to 3 min; duration 30 to 60 min [recalled]. Intranasal fentanyl: mean total 1.7 mcg/kg (150 mcg/mL concentrate) was equivalent to IV morphine 0.1 mg/kg in children 7 to 15 years with fractures, n=67 [verified] — [Borland 2007, PMID 17067720](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2006.06.016); 2 mcg/kg IN used as comparator in PRIME [verified] — [Frey 2019, PMID 30592476](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.4582).
- Midazolam/fentanyl had the highest respiratory adverse-event rate of the common pediatric regimens: 19.3% vs ketamine alone 6.1%, ketamine/midazolam 10%, midazolam alone 5.8% (n=2,500) [verified] — [Roback 2005, PMID 15930401](https://doi.org/10.1197/j.aem.2004.12.009).
- Propofol vs midazolam (each with fentanyl 3 mcg/kg), n=40: length of stay 29.25 vs 71.75 min [verified] — [Rahman 2011, PMID 21098799](https://doi.org/10.1136/emj.2009.085019).

**Methohexital (barbiturate; no analgesia)**
- Trial dose 1 mg/kg IV then 0.5 mg/kg every 2 min [verified] — [Miner 2003, PMID 12957974](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2003.tb00646.x).
- Label (Brevital, Par, published 20 May 2026): "Seizures may be elicited in subjects with a previous history of convulsive activity, especially partial seizure disorders"; apnea reported by any route; hiccups, laryngospasm, skeletal muscle hyperactivity listed; IV use in children not established [verified] — [Brevital label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=eccd8340-ead3-4363-8902-0c19d33aa2ac).
- Onset under 1 min, duration 5 to 7 min [recalled].

**Dexmedetomidine (minimal respiratory depression; weak analgesia)**
- Label (Precedex, Hospira, published June 2026): procedural sedation adults loading 1 mcg/kg over 10 min (0.5 mcg/kg over 10 min for less invasive procedures and for patients over 65), maintenance generally 0.6 mcg/kg/h (range 0.2 to 1); indicated for "non-intubated pediatric patients aged 1 month to less than 18 years prior to and during non-invasive procedures"; warning: "Clinically significant episodes of bradycardia and sinus arrest" with rapid IV or bolus administration; consider dose reduction in hepatic impairment [verified] — [Precedex label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=92b7ddaa-e45a-4b64-a802-bb5df8771dd6).
- Intranasal (off-label route): multicenter prospective cohort, n=578, age 2 months to 17 years, non-painful procedures; most common dose 3 to 3.9 mcg/kg (55%); acceptable sedation 81.1%; procedure completion 91.3%; mean onset 32.3 min; mean total sedation time 114.8 min; no serious airway/breathing/cardiovascular intervention [verified] — [Tsze 2023, PMID 36810939](https://doi.org/10.1542/hpeds.2022-007007).
- IN for CT: prospective pilot; mean time to sedation 13.4 min, no failed sedations (dose and n not printed in the abstract) [verified] — [Mekitarian Filho 2015, PMID 25748567](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2015.01.036).
- 2 to 3 mcg/kg IN as the usual pediatric imaging dose [recalled; consistent with Tsze 2023 and Whyte 2026 median 3.11 mcg/kg].

**Nitrous oxide 50 to 70% (inhaled; mild to moderate analgesia)**
- n=762 children aged 1 to 17 years; 72% received 70%, 13% received 50%; moderate/deep sedation in 3% at 70% and 0% at 50%; adverse events 8.3% (vomiting 5.7%); serious adverse events 2 patients (0.2%); no difference 70% (8.4%) vs 50% (9.9%) [verified] — [Babl 2008, PMID 18310173](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2007-1044).
- Adding IN fentanyl to N2O 70% (n=399 analysed, double-blind): no difference in FLACC, self-reported pain, sedation depth or adverse events [verified] — [Seiler 2019, PMID 30630844](https://doi.org/10.1136/emermed-2018-207892).
- Onset 2 to 5 min, offset within 5 min; contraindications pneumothorax, bowel obstruction, other closed air spaces, B12/folate pathway disorders [recalled, no primary source opened].

**Remimazolam (Byfavo; ultra-short benzodiazepine; no analgesia)**
- Label (Byfavo, Acacia, DailyMed version published 28 Feb 2025): indicated "for the induction and maintenance of procedural sedation in adults undergoing procedures lasting 30 minutes or less"; induction 5 mg IV over 1 minute; maintenance 2.5 mg IV over 15 seconds, at least 2 minutes between supplemental doses; ASA III to IV: induction 2.5 to 5 mg over 1 min, maintenance 1.25 to 2.5 mg; fentanyl 25 to 75 mcg was given before the first dose in trials; peak sedation about 3 to 3.5 min after a 5 mg dose; 20 mg vial reconstituted with 8.2 mL saline to 2.5 mg/mL, use within 8 h; incompatible with lactated Ringer's; contraindicated with severe hypersensitivity to dextran 40; pediatric safety not established; terminal half-life 37 to 53 min; clearance not related to body weight; titrate carefully in severe hepatic impairment; flumazenil should be immediately available; boxed warning on trained personnel/monitoring and opioid co-use [verified] — [Byfavo label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4e1838f2-b999-41a7-a761-d94f09aa531f).
- FDA approval year 2020 [recalled; not re-verified].
- ER data through Nov 2026: see trial table (Danish cohorts only; no ER RCT found).

**Alfentanil and remifentanil (opioid-only sedation; off-label)**
- Alfentanil observational, n=148 adults: airway/respiratory events leading to intervention 39% (supplemental oxygen 18%, BVM 3%, repositioning 2%, stimulation 18%); median pain VAS 26 mm, recall 98 mm [verified] — [Miner 2011, PMID 20947206](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2010.08.010). Dose used with propofol in the Hennepin trials: 10 mcg/kg [verified] — [Miner 2009, PMID 19845550](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2009.00487.x).
- Alfentanil vs propofol RCT (moderate sedation), n=108 (52 vs 56): interventions 23% vs 20% (p=0.657); pain reported 48% vs 13%; recall 75% vs 23%; satisfaction 87% vs 84% [verified] — [Miner 2017, PMID 28438447](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2017.04.041).
- Remifentanil vs propofol/fentanyl for shoulder reduction, n=64 (32 each): failed muscle relaxation 68.8% vs 12.5% (p<0.001); shorter onset and recovery; "significantly higher frequency of apnea" (rate not printed in abstract) [verified] — [Monsef Kasmaee 2019, PMID 30847445](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6377222/).

**Sub-dissociative ketamine for analgesia (0.1 to 0.3 mg/kg)**
- 0.3 mg/kg IV push over 3 to 5 min vs morphine 0.1 mg/kg, n=90 (45 each): pain score 8.6 vs 8.5 at baseline and 4.1 vs 3.9 at 30 min (difference 0.2, 95% CI -1.19 to 1.46); more minor adverse effects with ketamine at 15 min [verified] — [Motov 2015, PMID 25817884](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2015.03.004).
- 0.3 mg/kg IV push vs short infusion, n=48: feeling of unreality 92% vs 54%; median RASS at 5 min -2.0 vs 0.0; equal analgesia [verified] — [Motov 2017, PMID 28283340](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2017.03.004). (Infusion duration of 15 min is [recalled], not in the abstract.)
- Meta-analysis, 3 RCTs: ketamine non-inferior to morphine (relative reduction 0.42, 95% CI -0.70 to 1.54) [verified] — [Karlow 2018, PMID 30019434](https://doi.org/10.1111/acem.13502).
- Intranasal ketamine 1.5 mg/kg vs IN fentanyl 2 mcg/kg in children 8 to 17 years, n=90: VAS reduction 30.6 vs 31.9 mm; adverse events RR 2.5 (95% CI 1.5 to 4.0), all minor [verified] — [Frey 2019 PRIME, PMID 30592476](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.4582).

**Methoxyflurane (inhaled analgesic; not a sedative)**
- Pediatric ER RCT (MAGPIE), 3 mL inhaler vs placebo, age 6 to under 18, 192 treated: VAS change at 15 min -20.0 vs -13.2 mm (LS mean difference -6.8 mm, 95% CI -12.5 to -1.2, p=0.018, ages 9 to under 18); rescue medication 9.8% vs 30.0%; dizziness 41% vs 12% [verified] — [Hartshorn 2025, PMID 41242203](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.injury.2025.112830).
- Prehospital RCT (PreMeFen, Norway), n=338 randomised: NRS change at 10 min -3.31 methoxyflurane, -1.98 IN fentanyl, -2.74 IV morphine; methoxyflurane non-inferior to both [verified] — [Simensen, Lancet 406(10522):2957-2967, PMID 41275876](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01575-2). (PubMed prints the issue date as "2026 Dec 20" with Epub 20 Nov 2025; volume 406 suggests December 2025, treat the year as uncertain.)
- Meta-analysis, 7 RCTs, n=1,585: time to first pain relief -5.93 min (95% CI -6.86 to -5.00); rescue analgesia RR 0.40 (0.18 to 0.90) [verified] — [Husain 2026, PMID 42874195](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2026.09.008).
- Not FDA-approved / not marketed in the US as of my knowledge [recalled; US status not re-checked].

### Inferences
- For a US handout, the "label vs practice" gap is itself a teaching point: propofol label asks for slow 0.5 mg/kg over 3 to 5 min and forbids rapid boluses in older/ASA III to IV adults, whereas ER trials used 1 mg/kg boluses; ketamine label advises NPO compliance and an antisialagogue, both contradicted by ER literature.
- Remimazolam is the only agent whose labeled indication is literally procedural sedation with fixed (non weight-based) dosing, which simplifies pharmacy preparation but it needs reconstitution and a separate analgesic.

### Gaps
- Onset/duration columns are mostly [recalled]; no tertiary reference (Lexicomp, UpToDate) was accessible.
- Green 2011 full text not opened: exact adult vs pediatric dose wording, relative contraindication list and the "over 30 to 60 s" wording are unverified.
- No primary source opened for nitrous oxide contraindications or for IV fentanyl PSA dose/onset.
- No label retrieved for fentanyl or alfentanil (DailyMed search returned only a 2008 Alfenta entry, not opened).

## 2. Trial table: exact headline numbers (all [verified] against the PubMed abstract unless stated)

### Takeaway
Head-to-head ER trials consistently show equivalent airway/respiratory intervention rates between propofol, ketofol, etomidate, methohexital and alfentanil; differences are in secondary outcomes (myoclonus and lower success with etomidate, agitation and slower recovery with ketamine, less hypotension but more emergence reactions with ketofol). In children, ketamine alone has the lowest serious adverse event rate. No randomized ER trial of remimazolam exists as of November 2026.

## 3. Procedure-specific choices and analgesia pairing

### Takeaway
Evidence supports short-acting hypnotics (propofol, etomidate, methohexital) as interchangeable for cardioversion with propofol having the cleanest side-effect profile; ketamine or ketofol for painful orthopedic procedures; and non-IV options (IN midazolam 0.4 to 0.5 mg/kg, IN dexmedetomidine, nitrous oxide) for pediatric lacerations and imaging. Propofol, etomidate, methohexital, midazolam and remimazolam have no analgesic effect, yet adding an opioid to propofol has not improved pain or recall in trials and adds respiratory events.

### Cited Findings
- Cardioversion: propofol, etomidate, methohexital similar recovery times and safety (Bauer 2022); propofol "lacked the myoclonus, prolonged sedation, and resedation" of etomidate, midazolam, midazolam/flumazenil (Coll-Vinent 2003); midazolam and fentanyl associated with more adverse events in 1,736 cardioversions (Stiell 2021) [verified] — [PMID 35636047](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2022.05.036), [PMID 14634601](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0196-0644(03)00510-9), [PMID 34474123](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2021.08.018).
- Cardioversion made up 10% of propofol PSA in Burton 2006; dislocation 38%, fracture reduction 35%, abscess I and D 8%, CT 2% [verified] — [PMID 16365337](https://doi.org/10.1197/j.aem.2005.08.011).
- Orthopedic reduction: etomidate had lower procedural success than propofol (88.6% vs 97.2%) [verified] — [PMID 16997421](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2006.06.042). Remifentanil alone failed to give muscle relaxation in 68.8% [verified] — [PMID 30847445](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6377222/). Pediatric: ketofol slightly faster and less vomiting than ketamine (Shah) but higher SAE odds than ketamine alone in the large cohort (Bhatt) [verified].
- Opioid pairing with propofol: no difference in pain or recall, more stimulation needed (Miner 2009); qualitative review same conclusion (Black 2013) [verified]. Hennepin protocol instead treated pre-existing pain with IV morphine at least 20 minutes before sedation [verified] — [PMID 20624140](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2010.00776.x).
- Pediatric laceration: IN midazolam 0.4 to 0.5 mg/kg (Tsze 2025); IN dexmedetomidine gave less anxiety at positioning than IN midazolam (Neville 2016); ALICE adaptive trial of anxiolysis for laceration repair is at protocol stage (Poonai 2025, PMID 40465577) [verified].
- Imaging (non-painful): IN dexmedetomidine 3 to 3.9 mcg/kg most common, 91.3% completion, onset about 32 min (Tsze 2023); IV dexmedetomidine labeled for pediatric non-invasive procedures [verified].
- Painful minor procedures in children: 70% N2O with or without IN fentanyl equivalent (Seiler 2019); IN dexmedetomidine non-inferior to 50% N2O (Nikula 2024) [verified].
- Abscess I and D: no drug-comparison trial specific to abscess drainage was found; choice rests on the general trials above [gap stated as finding: PubMed searches this session returned none].

### Inferences
- "Propofol and etomidate need an analgesic" is pharmacologically true, but the trial evidence argues for treating baseline pain beforehand rather than co-injecting a potent opioid at induction.

### Gaps
- No trial data located for abscess I and D drug selection or for adult ER imaging sedation.

## 4. Reversal agents

### Takeaway
Reversal is for rescue, not routine: flumazenil carries seizure risk in benzodiazepine-dependent patients and resedation in 3 to 9%, and naloxone should be titrated in small increments to preserve analgesia.

### Cited Findings
- Flumazenil label, adults (reversal of conscious sedation): 0.2 mg IV over 15 seconds; repeat 0.2 mg at 60-second intervals to a maximum total of 1 mg; most respond to 0.6 to 1 mg; for resedation repeat at 20-minute intervals, no more than 1 mg at one time and no more than 3 mg in one hour [verified] — [flumazenil label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1855bb03-392f-4a0d-8361-24fc2a0e635e).
- Flumazenil label, pediatric (over 1 year): 0.01 mg/kg (up to 0.2 mg) over 15 seconds; repeat at 60-second intervals up to 4 more times; maximum total 0.05 mg/kg or 1 mg, whichever is lower; in the pediatric trial (n=107) 7 of 60 fully alert patients resedated, 19 to 50 minutes after dosing, all aged 1 to 5 years [verified] — same label.
- Flumazenil label: resedation in 3% to 9% of adult conscious-sedation patients; contraindicated when a benzodiazepine was given for a life-threatening condition (status epilepticus, ICP control) and in serious cyclic antidepressant overdose; seizure risk factors include "concurrent major sedative-hypnotic drug withdrawal, recent therapy with repeated doses of parenteral benzodiazepines" [verified] — same label.
- Flumazenil harms (suspected benzodiazepine overdose, 13 RCTs, n=990): adverse events 138/498 vs 47/492 (RR 2.85); serious adverse events 12/498 vs 2/492 (RR 3.81, 95% CI 1.28 to 11.39), mostly supraventricular arrhythmia and convulsions; "should not be used routinely" [verified] — [Penninga 2016, PMID 26096314](https://doi.org/10.1111/bcpt.12434).
- Naloxone label: postoperative opioid depression in adults "increments of 0.1 to 0.2 mg intravenously at two to three minute intervals"; overdose 0.4 to 2 mg IV repeated every 2 to 3 min; children postoperative increments of 0.005 to 0.01 mg IV at 2 to 3 minute intervals; neonates 0.01 mg/kg [verified] — [naloxone label, Somerset, published 19 Oct 2026](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b59ee3cc-9604-4abc-81be-897e2f42aa67).
- Starting at 0.04 mg IV in adults and pediatric 0.01 to 0.1 mg/kg (full reversal 0.1 mg/kg, max 2 mg) [recalled; the 0.04 mg figure is not in the label].
- Midazolam followed by flumazenil for cardioversion: all but 1 of 6 patients resedated [verified] — [PMID 14634601](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0196-0644(03)00510-9).
- No reversal agent exists for propofol, ketamine, etomidate, methohexital or dexmedetomidine [recalled].

### Inferences
- Remimazolam's flumazenil reversibility is promoted as an advantage, but the same resedation and seizure cautions apply in principle; no ER data address this.

### Gaps
- No guideline text confirming "reversal is not routine" was opened by me (covered by the framework researcher).

## 5. Drug-specific cautions

### Takeaway
Most traditional ketamine cautions (ICP, IOP, mandatory anticholinergic, routine midazolam in children) are not supported by current evidence, whereas the propofol egg/soy contraindication persists in the US label despite the AAAAI position that no precautions are needed.

### Cited Findings
- Propofol and egg/soy, label: "DIPRIVAN is contraindicated in patients with a history of anaphylaxis to eggs, egg products, soybeans or soy products" [verified] — [Diprivan label, published 22 Sep 2025](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e3105799-a4b6-f3f4-e6e2-b2b29bbdb538).
- Propofol and egg/soy, AAAAI: "Patients with soy allergy or egg allergy can receive propofol without any special precautions" (page shows date 5/12/2026) [verified by page fetch] — [AAAAI](https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/soy-egg-anesthesia).
- Evidence: 177,360 propofol anesthetics; proven propofol anaphylaxis 0.06 per 10,000 in non-allergic patients and 0 of 5,446 in egg- and soy-allergic patients [verified] — [Bagley 2021, PMID 33529424](https://doi.org/10.1111/pan.14147). 28 egg-allergic children, 43 administrations, 1 non-anaphylactic reaction (in a child with prior egg anaphylaxis) [verified] — [Murphy 2011, PMID 21467558](https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0b013e31821b450f).
- Propofol hypotension: 3.5% (Burton 2006); 8% vs 1% ketofol (POKER); 15.2 per 1,000 across adult PSA (Bellolio 2016); transient SBP decrease in 92% of children without poor perfusion (Bassett 2003) [verified].
- Ketamine and ICP: systematic review, 10 studies, 953 intubated patients: 2 of 8 studies showed small ICP reductions, 2 an increase; no differences in CPP, neurologic outcome, ICU stay or mortality [verified] — [Cohen 2015, PMID 25064742](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2014.06.018). Label still warns of raised ICP [verified].
- Ketamine and IOP (children, healthy eyes): mean rise 1.4 mm Hg at 2.5 min, n=80 (Halstead 2012); largest predicted change 1.09 mm Hg, n=25, doses up to 4 mg/kg (Drayna 2012); median rise 3 mm Hg (range 0 to 8), 15 of 60 had a transient rise of 5 mm Hg or more (Wadia 2014) [verified] — [PMID 23009160](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2012.01450.x), [PMID 22169582](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2011.06.001), [PMID 24582603](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2014.01.029). None studied injured eyes.
- Laryngospasm: 22/8,282 in ER children, idiosyncratic (Green 2010); 2.9 per 1,000 pediatric sedations, 33 of 34 with ketamine (Bellolio 2016); 7 of 12,780 in the PSRC ER registry (Green 2025) [verified].
- Hypersalivation/anticholinergics: not routinely needed (Brown 2008); atropine halves hypersalivation with IM ketamine but causes rash (Heinz 2006); anticholinergic co-administration was a predictor of airway events (OR 1.82) (Green 2009) [verified].
- Emergence/recovery agitation: adults 10 to 20% (Strayer and Nelson); label about 12%; adults midazolam 0.03 mg/kg reduces it (8% vs 25%); children no benefit from midazolam (Sherwin, Wathen) and more desaturation (7.3% vs 1.6%) [verified].
- Vomiting: 8.4% overall in children, peak at age 12, higher IM and with high IV dose (Green 2009b); IM 26.3% vs IV 11.9% (Roback 2006); ondansetron NNT 13 (Langston 2008); adults nausea/vomiting 28% IV, 34% IM (Sener 2011) [verified].
- Ketamine concentration errors: 9 pediatric ER cases of inadvertent overdose at 5 times (n=3), 10 times (n=5) or 100 times (n=1) the intended dose; all had prolonged sedation (3 to 24 h), 4 brief respiratory depression, 2 assisted ventilation, no adverse outcomes [verified] — [Green 1999, PMID 10499950](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0196-0644(99)80051-1). Three vial strengths 10, 50, 100 mg/mL; 100 mg/mL must be diluted for IV use [verified, label].
- Etomidate adrenal suppression after one dose (0.3 mg/kg RSI dose): normal cosyntropin response at 4 h in 30% vs 100% of midazolam controls (p=0.004); normal at 12 h in 100%; cortisol levels stayed in the reference range (n=18 analysed) [verified] — [Schenarts 2001, PMID 11136139](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2001.tb00537.x). No study of adrenal function after PSA-dose etomidate was found.
- Etomidate: myoclonus 20% (Miner), about 32% (label), 20 to 45% (Falk and Zed); injection pain about 20% (label); desaturation clustered in patients over 55 (Vinson) [verified]. A 2026 case report describes paradoxical agitation after low-dose etomidate for cardioversion [verified] — [Palmer 2026, PMID 42604833](https://doi.org/10.1177/08971900261477883).
- Dexmedetomidine: bradycardia and sinus arrest with rapid IV/bolus (label); mild bradycardia the main IN adverse event (Whyte 2026) [verified].
- Methohexital: seizures in patients with seizure history (label) [verified].
- Opioid chest wall rigidity with rapid high-dose fentanyl/alfentanil/remifentanil; managed with naloxone or neuromuscular blockade [recalled; PubMed search returned only case reports, none opened].
- Nitrous oxide contraindications (pneumothorax, bowel obstruction, middle ear disease, B12 deficiency/MTHFR) [recalled].

### Inferences
- For the handout, present egg/soy as "label contraindication limited to prior anaphylaxis; AAAAI says no precautions": the label wording is narrower than "egg allergy".
- Ketamine's label antisialagogue and NPO statements are likely to be cited by nursing/pharmacy reviewers; the Brown 2008 and Green 2009 data are the rebuttal.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm when the Diprivan label moved to PLR format or whether the egg/soy wording changed in 2024 to 2026; only the current (Sep 2025) version was read.
- No primary source for chest wall rigidity incidence at PSA doses.

## 6. Dose adjustment: older adults, obesity, pregnancy, organ impairment, instability

### Takeaway
Labels support dose reduction and slower administration in older adults for propofol (to about 80%, no rapid bolus), midazolam (no more than 1.5 mg over 2 min), dexmedetomidine (0.5 mcg/kg load) and etomidate; obesity weight-scalar guidance for PSA is extrapolated from anesthesia data and is largely unverified here.

### Cited Findings
- Propofol older adults: label, see section 1 (approximately 80% of usual dose, over 3 to 5 min, no rapid bolus); induction requirement 1.99 mg/kg up to age 54 vs 1.66 mg/kg above 54 (n=211) [verified] — [Diprivan label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e3105799-a4b6-f3f4-e6e2-b2b29bbdb538). Increasing age was associated with respiratory events in the 792-patient registry [verified] — [PMID 16365337](https://doi.org/10.1197/j.aem.2005.08.011).
- Midazolam age 60+: no more than 1.5 mg over at least 2 min; total over 3.5 mg rarely needed [verified, label].
- Dexmedetomidine over 65: load 0.5 mcg/kg over 10 min; consider reduction in hepatic impairment [verified, label].
- Etomidate: geriatric patients may need lower doses; "may induce cardiac depression in elderly patients, particularly those with hypertension"; renally excreted, caution in renal impairment [verified, label].
- Remimazolam: no overall age difference in trials but "greater sensitivity ... of some older individuals"; clearance "not related to body weight"; careful titration in severe hepatic impairment; ASA III to IV lower doses [verified, label].
- Cardioversion age 85+: OR 2.1 for important adverse events [verified] — [PMID 34474123](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cjca.2021.08.018).
- Obesity, propofol: in morbidly obese patients (BMI 40+), induction dosing by lean body weight matched the dose/time to loss of consciousness of lean controls dosed by total body weight; total-body-weight dosing gave a larger dose (n=90) [verified] — [Ingrande 2011, PMID 20861415](https://doi.org/10.1213/ANE.0b013e3181f6d9c0).
- Obesity, ketamine (ideal or adjusted body weight), fentanyl, midazolam weight scalars [recalled; PubMed search found no PSA-specific study].
- Hemodynamic instability: ketofol produced less hypotension than propofol (1% vs 8%, POKER); etomidate smaller SBP fall than propofol (3.8% vs 7.9%, Miner 2007); ketamine label warns that decreases in blood pressure, arrhythmias and cardiac decompensation "have also been observed" [verified].
- Pregnancy: remimazolam label warns of neonatal sedation and withdrawal with late-pregnancy benzodiazepine exposure; all GABA/NMDA agent labels carry the pediatric/third-trimester neurotoxicity warning for exposure longer than 3 hours [verified, Byfavo, Diprivan, Ketalar labels].

### Inferences
- The widely taught "halve the propofol dose in older adults" exceeds the label's 20% reduction; a defensible handout line is "start at 0.5 mg/kg or less, given slowly, and extend the redosing interval".

### Gaps
- No verified source for ketamine weight scalar in obesity, for PSA dosing in pregnancy, or for renal/hepatic adjustment of ketamine, propofol or fentanyl at PSA doses.
