# Insulin strategies for adult DKA and HHS in the US emergency department

**Scope:** Adults (non-pregnant) with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) in the US emergency department (ED), from arrival through stabilization, handoff or ED discharge. Covers IV regular insulin infusion, subcutaneous (SC) rapid-acting analog protocols, early basal insulin, dextrose titration, transition to SC insulin, computerized protocols, hypoglycemia/hypokalemia rates, and pharmacy practicalities. Pediatrics: one-line pointer only (covered by another researcher).

## 1. IV regular insulin infusion in DKA and HHS: dose, bolus, potassium gate, glucose targets, dextrose (guideline wording)

### Takeaway
All three current bodies use weight-based IV regular insulin at **0.1 units/kg/h for DKA** (US consensus and JBDS) and **0.05 units/kg/h for HHS without significant ketosis** (consensus and JBDS). The 2024 consensus allows a 0.1 units/kg bolus **only if venous access will be delayed**; JBDS says **"Do not use a priming (bolus) dose of insulin."** Insulin is held until **K+ >3.5 mmol/L** (consensus). When glucose falls below **250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L; JBDS uses <14 mmol/L)**, add **5-10% dextrose** and reduce insulin to **0.05 units/kg/h**, keeping glucose **about 200 mg/dL** (consensus) or 10-15 mmol/L (about 180-270 mg/dL; JBDS HHS). In HHS, JBDS gives fluids first and starts insulin only after glucose stops falling, unless ketones are >3.0 mmol/L. The 2024 consensus is **not graded**. ADA Standards of Care 2026 gives one Grade A recommendation (16.16) covering DKA/HHS management as a whole.

### Cited Findings

**2024 ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS consensus [CONS2024]**
- Grading: the consensus report has no evidence-grading system. It says: "The guidance represents the panel's collective analysis, evaluation, and expert opinion." [verified, via web-fetch read of PMC full text]
- IV dose: "a fixed-rate intravenous insulin infusion started at 0.1 units/kg/h" [verified]. An alternative is also described: "a nurse-driven insulin infusion protocol with a variable rate for DKA" [verified].
- Bolus: "In adults, treatment protocols recommend the initial administration of an insulin bolus (0.1 units/kg)" ... "(intravenously or intramuscularly) if a delay in obtaining venous access is anticipated." [verified]. So the bolus is conditional, not routine. Which trials were cited for this was not visible in the fetched text.
- Potassium gate: "insulin therapy should be delayed until the potassium level increases to >3.5 mmol/L" [verified]. "potassium replacement should be started after serum levels fall below 5.0 mmol/L" [verified]. Replacement rate: "potassium replacement should begin at a rate of 10 mmol/h" [verified]. In the fetch this sentence appeared in the context of the K <3.5 / insulin-delay statement. Whether 10 mmol/h applies to all replacement or only to K <3.5 was not confirmed [gap].
- Reducing the rate and adding dextrose in DKA: "Once the blood glucose falls below 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L), 5–10% dextrose should be added to the 0.9% saline infusion" and "the insulin infusion rate should be reduced to 0.05 units/kg/h" [verified].
- Glucose target during infusion: "intravenous insulin infusion should be adjusted to maintain glucose levels at approximately 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)" [verified]. The fetched text did NOT contain "150-200" or "150-250 mg/dL" ranges, and did not mention 0.02 units/kg/h [verified, absence in the fetched portion].
- Glucose fall with fluids alone in DKA: "Mean plasma glucose concentrations have been reported to drop by approximately 50–70 mg/dL/h" [verified].
- HHS maximum glucose decline: "the decline should not exceed 90–120 mg/dL/h (5–6.7 mmol/L/h)" [verified].
- HHS insulin: "If HHS is present with no ketosis or with mild or moderate ketonemia" "(blood β-hydroxybutyrate ≥1.0 to <3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones <2+)" "then a fixed-rate intravenous insulin infusion should be started at 0.05 units/kg/h." [verified]
- HHS timing: "Some have recommended that insulin be withheld until glucose has stopped dropping, with initial fluid administration alone to prevent a rapid fall in osmolality." [verified]. The consensus reports this view rather than mandating it.
- Glucose monitoring: "Capillary blood glucose testing should be performed during treatment every 1–2 h" "using a hospital-calibrated glucose meter." [verified]
- DKA resolution: "Resolution of DKA is defined as achieving plasma ketone <0.6 mmol/L and venous pH ≥7.3 or bicarbonate ≥18 mmol/L" [verified].
- HHS resolution: "the measured or calculated serum osmolality falls to <300 mOsm/kg" ... "the blood glucose is <250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L)" [verified]. The full criteria list (urine output and cognition) was not captured verbatim in the fetch [gap].

**ADA Standards of Care 2026, Section 16 [SOC2026-16]** (ADA grades: A = strong RCT evidence, B = supportive cohort evidence, C = poorly controlled or uncontrolled studies, E = expert consensus)
- **16.16 (Grade A):** "Manage diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) by administering intravenous fluids, insulin" ... "ensuring timely and bridged transition to maintenance subcutaneous insulin administration" [verified, fragments via web-fetch. The middle of the sentence (electrolytes, precipitant, monitoring) was not captured verbatim. See Gaps].
- **16.17 (Grade B):** "The discharge planning process should include education on the recognition, prevention, and management of DKA and/or HHS" [verified, fragment].
- **16.8a (Grade A):** "Continuous intravenous insulin infusion is recommended for achieving glycemic goals and avoiding hypoglycemia" (in critical illness) [verified, fragment].
- **16.5a (Grade A):** "Once therapy is initiated, a glycemic goal of 140–180 mg/dL (7.8–10.0 mmol/L) is recommended for most critically ill" individuals [verified, fragment]. This is the general ICU target, not a DKA-specific target.
- **16.10 (Grade A):** discourages "sole use of a correction or supplemental insulin without basal insulin (formerly referred to as a sliding scale)" [verified, fragment]. Relevant to post-DKA orders.
- SOC narrative on nurse-driven protocols: "the infusion rate should be no less than 1 unit/h to allow for acidosis resolution" [verified, fragment].

**JBDS DKA, revised March 2023 [JBDS-DKA2023]** (UK, consensus-based, ungraded "good practice recommendations")
- "A fixed rate intravenous insulin infusion (FRIII) calculated on 0.1 units/per kilogram body weight is recommended" [verified, PDF p.14].
- Preparation: "The insulin infusion is made up of 50 units of soluble human insulin in 49.5 ml 0.9% sodium chloride solution (i.e. 1 unit /ml)." [verified]. The flowchart says "50 units human soluble insulin (Actrapid ® or Humulin S ®) made up to 50ml with 0.9% sodium chloride solution" [verified].
- Weight-band table: 40-49 kg = 4 units/h ... 140-150 kg = 14 units/h; >150 kg = "15 (any dose higher than this should be on the advice of the Diabetes Specialist Team)" [verified].
- Standing practice (no longer called controversial): "Do not use a priming (bolus) dose of insulin" and "Subcutaneous long-acting analogue/human insulin should be continued" [verified, p.17].
- **Recommendation 1:** "Reduce rate of insulin infusion to 0.05 units/kg/hr when glucose drops to <14 mmol/L" [verified]. Rationale: a national survey found "rates of hypoglycaemia (<4.0 mmol/L) and hypokalaemia (<4.0 mmol/L) were 27.6% and 67% respectively" [verified]. The 2023 update notes this as "the new recommendation to consider de-escalating the insulin infusion rate from 0.1units/kg/hr to 0.05 units/kg/hr once the blood glucose falls below 14 mmol/L" [verified].
- **Recommendation 7:** "The rate of glucose lowering should be least 3.0 mmol/L/hr" [verified].
- Metabolic targets: "Reduction of the blood ketone concentration by 0.5 mmol/L/hour; Increase the venous bicarbonate by 3.0 mmol/L/hour; Reduce capillary blood glucose by 3.0 mmol/L/hour; Maintain potassium between 4.0 and 5.5 mmol/L" [verified]. If targets are missed: "increase the insulin infusion rate by 1.0 unit/hr increments hourly until the ketones are falling at target rates" [verified].
- Dextrose: "once the blood glucose falls below 14.0 mmol/L, a 10% dextrose infusion should be added" and the flowchart says "Add 10% glucose 125ml/hr if blood glucose falls below 14 mmol/L" [verified]. "The intravenous insulin and dextrose should not be discontinued until the person with diabetes is eating and drinking normally." [verified]
- Euglycemic DKA scenario: "Initiate glucose 10% straight away at 125 ml/hr because the glucose is <14 mmol/L" / "Begin with 0.1units/kg/hr insulin rate" / "If glucose falling despite 10% glucose reduce to 0.05 units/kg/hr" [verified].
- Potassium: 0.9% NaCl with KCl 40 mmol/L "is prescribed as long as the serum potassium level is below 5.5 mmol/L and the person is passing urine. If the serum potassium level falls below 3.5 mmol/L the potassium regimen needs review." [verified]. JBDS does not explicitly hold insulin for K <3.5 in the text read. It calls for senior review and more potassium [verified, absence noted].
- JBDS resolution: "ketones <0.6 mmol/L AND venous pH >7.3 (do not use bicarbonate as a surrogate...)" at 6 h and 12 h reviews [verified, flowchart and p.31]. The flowchart also allows "venous pH >7.3 and/or venous bicarbonate over 18 mmol/L" for continuing the FRIII [verified].

**JBDS HHS 2022 [JBDS-HHS2022]**
- "An FRIII should not be started as part of the initial treatment unless significant ketonaemia is present (>3.0 mmol/L or urine ketones >2+). In all other circumstances, intravenous fluids should be administered first, and an FRIII only started once the glucose has stopped falling." [verified, PMC full text §4.3]
- "The recommended initial insulin dose is an FRIII given at 0.05 units/kg/h. If an FRIII is already in place, the infusion rate can be increased by 1.0 unit/h." [verified, §4.6]
- "the plasma glucose should ideally fall by no more than 5 mmol/L/h" [verified]. Osmolality fall target is "3.0–8.0 mOsm/kg/h" [verified]. Sodium fall "should not exceed 10 mmol/L in 24 h" [verified].
- "An initial target glucose of between 10 and 15 mmol/L is a reasonable goal until the person is eating and drinking normally" [verified]. (10-15 mmol/L is about 180-270 mg/dL; conversion by researcher.)
- "Glucose infusion (5% or 10%) should be started once blood glucose is <14 mmol/L and run alongside FRIII" [verified].
- Mixed DKA/HHS: if osmolality ≥320, pH <7.3, bicarbonate <15 and ketones >3.0, "Start a Fixed Rate Intravenous Insulin Infusion (FRIII) and IV fluids immediately and treat according to DKA pathway using 0.1 units/kg/h." [verified]
- HHS resolution (Table 3): "Osmolality <300 mOsm/kg", "Hypovolaemia has been corrected (urine output ≥0.5 ml/kg/h)", "Clinical and cognitive status is back to the pre-morbid state", "Blood glucose <15 mmol/L" [verified].
- "IV insulin can usually be discontinued once they are eating and drinking" [verified].

**Bolus evidence**
- [KITABCHI2008] Prospective RCT, n=37 adults with DKA, 3 arms: (1) 0.07 units/kg bolus then 0.07 units/kg/h; (2) 0.07 units/kg/h without bolus; (3) 0.14 units/kg/h without bolus. "Five patients in the no load group required supplemental insulin doses to decrease initial glucose levels by 10%; patients in the twice no load and load groups did not." Time to glucose ≤250 mg/dL, pH ≥7.3 and HCO3 ≥15 did not differ. Conclusion: "A priming dose in low-dose insulin therapy in patients with DKA is unnecessary if an adequate dose of regular insulin of 0.14 unit x kg body weight(-1) x h(-1) (about 10 units/h in a 70-kg patient) is given." [verified, PubMed abstract]
- [GOYAL2010] ED observational cohort, n=157 (78 bolus vs 79 no bolus), Henry Ford. Hypoglycemia 6% vs 1% (p=0.12). Glucose fall 60 vs 56 mg/dL/h. AG closure 1.9 vs 1.9 mEq/L/h. ED LOS 8 vs 7 h. Hospital LOS 5.6 vs 5.9 days. All non-significant. "Administration of an initial bolus dose of insulin was not associated with significant benefit." [verified, PubMed abstract]
- Practical implication (researcher inference from the sources above): no-bolus 0.1 units/kg/h is consistent with JBDS and with Kitabchi's data for 0.14 units/kg/h without bolus. The US consensus keeps the bolus only as an option when IV access is delayed.

## 2. Subcutaneous rapid-acting analog protocols for mild and moderate DKA

### Takeaway
The 2024 consensus and ADA Standards 2026 both endorse **SC rapid-acting analogs every 1-2 h** for **uncomplicated mild-to-moderate DKA**, in the ED or step-down units. They do **not** endorse SC analogs for severe or complicated DKA or for HHS. Neither document's fetched text gives specific units/kg. The doses come from the trials:
- Umpierrez lispro 2004: 0.3 units/kg, then 0.1 units/kg every 1 h.
- Umpierrez aspart 2004: every-1-h and every-2-h arms. Exact doses were not in the abstract; recalled as 0.3 then 0.1 q1h, or 0.3 then 0.2 q2h.
- Kaiser San Jose (Rao 2022): glargine 0.3 units/kg plus lispro 0.3 units/kg, then lispro 0.2 units/kg every 4 h.
- CRABI-DKA (2026): glargine 0.2 units/kg plus lispro 0.2 units/kg, then lispro 0.1-0.2 units/kg every 3 h.

RCT evidence (Cochrane 2016, 5 RCTs, n=201) is low to very low quality and shows no difference in resolution time or hypoglycemia. US system-level cohorts (Kaiser, SQuID at Washington University) show **less ICU use** (-57% adjusted at Kaiser; ICU admission for low-to-moderate DKA fell from 54% to under 21% with SQuID) and **shorter ED LOS** (about 3 h), with similar safety. A 2026 Canadian cohort found SC took **8.4 h longer to close the anion gap** but caused much less hypoglycemia and hypokalemia.

### Cited Findings

**Guideline position**
- [CONS2024]: "Patients with uncomplicated mild or moderate DKA may be treated with subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analogs" [verified]. "subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analogs every 1–2 h is an effective alternative" [verified]. "This treatment can be delivered in emergency departments and step-down units without the need for ICU care." [verified]. "The use of rapid-acting subcutaneous insulin analogs is not recommended for the treatment of severe and complicated DKA or with HHS." [verified]. Table 2 lists "Regular or observation nursing unit" as the location for mild DKA [verified]. Units/kg for the SC regimen were NOT found in the fetched text; they may be in Figure/Table images [gap].
- [SOC2026-16]: "Individuals with mild and uncomplicated DKA can be managed with subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin doses given every 1–2 h" [verified, fragment]. It notes the ED or step-down unit as settings [verified, paraphrase from fetch]. This narrative is not a separately graded recommendation. It sits under Grade A recommendation 16.16.

**Original RCTs**
- Umpierrez GE, Latif K, Stoever J, et al. Efficacy of subcutaneous insulin lispro versus continuous intravenous regular insulin for the treatment of patients with diabetic ketoacidosis. Am J Med. 2004;117(5):291-296. doi:10.1016/j.amjmed.2004.05.010. PMID 15336577.
  - Design: RCT, n=40.
  - SC arm: "an initial injection of 0.3 unit/kg followed by 0.1 unit/kg/h until correction of hyperglycemia (blood glucose levels <250 mg/dL), followed by 0.05 to 0.1 unit/kg/h until resolution of diabetic ketoacidosis (pH ≥7.3, bicarbonate ≥18 mEq/L)".
  - IV arm: "initial bolus of 0.1 unit/kg, followed by an infusion of 0.1 unit/kg/h".
  - Results: time to glucose correction 7±3 vs 7±2 h; resolution 10±3 vs 11±4 h; no difference in hypoglycemia. ICU care cost 39% more ($14,429 vs $8801, P<0.01).
  - SC patients were managed on regular wards (n=10) or intermediate care (n=10). [verified, PubMed abstract]
- Umpierrez GE, Cuervo R, Karabell A, et al. Treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis with subcutaneous insulin aspart. Diabetes Care. 2004;27(8):1873-1878. doi:10.2337/diacare.27.8.1873. PMID 15277410.
  - Design: RCT, n=45. SC aspart every 1 h (n=15) vs every 2 h (n=15) vs IV regular (n=15).
  - Time to correct hyperglycemia: 6.9±4, 6.1±4, 7.1±5 h. Time to resolve ketoacidosis: 10±3, 10.7±3, 11±3 h. All NS.
  - No difference in hypoglycemic events. No deaths. [verified, PubMed abstract]
  - Exact aspart doses (recalled: 0.3 units/kg initial, then 0.1 units/kg q1h or 0.2 units/kg q2h, with halving once glucose <250 mg/dL) were NOT in the abstract [recalled].
- **Cochrane:** Andrade-Castellanos CA, Colunga-Lozano LE, Delgado-Figueroa N, et al. Subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analogues for diabetic ketoacidosis. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2016;(1):CD011281. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD011281.pub2. PMID 26798030. PMCID PMC8829395.
  - Scope: 5 RCTs, 201 participants (110 SC, 91 IV); 4 lispro, 1 aspart; mild or moderate DKA by ADA criteria.
  - Time to resolution, lispro vs IV: MD 0.2 h (95% CI -1.7 to 2.1), "very low-quality evidence". Aspart vs IV: MD -1 h (-3.2 to 1.2).
  - Hypoglycemia: 6/80 lispro vs 9/76 IV, RR 0.59 (0.23 to 1.52), "low-quality evidence".
  - Conclusion: "there are neither advantages nor disadvantages when comparing the effects of subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analogues versus intravenous regular insulin for treating mild or moderate DKA." [verified, PubMed abstract]
- An updated meta-analysis was registered and its protocol published: Lim BL, Lee WF, Lee B, et al. BMJ Open. 2023;13(2):e070131. doi:10.1136/bmjopen-2022-070131. PMID 36764729. PROSPERO CRD42022369518 [verified, protocol abstract]. I did not find the completed review's results this session [gap].

**US health-system ED protocols**
- **Kaiser Permanente Northern California (San Jose):** Rao P, Jiang SF, Kipnis P, et al. Evaluation of outcomes following hospital-wide implementation of a subcutaneous insulin protocol for diabetic ketoacidosis. JAMA Netw Open. 2022;5(4):e226417. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.6417. PMID 35389497. PMCID PMC8990349.
  - Design: difference-in-differences; 21 hospitals; 7989 DKA hospitalizations, 2010-2019.
  - Results: ICU admission aRR 0.43 (95% CI 0.33-0.56), "a 57% reduction". 30-day readmission aRR 0.50 (0.25-0.99). No change in LOS or death. [verified, abstract]
  - Protocol, Table 1, ED phase [verified, PMC full text]:
    - "SQ glargine 0.3 units/kg × 1 or the patient's usual home dosage of lantus"
    - "SQ lispro 0.3 units/kg × 1; do not give if glucose is <250 mg/dL; repeat dose in 4 h if glucose is still >250 mg/dL"
    - Fluids: "D5 + 0.45% NaCl + KCl at 40 mEq/L, or 150 mL/h" if K ≤5.5
    - "Monitor glucose every 2 h for 4 h after last dose of high-dose lispro, given then every 4 h"
  - Protocol, ward phase [verified]:
    - "SQ lispro 0.2 units/kg every 4 h until glucose <250 mg/dL"
    - Then a correction scale and the home insulin dose
    - "SQ lantus 0.3 units/kg or patients' usual dose 24 h after first dose"
  - Eligibility: adults, nonpregnant, GCS >8 [verified]. Severe DKA was eligible if no exclusion applied [verified].
  - Euglycemic DKA: clinicians were taught to skip high-dose lispro if glucose was <250 mg/dL [verified].
  - Rescue D50 use: 7.4% pre and 7.4% post at the intervention site [verified, Table 3].
- **SQuID (Washington University/Barnes-Jewish, St. Louis):**

  - Griffey RT, Schneider RM, Girardi M, et al. SQuID (subcutaneous insulin in diabetic ketoacidosis) II: clinical and operational effectiveness. Acad Emerg Med. 2025;32(1):61-71. doi:10.1111/acem.15020. PMID 39308229.

    - Author initials were confirmed from PubMed metadata [verified].
  - Related SQuID papers [verified, PubMed metadata]: Schneider RM, et al. J Emerg Nurs. 2025;51(5):796-804 (doi:10.1016/j.jen.2025.04.004); Griffey RT, et al. ## 3. Early basal insulin during IV insulin infusion

### Takeaway
Basal insulin given early (within roughly 2-12 h of starting the drip) at **0.15-0.3 units/kg** (2024 consensus; most RCTs used glargine **0.25-0.4 units/kg**, most commonly 0.3) shortens DKA resolution by about **4 h** in pooled RCTs:
- MD -4.02 h, 8 RCTs, n=468, Thammakosol 2026 DOM.
- MD -4.06 h, 8 RCTs, n=407, AbuJlambo 2026.
- SMD -0.61, 9 RCTs, n=652, Nguyen 2026.

It reduces IV insulin use by about 19 units and **prevents rebound hyperglycemia** after the drip stops (Hsia 2012: 93.5% vs 33.3%). Pooled analyses show **no increase in hypoglycemia or hypokalemia**. The effect on LOS is inconsistent. JBDS continues home basal and starts **0.25 units/kg once daily** in newly diagnosed patients. ADA Standards 2026 mentions low-dose basal 0.15-0.3 units/kg alongside IV insulin.

## 4. Transition from IV to subcutaneous insulin (overlap timing, starting doses, resolution criteria)

### Takeaway
**Overlap timing:**
- 2024 consensus: give basal (preferably basal-bolus) **at least 1-2 h before** stopping the IV infusion.
- ADA Standards 2026: the DKA/HHS section says **2-4 h**; the general transition section says **2 h**. These conflict internally and with the consensus.
- JBDS: basal is continued throughout. The SC rapid-acting dose is given **with a meal**, and IV insulin is stopped **30-60 min later**.

**Starting doses:**
- Known diabetes: resume the prior home regimen.
- Insulin-naive: **0.5-0.6 units/kg/day** (consensus). JBDS uses 0.5-0.75 units/kg/day, with 50% as basal.
- Hypoglycemia risk (kidney failure, frailty): **about 0.3 units/kg/day** (consensus).

**Resolution criteria:** The 2024 resolution definition (**ketones <0.6 mmol/L AND venous pH ≥7.3 OR bicarbonate ≥18 mmol/L**) replaced the 2009 glucose/AG-based criteria (2009 used glucose <200 plus two of: HCO3 ≥15, pH >7.3, AG ≤12 [recalled]). If basal insulin was given early (Section 3), much of the "overlap" is already in place by the time criteria are met. JBDS requires the person to be eating and drinking before transfer and advises against converting at bedtime.

### Cited Findings
- [CONS2024], overlap: "it is important to allow an overlap of 1–2 h between the administration of subcutaneous insulin" and "the discontinuation of intravenous insulin." [verified]. "with basal insulin initiated at least 1–2 h before cessation of intravenous insulin infusion." [verified]. Figure 5: "Basal-bolus insulin is the preferred regimen and should be started 1–2 h before cessation of intravenous insulin." [verified]
- [CONS2024], dosing:
  - "Patients with known diabetes may be given insulin at the dosage they were receiving before the admission." [verified]
  - "a weight-based formula may be considered for TDD calculation using 0.5–0.6 units/kg/day in insulin-naive patients" [verified]
  - "for people with risk factors for hypoglycemia, including kidney failure or frailty," "a calculation using approximately 0.3 units/kg/day may be more appropriate." [verified]. Only kidney failure and frailty are named; older age per se is not named in the fetched text.
  - "Calculation of the transition subcutaneous dose should account for hypoglycemia risk factors and anticipated nutritional intake." [verified]. No explicit "must be eating" requirement and no basal/bolus percentage split was found in the fetched text [verified absence].
- [CONS2024], infusion end-point: IV insulin "continued until the ketoacidosis is resolved" [verified]. Resolution: "plasma ketone <0.6 mmol/L and venous pH ≥7.3 or bicarbonate ≥18 mmol/L" [verified].

  - Moderate: BHB 3.0-6.0; pH 7.0-7.25 or HCO3 10 to <15; alert or drowsy.

- [JBDS-DKA2023]:
  - "Transfer to subcutaneous insulin if the individual is eating and drinking normally. Ensure that the subcutaneous insulin is started before the IV insulin is discontinued. Ideally give the subcutaneous fast acting insulin at a meal and discontinue IV insulin 30-60 minutes later" [verified, p.30].
  - Convert "when biochemically stable (blood ketones less than 0.6 mmol/L, pH over 7.3) and they are ready and able to eat" [verified, p.31].

## 5. Dextrose management: when, how much, and the two-bag system

### Takeaway
**Thresholds:**
- Consensus: add **D5-D10** to 0.9% saline when glucose is **<250 mg/dL**, and halve insulin to 0.05 units/kg/h.
- JBDS DKA: add **D10 at 125 mL/h** when glucose is **<14 mmol/L (about 250 mg/dL)**, and consider halving insulin.
- JBDS HHS: **D5 or D10** at <14 mmol/L. Choose based on fluid overload risk and cost.
- Euglycemic DKA: start D10 immediately (JBDS).

**Two-bag system:** Two bags of identical electrolyte fluid, one with and one without dextrose, are titrated against glucose while total fluid and insulin rates stay constant. A 2026 Annals of EM meta-analysis (9 adult and 12 pediatric observational studies plus RCTs) found **hypoglycemia halved (RR 0.50)**, **DKA resolution 1.76 h faster**, and in adults **insulin infusion 3.74 h shorter** and **hypokalemia RR 0.84**. Evidence is mostly observational. I found no adult head-to-head trial of D5 vs D10.

### Cited Findings
- [CONS2024]: "Once the blood glucose falls below 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L), 5–10% dextrose should be added to the 0.9% saline infusion" [verified]. The fetched consensus text does not mention a two-bag system [verified absence].
- [JBDS-DKA2023]:
  - "Add 10% glucose 125ml/hr if blood glucose falls below 14 mmol/L" [verified].
  - "It is quite often necessary to infuse 0.9% sodium chloride solution and 10% dextrose concurrently" [verified].
  - Euglycemic DKA: "Initiate glucose 10% straight away at 125 ml/hr because the glucose is <14 mmol/L" [verified].
- [JBDS-HHS2022]: "Glucose infusion (5% or 10%) should be started once blood glucose is <14 mmol/L and run alongside FRIII and other fluid replacement. Choice of the glucose fluid concentration (5% or 10%) depends on the individual patient factors (e.g., risk of fluid overloads) and/or institutional factors (e.g. cost and availability)." [verified]
- Munir I, Fargo R, Garrison R, et al. Comparison of a 'two-bag system' versus conventional treatment protocol ('one-bag system') in the management of diabetic ketoacidosis. BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care. 2017;5(1):e000395. doi:10.1136/bmjdrc-2017-000395. PMID 28878933. PMCID PMC5574429.
  - Design: retrospective, adults, Riverside CA; 249 one-bag vs 134 two-bag.
  - Results: AG closure 13.56 vs 10.94 h (p<0.0002). Hypoglycemia 8.43% vs 1.49% (p=0.0064). Time to HCO3 >18 and LOS did not differ. [verified, abstract]
- Haas NL, Gianchandani RY, Gunnerson KJ, et al. The two-bag method for treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis in adults. J Emerg Med. 2018;54(5):593-599. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2018.01.004. PMID 29628184.
  - Design: before-after, adult ED (Michigan); 68 two-bag vs 107 one-bag.
  - Results: time to HCO3 ≥18, 13.4 vs 20.0 h. Insulin infusion 14.1 vs 21.8 h. Fluid bags charged 5.2 vs 29.7. Hypoglycemia 2.9% vs 10.3% (p=0.07). Hypokalemia 16.2% vs 27.1% (p=0.09). [verified, abstract]

## 6. Computerized insulin infusion protocols (eGMS: Glucommander, EndoTool, others) vs paper protocols

### Takeaway
All comparative DKA data are retrospective. In the largest study (Glucommander, 34 US hospitals, n=2665, industry co-authors), hypoglycemia was lower (**12.9% vs 35%**) and resolution faster. A 2026 meta-analysis of 7 non-randomized studies (n=3874) found **no difference in time to resolution**. It found lower recorded hypoglycemia, but certainty was very low. No guideline grades or mandates computerized protocols. The fetched 2024 consensus text does not mention them, and I found no EndoTool-specific DKA outcome study this session.

## 7. Hypoglycemia and hypokalemia rates by strategy

### Takeaway
**Standard IV insulin:** hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL) in **16-28%** and severe hypoglycemia (<40) in **2%**; hypokalemia (<3.5) in about **55% of DKA** and **51% of HHS**; severe hypokalemia (<2.5) in **16%** of DKA and **9%** of HHS (consensus Table 3). A UK national audit under 0.1 units/kg/h found hypoglycemia (<4.0 mmol/L) in **27.6%** and hypokalemia (<4.0 mmol/L) in **67%**. That audit is why JBDS added the drop to 0.05 units/kg/h.

**SC analogs:** hypoglycemia is similar to IV in RCTs (Cochrane RR 0.59, NS). In cohorts it is similar or lower: 1.6% vs 19.6% in Canada 2026; rescue dextrose 8% vs 18% in SQuID II. Hypokalemia was also lower in the Canadian cohort (aOR 0.35).

**Other strategies:**
- Early basal: no increase in hypoglycemia or hypokalemia in meta-analyses, but CIs remain wide (Nguyen 2026).
- Two-bag: hypoglycemia RR 0.50; adult hypokalemia RR 0.84.
- Computerized protocols: lower recorded hypoglycemia (12.9% vs 35% in Ullal 2018), with very low certainty.
- Bolus: 6% vs 1% hypoglycemia, NS (Goyal 2010).

- [CONS2024], Table 3: "In studies of DKA treatment, the risk of hypoglycemia (<70 mg/dL [3.9 mmol/L]) varied between 16% and 28%," "with severe hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL [2.2 mmol/L]) occurring in 2% of cases." "Hypokalemia (<3.5 mmol/L) occurred in ∼55% of DKA and 51% of HHS patients." "Severe hypokalemia <2.5 mmol/L occurs in 16% of people with DKA and 9% of people with HHS." [verified]
- [JBDS-DKA2023]: national survey: "the rates of hypoglycaemia (<4.0 mmol/L) and hypokalaemia (<4.0 mmol/L) were 27.6% and 67% respectively" [verified].

  - Cochrane hypoglycemia 6/80 vs 9/76, RR 0.59 (0.23-1.52) [verified].
  - Qiang 2026 hypoglycemia 1.6% SC vs 19.6% IV; hypokalemia aOR 0.35 [verified].
  - SQuID II rescue dextrose 8% vs 18% [verified]. SQuID I 2.7% vs 3.6% [verified].
  - Kaiser D50 use 7.4% pre and post [verified].
  - CRABI-DKA "comparable" [verified].

## 8. Pharmacy practicalities: preparation, stability, adsorption, high-alert status, US product landscape (Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
**Standard drip:** regular human insulin **1 unit/mL in 0.9% NaCl**. Usual practice is 100 units/100 mL. JBDS makes it as 50 units in 49.5 mL by syringe pump.
- Humulin R label: 0.1-1 unit/mL in 0.9% NaCl. Bags are stable **48 h refrigerated, then up to 48 h more at room temperature**.
- Novolin R label: 0.05-1 unit/mL in **polypropylene** bags, in NS, D5 or D10 with KCl 40 mmol/L. Bags are stable **24 h at room temperature**.
- **Myxredlin** (Baxter): premixed 100 units/100 mL. Refrigerate; it can be kept at room temperature for up to 30 days. No additives, no series connections.

**Priming:** prime the tubing with **20 mL**. At 20 mL, loss is indistinguishable from maximal priming (3.4% loss). No dwell time is needed (Goldberg 2006; Thompson 2012).

**Safety:** insulin is an ISMP high-alert medication class, and **U-500 has "special emphasis."** U-500 is never given IV. The U-500 label's How Supplied now lists **only the KwikPen**; secondary sources say the US U-500 vial was discontinued in 2025. Never share pens between patients, even with a new needle (FDA label warning).

**US product changes:**
- Levemir was discontinued in the US (2024).
- Semglee (branded) was reportedly discontinued at the end of 2025. Unbranded glargine-yfgn reportedly continues.
- Lilly is ending the Basaglar Tempo pen [secondary].
- **Lantus SoloStar 3 mL pens were on intermittent back order** (ASHP bulletin updated July 10, 2026), but FDA did not list this as a formal shortage [secondary].
- New biosimilars: Merilog (aspart-szjj, Feb 2025, first rapid-acting biosimilar, not interchangeable); Kirsty (aspart-xjhz, Jul 2025, first interchangeable rapid-acting); Langlara (glargine-aldy, approved about Apr 29-May 4, 2026, third interchangeable Lantus biosimilar).

**Prices:** list-price cuts of 70-78% (2023-2024) and the Medicare $35/month cap (IRA, 2023) remain. A July 2026 ASPE brief reports ≤$35/month pathways across all payer types.

**ED relevance:** For drips, regular insulin vials and Myxredlin bags are what matter. I found no US shortage of regular human insulin for IV use this session. The pen and basal disruptions matter for ED discharge prescriptions and the early-basal doses in Sections 3-4.

## Gaps and conflicts

**Disagreements between sources**
- **Bolus:**
  - CONS2024 says protocols "recommend" a 0.1 units/kg bolus but frames it as given "if a delay in obtaining venous access is anticipated."
  - JBDS: "Do not use a priming (bolus) dose of insulin."
  - Kitabchi 2008 supports no bolus if the rate is about 0.14 units/kg/h. The consensus rate is 0.1 units/kg/h, so the no-bolus evidence was generated at a higher rate than the one now used.
- **Overlap before stopping the drip:**
  - CONS2024: basal "at least 1–2 h before".
  - SOC2026 DKA/HHS section: "2–4 h before".
  - SOC2026 general transition subsection: "2 h".
  - JBDS: SC rapid-acting with a meal, then stop IV 30-60 min later, with basal continued throughout.
  - The handout should state which source is used.
- **Glucose target during the drip:**
  - CONS2024: "approximately 200 mg/dL".
  - JBDS HHS: 10-15 mmol/L (about 180-270 mg/dL).
  - SOC2026 general critical-care goal: 140-180 mg/dL (Grade A 16.5a; not DKA-specific).
  - Ullal 2018 found best outcomes with a 120-180 mg/dL eGMS target.
  - The "150-200" and "150-250 mg/dL" ranges named in the assignment were NOT found in the 2024 consensus text read. They likely come from older ADA statements (2009) or institutional protocols [recalled; not verified].
- **Potassium gate:** CONS2024 delays insulin until K >3.5 mmol/L. JBDS does not explicitly hold insulin at K <3.5. It calls for senior review and more K, and maintains K at 4.0-5.5.
- **HHS insulin timing:** JBDS mandates fluids first and starting the FRIII only when glucose stops falling, unless ketones are >3.0. CONS2024 only reports this as something "some have recommended" and gives 0.05 units/kg/h for HHS with BHB <3.0.
- **Insulin-naive TDD:** CONS2024 0.5-0.6 units/kg/day (0.3 if kidney failure or frailty) vs JBDS 0.5-0.75 units/kg/day (0.75 if insulin resistant).
- **Early basal dose:** CONS2024/SOC 0.15-0.3 units/kg vs JBDS 0.25 units/kg vs RCT doses of 0.25 (Hsia), 0.3 (Thammakosol, El Feky, Rao protocol) and 0.4 units/kg (Houshyar).
- **SC vs IV time to resolution:** RCTs and Cochrane found no difference. SQuID II was faster on SC (but against a sicker IV cohort). Qiang 2026 found AG closure 8.4 h slower on SC.
- **Severe DKA on SC:** Kaiser's protocol allowed severe DKA without ICU-level comorbidity. CONS2024 says SC analogs are "not recommended for the treatment of severe and complicated DKA."
- **Rezvoglar timeline:** Goodwin's blog says 2023. The researcher recalls approval in Dec 2021 with interchangeability in Nov 2022 [recalled]. Unresolved.
- **Langlara approval date:** May 4, 2026 vs Apr 29, 2026 (secondary sources disagree). The FDA letter BLA 761412 was not opened.
