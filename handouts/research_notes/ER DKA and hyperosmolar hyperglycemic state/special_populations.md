## 1. SGLT2 inhibitor-associated and euglycemic DKA

### Takeaway
(Draft — see end of section for synthesis.) Every US SGLT2-inhibitor label (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin, bexagliflozin, sotagliflozin) now carries an identical class warning titled "Diabetic Ketoacidosis in Patients with Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus and Other Ketoacidosis": glucose may be "less than 250 mg/dL", urinary glucose excretion persists 3 days after the last dose (4 days for ertugliflozin) but ketoacidosis/glucosuria has lasted ">6 days and some up to 2 weeks", hold at least 3 days before surgery (4 days for ertugliflozin), and restart only after ketoacidosis resolves and the patient is clinically stable and eating. The 2024 consensus defines euglycemic DKA as glucose <200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L), says to start 5-10% dextrose at the same time as 0.9% saline, stop the SGLT2 inhibitor on admission, and does not routinely recommend restarting it after DKA in type 2 diabetes.

**1b. 2024 consensus wording on SGLT2-inhibitor DKA (CONSENSUS 2024, Diabetologia PMC11343900 full text, read this session)**
- Incidence: "DKA risk is also increased with SGLT2 inhibitors in adults with type 1 diabetes and insulin-deficient type 2 diabetes. SGLT2 inhibitor-associated DKA occurs in approximately 4% of people with type 1 diabetes; the risk can be 5–17 times higher than in people with type 1 diabetes not treated with SGLT2 inhibitors. In contrast, observational studies and randomised controlled trials have shown that DKA is uncommon in people with type 2 diabetes treated with SGLT2 inhibitors, with an estimated incidence of 0.6–4.9 events per 1000 patient-years." RR vs comparator in T2D: "2.46 (95% CI 1.16, 5.21)" (4 RCTs) and "1.74 (95% CI 1.07, 2.83)" (5 observational studies). [verified]
- Risk factors (T2D on SGLT2i): "very-low-carbohydrate diets and prolonged fasting, dehydration, excessive alcohol intake and the presence of autoimmunity, in addition to typical precipitating factors". [verified]
- Glucose at presentation: "in one series, 35% of people treated with SGLT2 inhibitors presenting with DKA had glucose levels <11.1 mmol/l (200 mg/dl), and in another series, 71% of people treated with SGLT2 inhibitors presenting with DKA had glucose levels ≤13.9 mmol/l (250 mg/dl)". [verified]
- Euglycemic DKA definition: "Approximately 10% of patients with DKA present with euglycaemic DKA, which is defined as plasma glucose levels <11.1 mmol/l (200 mg/dl) in the presence of ketosis and metabolic acidosis criteria of DKA"; causes "exogenous insulin injection, reduced food intake, pregnancy, or impaired gluconeogenesis due to alcohol use, liver failure and/or SGLT2 inhibitor therapy"; SGLT2 inhibitors account for "the majority of cases of euglycaemic DKA". The 2024 criteria: glucose ≥200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) "or a prior history of diabetes irrespective of the presenting glucose value". [verified]
- Table 4 "special populations", SGLT2 inhibitor row (verbatim): Clinical: "May be spontaneous or preceded by insulin dose reduction or insulin omission, prolonged fasting or acute illness. May be prevented using specific 'sick day rules.'" Diagnosis: "May present with near-normal glucose concentrations or euglycaemic DKA (glucose <11.1 mmol/l [200 mg/dl])." Management: "Acute management as for 'general' DKA. In euglycaemic DKA, 5–10% dextrose should be added to intravenous fluid or started at the same time as the 0.9% sodium chloride. SGLT2 inhibitors should be stopped on admission." Follow-up: "SGLT2 inhibitor therapy is not recommended for patients with T1D. In patients with T2D, because of the lack of safety data, initiation or continuation of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy after DKA resolution is not routinely recommended." [verified]
- Contrast, ADA Standards of Care 2026 Rec 16.11 (grade A) supports initiating/continuing an SGLT2 inhibitor during hospitalization "if indicated for heart failure, providing there are no contraindications" [secondary here — verified by the sibling researcher in guidelines_diagnosis.md, ADA SOC 2026 Section 16, doi:10.2337/dc26-S016, PMID 41358892].

**1d. International consensus on ketone monitoring with SGLT inhibitors in type 1 diabetes**
- Danne T, Garg S, Peters AL, et al. International consensus on risk management of diabetic ketoacidosis in patients with type 1 diabetes treated with sodium-glucose cotransporter (SGLT) inhibitors. *Diabetes Care.* 2019;42(6):1147-1154. doi:10.2337/dc18-2316. PMID 30728224. PMCID PMC6973545. (ATTD consensus conference, June 2018; 26 experts; no evidence grades.) [verified, PMC full text]
  - euDKA: "normal or modestly elevated blood glucose levels (<250 mg/dL [13.9 mmol/L])"; "DKA is usually associated with hyperglycemia in individuals with diabetes and serum ketones >3.0 mmol/L." [verified]
  - Table 1 cut points (blood BHB / urine / action): "<0.6 mmol/L (normal)" negative, no action; "0.6–1.5 mmol/L (ketonemia)" trace or small: "Ingest 15–30 g rapidly absorbed carbohydrate and maintain fluid consumption (300–500 mL) hourly", "Administer rapid-acting insulin based on carbohydrate intake (hourly)", "Check blood/urine ketones (every 3–4 h) until resolution"; "1.6–3.0 mmol/L (impending DKA)" moderate: same, "Consider seeking immediate medical attention"; ">3.0 mmol/L (probable DKA)" large to very large: "Seek immediate medical attention". [verified]
  - ER-relevant: "If patients go to the emergency department or an urgent care center, they should inform the medical personnel that they have type 1 diabetes and are on an SGLT inhibitor, which means they may have DKA with a relatively normal glucose level. Patients should have an evaluation that includes measurements of capillary/venous pH, blood bicarbonate, anion gap, and blood ketones (β-hydroxybutyrate)." "it is important that emergency departments are made aware that DKA can present without overtly elevated glucose levels". [verified]
  - Urine ketone pitfall: "Because β-hydroxybutyrate is oxidized to acetoacetate with treatment of DKA, urine ketone readings will rise with treatment even if blood β-hydroxybutyrate concentrations are dropping." [verified]
  - Holding: "SGLT inhibitor therapy should be withheld immediately if the patient is hospitalized, acutely ill, or unable to eat and drink normally. SGLT inhibitors should be withheld or discontinued prior to any medical procedure (ideally for 3 days)". STICH protocol: "STop SGLT inhibitor treatment for a few days; Insulin administration; Carbohydrate consumption; Hydration". [verified]
  - Pregnancy: "SGLT2 inhibitors should not be used in pregnant women with type 1 diabetes as pregnancy is associated with an increased risk of ketoacidosis, which is associated with a high risk of fetal mortality". Pump users "are also at increased risk because of the possibility of pump or insulin infusion set malfunction". Absolute risk increase vs placebo "in the range of 4% per year". [verified]

**1g. Restarting after DKA**
- FDA labels: "Monitor patients for resolution of ketoacidosis before restarting"; resume "when the patient is clinically stable and has resumed oral intake". [verified]
- CONSENSUS 2024: not recommended in T1D; in T2D "initiation or continuation ... after DKA resolution is not routinely recommended". [verified]
- No prospective study of rechallenge after SGLT2i-associated DKA was found (PubMed search "SGLT2 inhibitor rechallenge OR resumption after ketoacidosis"; only case reports, e.g., Silveira 2026, where rechallenge "was not recommended"). [verified negative search]

**1h. Section 1 synthesis (for the handout)**
- Diagnosis: test ketones (serum BHB) and a VBG/anion gap in any SGLT2-inhibitor user with nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, malaise or dyspnea regardless of glucose; the US label threshold language is "less than 250 mg/dL", the consensus definition of euglycemic DKA is <200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L), and Danne 2019/JBDS use <250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) / <14 mmol/L. Applies to heart-failure/CKD users without diabetes too (Silveira 2026 case; labels). [verified sources above]
- Fluids/dextrose: start dextrose 5-10% with (not after) the 0.9% saline when glucose is <200 mg/dL (CONSENSUS 2024 Table 4). [verified]
- Insulin: same fixed-rate infusion as other DKA; observational data show total insulin needs and infusion duration are often higher/longer, because dextrose must be given to allow the insulin needed to clear ketones (Papanastasiou 2021; Park 2025). The exact "keep insulin at 0.1 units/kg/h and titrate dextrose" phrasing is a common teaching point; the consensus wording only says "Acute management as for 'general' DKA" plus dextrose. [verified for studies; teaching phrasing = inference]
- Monitoring: glucosuria/ketosis can persist >6 days and up to 2 weeks (labels); urine ketones lag BHB (Danne 2019). [verified]

## 2. GLP-1 receptor agonists and tirzepatide

### Takeaway
GLP-1 RAs and tirzepatide carry no US boxed warning or Warnings-and-Precautions section on DKA; the US labels (Ozempic, Mounjaro, Zepbound read this session) mention DKA only as a pregnancy background risk. The DKA signal comes from (1) rapid insulin reduction or discontinuation when a GLP-1 RA is started in insulin-treated diabetes (UK MHRA 2019 alert; EU SmPC change), (2) starvation/euglycemic ketoacidosis from vomiting, poor intake and carbohydrate restriction in weight-loss users, including people without diabetes (2025-2026 case reports), and (3) combination with SGLT2 inhibitors. In type 1 diabetes, 2025-2026 RCTs and large cohorts show no excess DKA. ER implications: GLP-1 RAs are "not substitutes for insulin", check ketones in vomiting patients on these drugs even if glucose is normal, and plan airway care for retained gastric contents (US label "Pulmonary Aspiration During General Anesthesia or Deep Sedation").

## 3. Other drug and toxic causes: antipsychotics, corticosteroids, immune checkpoint inhibitors, thiazides, pentamidine, cocaine, cannabis, alcohol, ketogenic diet

### Takeaway
The 2024 consensus names glucocorticoids, antipsychotics, checkpoint inhibitors (and, for HHS, corticosteroids, sympathomimetics and antipsychotics) as precipitants; treatment is standard DKA/HHS care, but the cause changes the workup and the disposition. Checkpoint-inhibitor diabetes presents with DKA in roughly 70% of published cases (69.7% of 278; 71% of 90), is insulin-deficient (C-peptide often undetectable) and needs lifelong insulin. Atypical antipsychotic labels carry class wording on hyperglycemia "associated with ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar coma or death". Cannabis hyperemesis can mimic DKA with near-normal pH (ketosis with pH ≥7.4); cocaine is an independent risk factor for recurrent DKA (OR 4.38). Alcoholic and starvation ketoacidosis are the main non-diabetic mimics and need dextrose rather than an insulin infusion.

## 4. Pregnancy

### Takeaway
Pregnancy is "a ketogenic state" and DKA occurs at lower glucose (ADA 2026), including euglycemic DKA <200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) (2024 consensus; ADA 2026 Section 6); up to 2% of pregnancies with pregestational diabetes develop DKA, mostly T1D. Fetal loss in contemporary series ranges from 6.3% (US, Ohio, 2012-2021) to 17.2% (Mayo, 2004-2021) to 28% (South Africa, 2020-2022), with maternal hypokalemia linked to fetal death. Management is standard DKA with these changes: involve obstetrics/senior medicine immediately and manage in a delivery suite/HDU/ICU; use a pregnancy bicarbonate cut-off of <15 mmol/L (JBDS) because bicarbonate is physiologically low; start 10% dextrose alongside saline earlier (JBDS: when glucose <14 mmol/L [~250 mg/dL]); give fluids more cautiously; keep K+ 4.0-5.5 mmol/L; continuous fetal monitoring, expecting fetal heart abnormalities to improve as the mother is corrected; consider antenatal betamethasone and hyperemesis as precipitants. No ACOG, SMFM or JBDS pregnancy-DKA update dated 2024-2026 was found; the JBDS pregnancy document is still the May 2017 version.

  - Diagnosis: "Presence of diabetes mellitus (of any kind, DKA can occur in pregnancy in a woman with known diabetes with a normal blood glucose)"; "Ketosis: urinary ketones >++ or blood ketones >3.0 mmol/L (high risk 1.5 mmol/L)"; "Acidosis: blood gas pH <7.3 and/or bicarbonate <15 mmol/L (N.B. bicarbonate is reduced in pregnancy). Use venous blood gases." "The normal range in pregnancy is not established, but outside pregnancy <1.0 mmol/L is normal."
  - Pump: "If the woman is using an insulin pump discontinue the insulin pump and start intravenous insulin infusion at a fixed rate."
  - Insulin: "deliver insulin at a fixed rate of 0.1 unit/kg of body weight/hour"; "A maximum dose limit of 14 units per hour"; increase "by 1 unit/hour if there is inadequate response (less than 3 mmol/L drop in CBG per hour or less than 0.5 mmol/L drop in blood ketone or less than 3 mmol/L rise in venous bicarbonate per hour)"; "Glucose level is not an accurate indicator of resolution of acidosis in euglycaemic ketoacidosis, so the acidosis resolution should be verified by venous gas analysis."; "Continue with the basal insulin ... but discontinue short acting insulin."
  - Fluids/dextrose: "The fluid requirement may be lower in pregnancy. Start with 1L 0.9% NaCl over 60 minutes"; "Add 10% dextrose to run alongside 0.9% NaCl when capillary glucose <14 mmol/L. Initially this should be administered at a rate of 125 ml/hr but rate of infusion may need to be adjusted to prevent hypoglycaemia and avoid fluid overload or hyponatraemia."
  - Potassium: "Potassium may not be needed in the first bag. Aim for keeping K+ between 4.0 and 5.5 mmol/L. Add 40 mmol/L of normal saline from the 2nd litre of fluids onward."
  - Monitoring: "Monitor CBG and capillary ketones (if available) hourly, venous bicarbonate and potassium at 1 hour, 2 hours and 4 hours, plasma electrolytes 4 hourly." "The fetus should be continually monitored but abnormalities of the fetal heart may improve with improvement of the maternal condition."
  - Steroids: "CBG should be monitored hourly when mothers are administered steroids in pregnancy. The most effective way to control steroid-induced hyperglycaemia is by using VRIII". [verified]
- JBDS DKA adult guideline (March 2023): DKA in pregnancy "can occur with only very modest elevation of glucose levels, i.e. <13.9mmol/l, ('euglycaemic DKA')". [secondary here — verified by sibling researcher, guidelines_diagnosis.md]
