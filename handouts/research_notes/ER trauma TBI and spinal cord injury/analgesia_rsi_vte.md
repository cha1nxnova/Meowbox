- EAST/Trauma Anesthesiology Society 2016 blunt thoracic trauma pain PMG (Galvagno SM, Smith CE, Varon AJ, ... Haut ER. J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2016;81(5):936-951. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000001209. PMID 27533913). GRADE; 5 systematic reviews; literature Jan 1967 to Aug 2015; 70 articles identified, 28 used. Abstract wording: "We conditionally recommend epidural analgesia and multimodal analgesia as options for patients with blunt thoracic trauma, but the overall quality of evidence supporting these modalities is low in trauma patients." Quality of evidence for paravertebral block, intrapleural analgesia, multimodal analgesia and intercostal nerve blocks "very low"; "The limitations with the available literature precluded the formulation of strong recommendations"; recommendations "are in contradistinction to the previously published Practice Management Guideline published by EAST" (2005, which had recommended epidural) [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27533913/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000001209)
- EAST + Chest Wall Injury Society 2023 older adults with multiple rib fractures (Mukherjee K, Schubl SD, Tominaga G, ... Haut ER, Kasotakis G. J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2023;94(3):398-407. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000003830. PMID 36730672; erratum J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2023;95(3):e23, PMID 37608454). Recommendation wording (abstract): "We do not recommend for or against routine ICU admission. We recommend use of IS [incentive spirometry] to inform ICU status and conditionally recommend use of NIPPV in patients without risk of airway loss. We offer no recommendation for or against ketamine, epidural, or other locoregional analgesia." Data: NIPPV associated with 85% reduction in odds of pneumonia (p<0.0001) and 81% reduction in odds of mortality (p=0.03); "Ketamine use demonstrated no significant reduction in pain score but a trend toward reduced opioid use"; epidural/locoregional techniques did not affect pneumonia, ventilator days, LOS or mortality [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36730672/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000003830)
- Carver TW et al. Ketamine infusion for rib fractures RCT (J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2019;86(2):181-188. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000002103. PMID 30376537): single Level I center, double-blind placebo-controlled; >=3 rib fractures; excluded age >64, GCS <13, chronic opioid use. Low-dose ketamine 2.5 mcg/kg/min (= 0.15 mg/kg/h) vs saline x 48 h. n=91 (45 ketamine). Primary outcome (24-h numeric pain score) NOT reduced; OME not reduced overall. Subgroup ISS >15 (n=45): OME 0-24 h 35.7 vs 68 (p=0.03); 24-48 h 64.2 vs 96 (p=0.03); overall 152.1 vs 198 (p=0.048). No difference in adverse events [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30376537/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000002103)
- WSES 2023 guidelines on trauma in elderly/frail patients (De Simone B et al. World J Emerg Surg 2024;19(1):18. DOI 10.1186/s13017-024-00537-8. PMID 38816766): "Acute trauma pain in the elderly has to be managed in a multimodal analgesic approach, to avoid side effects of opioid use." (non-US society, GRADE) [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38816766/)
- O'Donohoe RB et al. Alfred (Melbourne) blunt chest wall pathway, age >=65 (Injury 2026;57(9):113155. DOI 10.1016/j.injury.2026.113155. PMID 41881750): n=2488; adequate analgesia 87.7% post-pathway vs 76.4% pre (p<0.001); receiving adequate analgesia associated with lower in-hospital mortality (OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.36-0.91) — observational [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41881750/)

PMID 41369227. PMC12711137. NCT05277896). 14 US EDs and ICUs; n=2365 (ketamine 1176, etomidate 1189). Primary: in-hospital death by day 28: 330/1173 (28.1%) ketamine vs 345/1186 (29.1%) etomidate; site-adjusted risk difference -0.8 percentage points (95% CI -4.5 to 2.9; P=0.65). McDougall 2026 Chest NMA (DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2026.07.5239; PMID 42612901): 21 RCTs, 6031 pts; ketamine vs etomidate hemodynamic instability RR 1.31 (1.15-1.48; moderate); ketamine-propofol vs etomidate RR 0.44 (0.27-0.72; low); etomidate increased adrenal suppression [verified: abstract]. Loughnan 2026 BJA meta-analysis (DOI 10.1016/j.bja.2026.02.023; PMID 41951491): 10 RCTs, n=4673; postinduction instability RR 1.28 (1.13-1.45) ketamine vs etomidate; no mortality difference [verified: abstract] — [Zampieri](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-026-06067-w); [McDougall](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2026.07.5239); [Loughnan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bja.2026.02.023)

## Q3. VTE prophylaxis in trauma (agents, dosing, anti-Xa, timing by injury type, IVC filters)

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings
- PREVENT CLOT (Major Extremity Trauma Research Consortium [METRC]; O'Toole RV, Stein DM, O'Hara NN, ... Haut ER, ... Goldhaber SZ, Marvel D, Castillo RC. Aspirin or Low-Molecular-Weight Heparin for Thromboprophylaxis after a Fracture. N Engl J Med 2023;388(3):203-213. DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2205973. PMID 36652352. NCT02984384). Pragmatic multicenter noninferiority RCT; age >=18 with operatively treated extremity fracture (hip to midfoot or shoulder to wrist) or any pelvic/acetabular fracture; enoxaparin 30 mg BID vs aspirin 81 mg BID in hospital; post-discharge per hospital protocol. n=12,211 (aspirin 6101; LMWH 6110); mean age 44.6 +/- 17.8; 0.7% prior VTE; 2.5% history of cancer; mean 8.8 +/- 10.6 in-hospital doses; median 21-day supply at discharge. Primary (90-day all-cause death): 47 (0.78%) aspirin vs 45 (0.73%) LMWH; difference 0.05 percentage points (96.2% CI -0.27 to 0.38; P<0.001 for noninferiority margin 0.75 points). DVT 2.51% vs 1.71% (difference 0.80 points; 95% CI 0.28 to 1.31). PE 1.49% in each group; bleeding complications and other serious AEs similar [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36652352/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2205973)
- Western Trauma Association 2020 updated VTE algorithm (Ley EJ, Brown CVR, Moore EE, Sava JA, Peck K, Ciesla DJ, Sperry JL, Rizzo AG, Rosen NG, Brasel KJ, Kozar R, Inaba K, Martin MJ. J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2020;89(5):971-981. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000002830. PMID 32590563. PMC7587238). No graded evidence levels: "these recommendations are based primarily on published prospective and retrospective cohort studies, and expert opinion of the WTA members." Key statements, quoted from PMC full text [verified: full text]:
  - Risk: "an ISS of 10 or more suggests that pharmacologic prophylaxis should be initiated as soon as possible"; "trauma patients who require hospital admission for more than 24 hours require pharmacologic prophylaxis, whereas those hospitalized for less than 24 hours do not." Spine/pelvic fractures, venous repair, prior VTE, thrombophilia = higher risk; minor-injury predictors = age, obesity, lower extremity fracture.
  - Bleeding hold criteria: "a hemoglobin drop of greater than 2 g/dL in under 12 hours or ongoing blood transfusion is an appropriate indication to delay"; trauma hypocoagulability "largely resolves within 24 hours, after which hypercoagulability becomes prevalent."
  - Solid organ injury: early prophylaxis within 12 to 24 h "appeared to be safe across moderate AAST injury grade"; "Although those with grade IV and V injuries should be approached with caution, pharmacologic prophylaxis may be initiated within 24 hours for most patients with solid organ injury."
  - TBI: "cerebral contusion, localized petechial hemorrhages, or diffuse axonal damage" may receive prophylaxis without delay; hold if follow-up CT shows progression until a CT shows no progression; "progression of TBI occurs in about 10% of patients with a stable follow-up CT, regardless of whether pharmacologic prophylaxis is provided or not"; "The majority of TBI patients with a stable CT may be initiated on enoxaparin within 24 hours, and nearly all TBI patients should receive pharmacologic prophylaxis within 72 hours of the time of injury."
  - Spine: "delays longer than 72 hours lead to a substantial increase in the VTE rate. Pharmacologic prophylaxis must be initiated as soon as possible after spine surgery or any spine injury"; prophylaxis within 48 h of operative fixation did not increase bleeding, neuro progression or spinal hematoma.
  - General timing (section G): "Pharmacologic prophylaxis must be initiated as soon as possible and for most trauma patients may be initiated within 24 hours ... in almost every case, pharmacologic prophylaxis may be started in less than 72 hours." Holding 12 to 24 h before surgery "is almost always unnecessary"; benefit lost if dose given >12 h pre-op.
  - Dosing (section H): "Enoxaparin is the recommended choice for most trauma patients with higher doses now considered the standard of care." "40 mg of enoxaparin twice daily should be considered the standard for most trauma patients, as 30 mg twice daily frequently results in inadequate pharmacologic prophylaxis" — for age 18 to 65 y, weight >50 kg, CrCl >60 (text says "mg/dL"; presumably mL/min). Age >65 y, weight <50 kg, or CrCl 30 to 60: 30 mg BID. Weight-based options: 0.5 mg/kg BID, 0.6 mg/kg BID, or 30 mg (50-60 kg) / 40 mg (61-99 kg) / 50 mg (>100 kg); monitor anti-Xa when weight-based. ESRD or CrCl <30: UFH 5000 U q8h. TBI and spine: start enoxaparin 30 mg BID and consider anti-Xa-guided adjustment; enoxaparin associated with less VTE and higher survival than UFH in TBI with no difference in lesion progression. Pregnancy: 30 mg BID titrated to anti-Xa peak 0.2-0.4 or trough 0.1-0.2 IU/mL; >90 kg start 40 mg BID. DOACs or aspirin "should not be a primary choice ... may be considered in the setting of isolated orthopedic injuries, but only if the patient declines injection" (note: pre-dates PREVENT CLOT 2023 — superseded in part).
  - UFH 5000 U TID vs enoxaparin: trial cited showed UFH 8.2% vs enoxaparin 5.1% VTE (p=0.2), underpowered; "more recently, 30 mg of enoxaparin twice daily was established as superior to 5000 U of unfractionated heparin three times daily".
  - Anti-Xa (section I): "84% of trauma patients required doses of 40 mg or more, and 18% required doses of 50 mg or more"; "consensus suggests targeting 0.2 to 0.4 IU/mL for peak levels or 0.1 to 0.2 IU/mL for trough levels."
  - Missed doses (section J): missing 2 to 4 doses = 8.5x higher DVT risk; interrupted dosing in TBI ~600% increase in VTE. Epidural: 12-h interval between prophylactic enoxaparin and catheter placement/removal, resume 4 to 12 h after; UFH 4 to 6 h before, 1 h after. HIT: clinical HIT 2.7% with prophylactic heparin vs 0% with enoxaparin; platelet monitoring for high-risk about every 3 days from day 4 to 14.
  - IVC filter (section K): "prophylactic placement is not recommended"; filter "should be considered" for acute proximal DVT or PE when therapeutic anticoagulation is contraindicated.
  - Post-discharge (section L): consider for TBI, orthopedic/spine injury, major surgery; avoid doses >30 mg BID post-discharge; up to 4 weeks from admission; up to 35 days after major orthopedic surgery; aspirin or DOAC options.
  — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7587238/) / [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000002830)
- Geerts WH et al. (N Engl J Med 1996;335(10):701-707. DOI 10.1056/NEJM199609053351003. PMID 8703169): RCT, ISS >=9, no intracranial bleeding; UFH 5000 U q12h vs enoxaparin 30 mg q12h, started within 36 h. Venography-adequate n=265: DVT 44% (60/136) UFH vs 31% (40/129) enoxaparin (P=0.014); proximal DVT 15% vs 6% (P=0.012); RRR 30% (95% CI 4-50) all DVT, 58% (12-87) proximal; major bleeding 1.7% overall (1 UFH, 5 enoxaparin; P=0.12) [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8703169/)
- Ho KM et al. Vena cava filter trial (N Engl J Med 2019;381(4):328-337. DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1806515. PMID 31259488): n=240, ISS >15 with contraindication to anticoagulation; filter within 72 h vs none. Composite symptomatic PE or death at 90 d: 13.9% vs 14.4% (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.51-1.94; P=0.98). Subgroup not anticoagulated within 7 days (46 filter, 34 control): PE 0 vs 5 (14.7%) [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31259488/)
- AAST/ACS-COT inpatient VTE prophylaxis clinical protocol (Yorkgitis BK, Berndtson AE, Cross A, Kennedy R, Kochuba MP, Tignanelli C, Tominaga GT, Jacobs DG, Marx WH, Ashley DW, Ley EJ, Napolitano L, Costantini TW. J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2022;92(3):597-604. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000003475. PMID 34797813). Consensus (Level V), adults age >=15; goals "(1) provide standardized medication dosing for VTE prophylaxis in the injured patient; and (2) promote evidence-based, prompt VTE prophylaxis in common, high-risk traumatic injuries" [verified: abstract; body not open access]
- AAST/ACS-COT clinical protocol for postdischarge VTE prophylaxis after trauma (Berndtson AE et al. J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2024;96(6):980-985. DOI 10.1097/TA.0000000000004307. PMID 38523134): aims to identify highest-risk patients after discharge (lower extremity fractures, limited mobility) and offer pharmacologic strategies [verified: abstract only; specific agents/durations not in abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38523134/)
- Erstad BL (Am J Health Syst Pharm 2023;80(5):258-266. DOI 10.1093/ajhp/zxac353. PMID 36445848): AAST and ACS (TQIP) documents give obesity dosing recommendations "but they were not formulated using GRADE"; "Dosing strategies for obese trauma patients are largely extrapolated from studies conducted in nonobese patients"; most studies rely on anti-Xa rather than VTE outcomes; evidence strength low [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36445848/)
- Alexander KM et al. AAST survey (Trauma Surg Acute Care Open 2023;8(1):e001070. DOI 10.1136/tsaco-2022-001070. PMID 37205274): 118 respondents (6.9% response); most common dose enoxaparin 30 mg q12h (67.8%); 74.6% adjust in obesity; 66.1% routinely use anti-Xa; guideline-directed dosing more often when team included a clinical pharmacist (88.2% vs 69.0%; p=0.0142); wide variability in timing after TBI, solid organ injury, SCI [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37205274/)
- Heye 2023 (J Surg Res 2023;290:209-214; DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2023.04.019; PMID 37285702): spine fixation pts, enoxaparin 0.5 mg/kg q12h with anti-Xa monitoring started 24-48 h post-op; bleeding 0% (0/68) vs 2.94% fixed dose (30 mg BID or 40 mg daily) — small, retrospective [verified: abstract]
- Kakkos 2022 Cochrane (CD005258.pub4; PMID 35089599): IPC + pharmacologic vs pharmacologic alone: PE 0.91% vs 1.84% (OR 0.46, 0.30-0.71; low certainty); DVT 5.48% vs 9.28% (OR 0.38, 0.21-0.70; high certainty); adding IPC did not increase bleeding [verified: abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005258.pub4)
- WSES 2023 elderly trauma: "Venous thromboembolism prophylaxis with LMWH or UFH should be administrated as soon as possible in high and moderate-risk elderly trauma patients according to the renal function, weight of the patient and bleeding risk." [verified: abstract] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38816766/)
