# ER Management of Adult Traumatic Brain Injury: Pharmacy Focus (research notes, current to Nov 20, 2026)

Scope: adults, US ER, stabilization and first hours; short pediatric note. Audience: ER clinical pharmacist preceptor.
Tag legend (applies to every bullet): [verified] = read this session in the primary document (guideline PDF, PMC full text, PubMed abstract, or ClinicalTrials.gov record); [secondary] = news/regulatory digest, aggregator, or a guideline's reproduction of another document; [recalled] = from memory, NOT confirmed this session — check before printing.
Citation-author rule: for PubMed-derived citations, the first two authors, title, journal, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI and PMID were read from PubMed metadata this session [verified]; any third-or-later author named in a citation is [recalled] unless the full author list is given (full lists were read for Carney 2017 exec summary, Frontera 2024, Vallejo 2026, Bernard 2022, Tatro 2021, Karamian 2024, Puzio 2021, Huang 2020).
Big "what changed" flags for Nov 2026: (1) andexanet alfa withdrawn from US market Dec 2025; (2) NCS/SCCM focused reversal-guideline update epub Sept 24, 2026 (4F-PCC over andexanet; no recommendation on platelets/DDAVP in traumatic ICH); (3) NCS 2024 seizure-prophylaxis guideline (prophylaxis optional; LEV preferred if used; ≤7 days); (4) TRAIN (2024) favors Hb <9 trigger over <7 in acute brain injury, HEMOTION (2024) neutral; (5) RSI trial (NEJM 2026) ketamine = etomidate for 28-day mortality, more peri-intubation collapse with ketamine; (6) CRASH-4 and BOOST-3 have NOT reported results; (7) no BTF adult severe-TBI 5th edition found (BTF said it would not produce one; only living update = 2020 decompressive craniectomy).

## 1. BTF Guidelines for the Management of Severe TBI, 4th edition (2016/2017) and 2020 DC living update: every recommendation and its level

### Takeaway
BTF 4th edition has 28 evidence-based recommendations: 1 Level I (AGAINST steroids; high-dose methylprednisolone contraindicated), 7 Level IIA, 10 Level IIB, 10 Level III. Pharmacy-relevant: phenytoin for early PTS (IIA; LEV "insufficient evidence"), barbiturates/propofol (IIB), DVT prophylaxis (III; agent/dose/timing insufficient), nutrition by day 5-7 (IIA), SBP thresholds (III), ICP >22 (IIB), CPP 60-70 (IIB). Hyperosmolar therapy has NO graded recommendation (mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg statement is a carried-over 3rd-edition statement "not supported by evidence meeting current standards").

### Cited Findings

Guidelines for the Management of Severe Traumatic Brain Injury, Fourth Edition. Neurosurgery. 2017;80(1):6-15 (online Sept 2016). doi:10.1227/NEU.0000000000001432. PMID 27654000. Endorsed by AANS and CNS. Population: adults, GCS 3-8. Full guideline: https://braintrauma.org/s/Management_of_Severe_TBI_4th_Edition.pdf.

General

These include 1 Level I, 7 Level IIA, 10 Level IIB, and 10 Level III recommendations. There are 7 Third Edition recommendations that are restated here but are no longer substantiated by evidence meeting current standards." Evidence: 189 publications (5 Class 1, 46 Class 2, 136 Class 3, 2 meta-analyses), 18 topics. [verified] — [BTF Exec Summary PDF](https://braintrauma.org/s/Management_of_Severe_TBI_ExecSmry_4th_Edition.pdf)
- Level definitions: "Level I recommendations were based on a high-quality body of evidence. Level IIA recommendations were based on a moderate-quality body of evidence. Level IIB and III recommendations were based on a low-quality body of evidence." IIB = Class 2 direct evidence of overall low quality; III = Class 3 or indirect Class 2. [verified] — same

Treatments (Table 1)
- Decompressive craniectomy, Level IIA (2016; later revised — see 2020 update): "Bifrontal DC is not recommended to improve outcomes as measured by the GOS-E score at 6 mo post-injury in severe TBI patients with diffuse injury (without mass lesions), and with ICP elevation to values >20 mm Hg for more than 15 min within a 1-h period that are refractory to first-tier therapies. However, this procedure has been demonstrated to reduce ICP and to minimize days in the ICU." / "A large frontotemporoparietal DC (not less than 12 x 15 cm or 15 cm diameter) is recommended over a small frontotemporoparietal DC for reduced mortality and improved neurologic outcomes in patients with severe TBI." [verified] — same
- Prophylactic hypothermia, Level IIB: "Early (within 2.5 h), short-term (48 h post-injury), prophylactic hypothermia is not recommended to improve outcomes in patients with diffuse injury." [verified] — same
- Hyperosmolar therapy — no graded recommendation; listed under "Recommendations from the prior (Third) Edition not supported by evidence meeting current standards. Mannitol is effective for control of raised ICP at doses of 0.25 to 1 g/kg body weight. Arterial hypotension (systolic blood pressure <90 mm Hg) should be avoided. Restrict mannitol use prior to ICP monitoring to patients with signs of transtentorial herniation or progressive neurologic deterioration not attributable to extracranial causes." [verified] — same
- CSF drainage, Level III: "An EVD system zeroed at the midbrain with continuous drainage of CSF may be considered to lower ICP burden more effectively than intermittent use." / "Use of CSF drainage to lower ICP in patients with an initial GCS <6 during the first 12 h after injury may be considered." [verified] — same
- Ventilation therapies, Level IIB: "Prolonged prophylactic hyperventilation with PaCO2 of ≤25 mm Hg is not recommended." Carried-over (not current-standard evidence): "Hyperventilation is recommended as a temporizing measure for the reduction of elevated ICP. Hyperventilation should be avoided during the first 24 h after injury when CBF often is reduced critically. If hyperventilation is used, SjO2 or BtpO2 measurements are recommended to monitor oxygen delivery." [verified] — same
- Anesthetics, analgesics, sedatives, Level IIB (all three bullets sit under one "Level IIB" heading): "Administration of barbiturates to induce burst suppression measured by EEG as prophylaxis against the development of intracranial hypertension is not recommended." / "High-dose barbiturate administration is recommended to control elevated ICP refractory to maximum standard medical and surgical treatment. Hemodynamic stability is essential before and during barbiturate therapy." / "Although propofol is recommended for the control of ICP, it is not recommended for improvement in mortality or 6-month outcomes. Caution is required as high-dose propofol can produce significant morbidity." [verified text; level attribution by table layout] — same
- Steroids, Level I: "The use of steroids is not recommended for improving outcome or reducing ICP. In patients with severe TBI, high-dose methylprednisolone was associated with increased mortality and is contraindicated." [verified] — same
- Nutrition, Level IIA: "Feeding patients to attain basal caloric replacement at least by the fifth day and at most by the seventh day post-injury is recommended to decrease mortality." Level IIB: "Transgastric jejunal feeding is recommended to reduce the incidence of ventilator-associated pneumonia." [verified] — same
- Infection prophylaxis, Level IIA: "Early tracheostomy is recommended to reduce mechanical ventilation days when the overall benefit is thought to outweigh the complications associated with such a procedure. However, there is no evidence that early tracheostomy reduces mortality or the rate of nosocomial pneumonia." / "The use of PI [povidone-iodine] oral care is not recommended to reduce ventilator-associated pneumonia and may cause an increased risk of acute respiratory distress syndrome." Level III: "Antimicrobial-impregnated catheters may be considered to prevent catheter-related infections during external ventricular drainage." [verified] — same
- DVT prophylaxis, Level III: "LMWH or low-dose unfractioned heparin may be used in combination with mechanical prophylaxis. However, there is an increased risk for expansion of intracranial hemorrhage." / "In addition to compression stockings, pharmacologic prophylaxis may be considered if the brain injury is stable and the benefit is considered to outweigh the risk of increased intracranial hemorrhage." / "There is insufficient evidence to support recommendations regarding the preferred agent, dose, or timing of pharmacologic prophylaxis for deep vein thrombosis." [verified] — same
- Seizure prophylaxis, Level IIA: "Prophylactic use of phenytoin or valproate is not recommended for preventing late PTS." / "Phenytoin is recommended to decrease the incidence of early PTS (within 7 d of injury), when the overall benefit is thought to outweigh the complications associated with such treatment. However, early PTS have not been associated with worse outcomes." / "At the present time there is insufficient evidence to recommend levetiracetam compared with phenytoin regarding efficacy in preventing early post-traumatic seizures and toxicity." [verified] — same

Monitoring (Table 2)
- ICP monitoring, Level IIB: "Management of severe TBI patients using information from ICP monitoring is recommended to reduce in-hospital and 2-week post-injury mortality." (Carried-over 3rd-ed indications, not current-standard evidence: GCS 3-8 + abnormal CT; or normal CT with ≥2 of age >40, posturing, SBP <90.) [verified] — same
- CPP monitoring, Level IIB: "Management of severe TBI patients using guidelines-based recommendations for CPP monitoring is recommended to decrease 2-wk mortality." [verified] — same
- Advanced cerebral monitoring, Level III: jugular bulb AVDO2 "may be considered to reduce mortality and improve outcomes at 3 and 6 mo post-injury." [verified] — same

Thresholds (Table 3)
- Blood pressure, Level III: "Maintaining SBP at ≥100 mm Hg for patients 50 to 69 years old or at ≥110 mm Hg or above for patients 15 to 49 or >70 years old may be considered to decrease mortality and improve outcomes." [verified] — same
- ICP, Level IIB: "Treating ICP >22 mm Hg is recommended because values above this level are associated with increased mortality." Level III: "A combination of ICP values and clinical and brain CT findings may be used to make management decisions." [verified] — same
- CPP, Level IIB: "The recommended target CPP value for survival and favorable outcomes is between 60 and 70 mm Hg. Whether 60 or 70 mm Hg is the minimum optimal CPP threshold is unclear and may depend upon the autoregulatory status of the patient." Level III: "Avoiding aggressive attempts to maintain CPP >70 mm Hg with fluids and pressors may be considered because of the risk of adult respiratory failure." [verified] — same
- Advanced monitoring threshold, Level III: "Jugular venous saturation of <50% may be a threshold to avoid in order to reduce mortality and improve outcomes." [verified] — same

### Inferences
- The only Level I statement for adult sTBI is AGAINST steroids; everything a pharmacist doses acutely (hyperosmolars, sedatives, ASMs, VTE prophylaxis) rests on IIA-III evidence or ungraded consensus. BTF gives no graded mannitol-vs-HTS preference; SIBICC/TQIP treat them as equivalent Tier 1 options with the same Na/Osm ceilings.
- BTF BP thresholds (age-banded, Level III) have effectively been superseded in practice by the simpler "SBP ≥110 mm Hg" target used in BTF Prehospital 3rd ed (2023) and TQIP 2024 for all adults (both verified).

### Gaps
- No BTF adult severe-TBI 5th edition or living updates beyond the 2020 DC update were found (searched braintrauma.org links surfaced by web search; not exhaustive). Recommend the handout state "4th edition (2016) + 2020 DC update remain current BTF adult documents as of Nov 2026" with that caveat.
- The 10 Level III / 10 Level IIB tallies were taken from the synopsis; the full 4th-edition document's per-topic "Evaluation of the Evidence" was not read.

## 2. BTF Prehospital Guidelines, 3rd edition (2023): targets (SpO2, SBP, EtCO2) and TXA

### Takeaway
GRADE-style strong/weak recommendations (40 total: 30 strong, 10 weak). Adults: SBP ≥110 mm Hg (weak; "optimal targets may be higher"), SpO2 >90% with supplemental O2 for all suspected severe TBI (strong), EtCO2 35-45 mm Hg with ~10 breaths/min (strong), hyperventilation only for active herniation to EtCO2 30-35 (strong), no prophylactic prehospital hyperosmolar therapy (weak), and "Prehospital administration of TXA ... is not generally and widely indicated" (strong).

### Cited Findings
- Citations: Lulla A, Lumba-Brown A, Totten AM, et al. Prehospital Guidelines for the Management of Traumatic Brain Injury - 3rd Edition. Prehosp Emerg Care. 2023;27(5):507-538. doi:10.1080/10903127.2023.2187905. PMID 37079803 [verified citation; no PubMed abstract]. Executive summary read in full: Guidelines for Prehospital Management of Traumatic Brain Injury 3rd Edition: Executive Summary. Neurosurgery. 2023 (Dec). doi:10.1227/neu.0000000000002672. PMID 37750693. PMC10627685 — recommendation tables "Reproduced from Lulla et al 2023 with permission". [verified] — [PMC10627685](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627685/)
- Evidence base: 122 articles (5 Class I, 35 Class II, 98 Class III), literature May 2005-Jan 2022, PROSPERO CRD42021269941; "40 evidence-based recommendations were generated, 30 of which were strong and 10 of which were weak" (2nd edition: all weak). [verified] — same
- Monitoring (Strong): "Patients with suspected TBI should be carefully monitored in the prehospital setting for hypoxemia (<90% arterial hemoglobin saturation), hypotension (<100 mm Hg SBP), hypertension (150 mm Hg SBP or higher), hyperventilation (end tidal CO2 reading between 35 to 45 mm Hg), and hypothermia or hyperthermia". [verified] — same (note: this monitoring bullet uses <100 while the target bullet uses ≥110)
- Adult SBP (Weak): "While no specific data exist for hard cutoff values, optimal adult specific SBPs following TBI are dependent on a variety of factors and should be targeted to 110 mm Hg or greater as lower values are associated with worse outcomes. Optimal targets may be higher". [verified] — same
- Pediatric SBP (Weak): ≥75th percentile for age; ≤28 days >70; 1-12 months >84; 1-5 years >90; ≥6 years >100; adults ≥110. [verified] — same
- Oxygen (Strong): "All patients with suspected severe TBI should be placed on continuous oxygen supplementation via nasal cannula or face mask"; (Strong) pulse oximetry with O2 "to maintain blood oxygen saturation above 90%"; (Weak) "should be targeted to 90% or greater ... Optimal targets may be higher". [verified] — same
- BP measured every 5-10 min or continuously (Strong); euthermia 98-99°F / 36-37°C (Weak, new); GCS every 30 min (Weak). [verified] — same
- Airway (Strong): establish airway for "signs of severe TBI, GCS <9, or 9 and decompensating, the inability to maintain an adequate airway, or if hypoxemia is not corrected by supplemental oxygen"; confirm ETT with ETCO2/capnography. [verified] — same
- Ventilation (Strong): "normal breathing rates (approximately 10 breaths per minute with ETCO2 35-45 mm Hg), and hyperventilation (ETCO2 <35 mm Hg) should be avoided." (Strong) "Ventilation strategies should target eucapnia and avoid hypocapnia (ie, ETCO2 of 35-40)". (Strong) "When used to address signs of active and imminent herniation, hyperventilation should target an ETCO2 of 30–35 mm Hg using capnography". Herniation signs: Cushing triad, GCS <9, posturing/lateralizing, progressive deterioration, fixed dilated pupil(s). [verified] — same
- Hyperosmolar (Weak): "Hyperosmolar therapy (mannitol and hypertonic saline) should not be administered for the prophylactic treatment of suspected elevated ICP, with or without signs of cerebral herniation, in the prehospital setting at this time". [verified] — same
- TXA (Strong): "Prehospital administration of TXA therapy is not generally and widely indicated for the prophylactic treatment of suspected ICH or elevated ICP at this time. However, decisions by health care systems may vary and further evidence may support more general use". Rationale: BRAIN-PROTECT registry (1827 pts; TXA associated with increased mortality, OR 1.34, p <.001), CRASH-3 benefit only mild/moderate, Rowell 2020 no benefit. [verified] — same
- Fluids: "Intravenous fluids should be administered ... to treat hypotension" (Strong); "Hypotensive patients should be treated with blood products and/or isotonic fluids" (Strong); "Hypertonic fluid resuscitation may be administered to patients with a GCS <8 in whom increased ICP is suspected in the prehospital setting" (Weak). [verified] — same
- Basis for higher SBP: Spaite DW, Hu C, Bobrow BJ, et al. Mortality and Prehospital Blood Pressure in Patients With Major TBI: Implications for the Hypotension Threshold. JAMA Surg. 2017;152(4):360-368. doi:10.1001/jamasurg.2016.4686. PMID 27926759. EPIC cohort, 3844 pts with SBP 40-119: no threshold; each 10-mm Hg increase → adjusted OR of death 0.812 (0.748-0.883). Exec summary: in the larger EPIC analysis (n = 12,169), lowest mortality with SBP >125. [verified] — [PubMed 27926759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27926759/)

### Inferences
- Prehospital and ED pharmacist messaging aligns: avoid even single hypotensive (SBP <110) or hypoxic (SpO2 <90%) episodes; do not give prophylactic hyperosmolars without herniation signs; avoid routine prehospital TXA for isolated TBI.

- Author list/volume/pages of the Neurosurgery executive summary not captured (PMID 37750693 verified). Full Lulla 2023 text (algorithm figure) not read.

## 3. ACS TQIP Best Practices Guidelines: The Management of TBI (2024): pharmacy-relevant items

### Takeaway
TQIP 2024 (released Oct 29, 2024; 99 pages; ungraded "Key Points") is the most pharmacy-dense US document: goal table (SBP ≥110, ICP <22, CPP 60-70, Na 135-145, Osm ≤320, INR ≤1.4, plt ≥75k, glucose 100-180, Hb ≥7, temp 36-37.9), Na 155 / Osm 320 ceilings for HTS or mannitol, ASM prophylaxis "consider" 7 days only if risk factors (PHT ≈ LEV; NOT valproate), INR-banded 4F-PCC table, no routine platelets for antiplatelet agents, VTE prophylaxis 24 h (low risk) or 24-48 h (moderate/high risk) after stable repeat CT with LMWH preferred. TXA is not mentioned anywhere in the document. The andexanet dosing in Table 7 is now outdated in the US.

### Cited Findings
Citation: American College of Surgeons Trauma Quality Programs. Best Practices Guidelines: The Management of Traumatic Brain Injury. Chicago: ACS; 2024. https://www.facs.org/media/vgfgjpfk/best-practices-guidelines-traumatic-brain-injury.pdf (PDF read in full-text extraction this session; page numbers = printed pages). Release date per ACS news: [ACS Brief Oct 29, 2024](https://ww2.facs.org/for-medical-professionals/news-publications/news-and-articles/acs-brief/october-29-2024-issue/acs-releases-revised-best-practice-guidelines-in-management-of-traumatic-brain-injury) [secondary].
- Table 4 "Goals of Treatment Recommended Parameters" (p20): Pulse oximetry ≥94%; PaO2 80–100 mm Hg; PaCO2 35–45 mm Hg; Systolic blood pressure ≥110 mm Hg; ICP <22 mm Hg; PbtO2 ≥15 mm Hg; CPP 60–70 mm Hg ("Depending on status of cerebral autoregulation"); Serum sodium 135–145 mEq/L; Serum osmolality ≤320 mOsm; INR ≤1.4; Temperature 36.0–37.9°C; Platelets ≥75 × 10^3/mm3; pH 7.35–7.45; Glucose 100–180 mg/dL; Hemoglobin ≥7 g/dL. These "represent ideal ranges rather than mandatory, at-all-costs goals". [verified] — [TQIP 2024 PDF](https://www.facs.org/media/vgfgjpfk/best-practices-guidelines-traumatic-brain-injury.pdf)
- BP (p20): "Providing treatment targeting SBP ≥ 110 mm Hg/MAP > 80 mm Hg will allow adequate cerebral perfusion in most cases when ICP is not being monitored." "...the probability of mortality increases linearly with every 10-point drop of SBP below 119 mm Hg in patients with TBI, suggesting that higher targets may be indicated than previously recognized." (No separate age-banded older-adult BP target found in TQIP 2024.) [verified] — same
- ICP/CPP/O2 (p20): "An ICP of 22 mm Hg is a useful initial threshold for treatment ... consider a treatment range of 20–25 mm Hg" for hazardous therapies; "A CPP of ≥ 60 mm Hg is a practical target"; "avoid PaO2 > 100 mm Hg, unless guided by brain oximetry." [verified] — same
- Electrolytes, glucose, temperature, heme (p20-21): "Preventing hyponatremia is critical to avoid exacerbation of cerebral edema." "Measure osmolality when mannitol is used as an osmotic agent." Glucose 100-180 mg/dL. Normothermia 36–37.9°C; "Treat fever aggressively." Transfusion threshold "≥ 7 g/dL" (written before TRAIN — see Section 8). "a target INR ≤ 1.4 is appropriate in most cases"; TEG/ROTEM/platelet function assays may guide targeted reversal. [verified] — same
- Hyperosmolar ceilings (p26, SIBICC footnote): "Limits for sodium (155 mEq/L) and osmolality (320 mEq/L) are recommended for administration of either hypertonic saline or mannitol." Tier One: "Administer mannitol by intermittent bolus (0.25–1.0 g/kg)" and "Administer hypertonic saline by intermittent bolus" (no HTS concentration/volume specified for adults). [verified] — same
- Older adults (p27): "evaluate pharmacotherapeutic strategies for dosage adjustments to prevent toxicity and prolonged duration of action" (lower Vd, hypoalbuminemia, reduced hepatic metabolism and renal function). [verified] — same
- Seizure prophylaxis (p54-55): Key Point: "Consider the use of ASM prophylaxis for 7 days following severe TBI to prevent early PTS if risk factors are present; however, its use for more than 7 days to prevent late PTS is not recommended." "Seizure prophylaxis is not recommended in TBI patients without intracranial bleeding, or in patients with isolated traumatic subarachnoid hemorrhage." Risk factors: penetrating injury, immediate PTS, depressed skull fracture, SDH, ICH, GCS <10, cortical contusions. "phenytoin and levetiracetam appear to be similar in safety and efficacy for early PTS". Phenytoin level "usually monitored 24 hours after initial dosing and once more during the 7 days"; monitor albumin/free phenytoin. LEV behavioral ADRs; "Pyridoxine 50–100 mg daily supplementation may limit these behavioral ADRs." "Valproic acid is not recommended for early PTS prophylaxis due to an increased risk of death compared to phenytoin." "Do not continue PTS prophylaxis for more than 7 days in patients with no clinical or EEG seizure activity, or with seizure activity only within the first 24 hours postinjury." [verified] — same
- Anticoagulant reversal (p55): "If the last dose of a DOAC was within the past 8–12 hours (or the INR is elevated ≥ 1.5 for patients receiving warfarin), the benefits of a reversal agent will most likely outweigh the risks." Some use fixed 4F-PCC 1500-2500 units; "Lower doses of 4PCC (10–20 units/kg) are suggested for patients with an INR 1.5–1.9" (higher doses >2000-3000 units associated with VTE in nontraumatic ICH). FFP: "approximately 8–16 units of FFP are equivalent to approximately 20–50 units/kg of 4PCC"; lowest achievable INR with FFP ~1.5-1.7. "Do not delay reversal therapy while waiting for laboratory results in emergent situations". Normal PT/aPTT may not rule out therapeutic DOAC levels. [verified] — same
- Table 7 "Anticoagulation Reversal Strategy for Patients with TBI Needing Emergent Surgery" (p56), verbatim: Warfarin — "2.5–10 mg of IV vitamin K and: If INR is 1.5–1.9, give 15 units/kg 4PCC IV; If INR is 2–3.9, give 25 units/kg 4PCC IV; If INR is 4–6, give 35 units/kg 4PCC IV; If INR is > 6, give 50 units/kg 4PCC IV -or- 1500 units 4PCC (fixed dose)"; recheck INR 1 h after 4PCC (if >1.4, consider 2–4 units FFP) and at 6–8 h (if >1.4, another 2.5–10 mg IV vitamin K). DOACs (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) — charcoal if last dose <2 h; "Andexanet alfa – If last dose was > 7 hours, administer 400 mg IV bolus followed by 480 mg IV infusion – If last dose was < 7 hours, administer 800 mg IV bolus followed by 960 mg IV infusion"; "If andexanet alfa is not available, use 25–50 units/kg of 4PCC or a fixed dose of 2000 units"; last dose >18–24 h → reversal "may not be beneficial". Dabigatran — charcoal if <2 h; "two consecutive doses of 2.5 g of idarucizumab IV, with each dose being infused over 10 minutes"; >24 h may not benefit; consider dialysis; if no idarucizumab, 4PCC or single-factor [3F] PCC 50 units/kg. [verified] — same. SUPERSEDED: andexanet line (US withdrawal Dec 2025; NCS/SCCM 2026 prefers 4F-PCC).
- Restart anticoagulation: "no later than 14–90 days after TBI"; "waiting 2 weeks or less for patients at high risk and 7–8 weeks for low-risk patients, but not more than 90 days". High-dose vitamin K (>2.5 mg) may delay warfarin re-effect for weeks → bridge with LMWH/UFH. [verified] — same (p54, 56)
- Antiplatelets (p54, 57): "Routine platelet transfusion is not recommended for use in reversing antiplatelet agent effects." For a neurosurgical procedure: "consider one single-donor apheresis unit of platelets (equivalent to 6 pooled units or 1 random-donor unit per 10 kg of body weight). Desmopressin (0.4 mcg/kg IV) was also suggested for consideration alone or in addition to a platelet transfusion for neurosurgical patients who received aspirin, clopidogrel, or ticagrelor. If desmopressin is administered, monitor for hyponatremia". "Platelet function testing prior to transfusion is recommended whenever possible." "Until more data are available, platelet transfusion and desmopressin are not recommended for patients with TBI who are not candidates for an invasive intervention." Restart antiplatelets "as early as 4 days after injury". [verified] — same
- VTE prophylaxis (p50-51) Key Points: "VTE prophylaxis is recommended within 24 hours after injury in patients with low-risk nonoperative TBI, provided the follow-up CT shows no progression of intracranial injury." / "within 24 to 48 hours after injury in patients with moderate- or high-risk nonoperative TBI, provided the follow-up head CT shows no progression" / post-craniotomy or craniectomy "consider initiating or resuming ... within 24 to 48 hours after surgery if ICH is stable on postoperative CT" / "Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) is preferred over unfractionated heparin (UFH) for prophylaxis in TBI patients." Mechanical prophylaxis for all on admission; repeat head CT within 24 h before chemoprophylaxis; "Withholding pharmacologic VTE prophylaxis prior to ICP monitor placement or while the monitor is in place is not recommended." Risk tiers (modified Berne-Norwood): moderate = SDH/EDH >8 mm, contusion or IVH >2 cm, multiple contusions per lobe, scattered SAH, progression at 24 h; high = ICP monitor, craniotomy, progression at 72 h. [verified] — same
- Beta-blockers (p54, 58): "Beta blockers (e.g., propranolol) may be considered for treatment of TBI patients with adrenergic stress or paroxysmal hyperactivity syndrome ('storming')"; "Propranolol ... has the most evidence"; evidence low-quality, mostly observational. [verified] — same
- Amantadine (p58): "amantadine (100–200 mg twice daily) is recommended for adults with vegetative state/unresponsive wakefulness syndrome or minimally conscious state (4–16 weeks postinjury)" — not an ED therapy. [verified] — same

## 4. SIBICC tiered algorithms (Hawryluk 2019, ICP-monitored; Chesnut 2020, PbtO2 + ICP) and CREVICE (non-monitored)

### Takeaway
SIBICC (consensus, Class III, 42 experts, ≥80% agreement) defines Tier 0 basic care plus Tiers 1-3 for raised ICP (10, 4, and 3 interventions). Tier 1 includes analgesia/sedation to lower ICP, PaCO2 35-38, mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg bolus or HTS bolus (no preferred agent, same Na/Osm ceilings), CSF drainage, 1-week ASM prophylaxis; Tier 2 mild hypocapnia 32-35, NMB trial, MAP challenge; Tier 3 barbiturates (only if test dose works), secondary DC, mild hypothermia 35-36°C. "Not recommended" list includes continuous mannitol infusion, scheduled hyperosmolar dosing, furosemide, routine steroids, high-dose propofol for burst suppression, PaCO2 <30, CPP >90, hypothermia <35°C, lumbar drain. Note: the 2020 Chesnut SIBICC paper is the PbtO2 + ICP algorithm; the non-monitored consensus is CREVICE (Chesnut, J Neurotrauma 2020).

## 5. Hyperosmolar therapy: mannitol vs hypertonic saline, limits, COBI, prehospital HTS, meta-analyses 2020-2026

### Takeaway
Mannitol 0.25-1 g/kg bolus and HTS bolus are equivalent Tier 1 options (SIBICC/TQIP), with ceilings Na 155 mEq/L and osmolality 320; no outcome difference in RCT meta-analyses (HTS: more hypernatremia, possibly better CPP at 30-60 min, longer effect, shorter ICU stay). Continuous 20% saline (COBI) did not improve 6-month outcome. Prehospital 250 mL 7.5% saline ± dextran (Bulger ROC 2010) did not improve outcome or survival. Prefer HTS when hypotensive/hypovolemic (mannitol is an osmotic diuretic; BTF carried-over statement warns against SBP <90).

## 7. TXA in TBI: CRASH-3, Rowell 2020, PATCH-Trauma, CRASH-4, meta-analyses

## 8. Transfusion thresholds: HEMOTION and TRAIN

### Takeaway
HEMOTION (TBI-only, ≤10 vs ≤7 g/dL) was neutral on the primary outcome (point estimate favoring liberal, 68.4% vs 73.5% unfavorable). TRAIN (TBI, aSAH, ICH; <9 vs <7 g/dL) found less unfavorable outcome with liberal transfusion (62.6% vs 72.6%; P = .002) and less cerebral ischemia. ## 9. Antithrombotic reversal in traumatic intracranial hemorrhage

### Takeaway
Andexanet is gone from the US (FDA safety communication Dec 18, 2025; sales stopped Dec 22, 2025). The NCS/SCCM focused update (epub Sept 24, 2026) conditionally recommends 4F-PCC rather than andexanet for factor Xa inhibitors, VHA (TEG/ROTEM)-guided treatment of coagulopathy in traumatic ICH, and makes NO recommendation on platelet transfusion or desmopressin for antiplatelet agents in traumatic ICH. ## 10. Other drugs and trials: induction/sedation, steroids, progesterone, EPO, hypothermia, beta-blockers, amantadine, glucose, normothermia, brain-oxygen trials, 2024-2026 landmarks

### Takeaway
Steroids harm (CRASH: death 21.1% vs 17.9%). Progesterone (ProTECT III, SyNAPSe) and EPO (EPO-TBI) no benefit. Prophylactic hypothermia no benefit (POLAR) and therapeutic hypothermia for raised ICP harmful (Eurotherm3235). Ketamine does not meaningfully raise ICP (low-quality evidence) and the 2026 RSI trial found no mortality difference vs etomidate but more peri-intubation cardiovascular collapse with ketamine (not TBI-specific). PbtO2-guided care: OXY-TC neutral; BOOST-3 not reported (primary completion Nov 2027).

### Inferences
- Strongest evidence statements for the preceptor discussion: Level I no steroids; RCT evidence that TXA helps only early mild-moderate TBI; hyperosmolar agents are interchangeable with Na/Osm ceilings; seizure prophylaxis is optional and short; reversal now pivots on 4F-PCC.
