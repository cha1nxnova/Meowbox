#### Raw findings log (appended as gathered; synthesized below at end)
- Patanwala AE, Hays DP. Pharmacist's activities on a trauma response team in the emergency department. Am J Health Syst Pharm. 2010;67(18):1536-8. doi:10.2146/ajhp090311. PMID 20811031. Level I academic center, ED pharmacist 40 h/wk; 2-month period; 304 interventions; dosage recommendations 60%, drug information 27%; top classes analgesics, sedatives, antimicrobials, vaccines, fluids; pharmacist facilitated drug administration in 83% of interventions. [verified: abstract]
- Montgomery K, Hall AB, Keriazes G. Pharmacist's impact on acute pain management during trauma resuscitation. J Trauma Nurs. 2015;22(2):87-90. doi:10.1097/JTN.0000000000000112. PMID 25768964. Level II center, 2009-2013, 340 patients receiving IV opioid in trauma bay; mean time to first analgesic 17 vs 21 min with vs without pharmacist (P=.03); pain-score reduction similar (2.4 vs 2.7 points). [verified: abstract]
- Porter AL, Zaeem M, Hewes A, Hale S, Jones C, Gestring ML (first six authors listed). Pharmacist involvement in trauma resuscitation across the United States: a 10-year follow-up survey. Am J Health Syst Pharm. 2019;76(16):1226-1230. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxz124. PMID 31369114. Survey March 2017, response 33% (110/335 ACS trauma centers); pharmacist involvement rose from 23% (2007) to 70% (2017) (p<0.001); 71% of these provided by ED-based pharmacists; bedside roles: calculating doses 96%, preparing medications 89%, medication information 79%; administrative roles 45% (education, med safety, QI, ACS accreditation prep); main reason for no pharmacist: unfamiliarity with role/benefits. [verified: abstract]
- Masic D, Hidalgo DC, Kuhrau S, Chaney W, Rech MA. Pharmacist presence decreases time to prothrombin complex concentrate in emergency department patients with life-threatening bleeding and urgent procedures. J Emerg Med. 2019;57(5):620-628. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2019.06.027. PMID 31447188. Retrospective 2014-2018; 116 included (PharmD n=50, control n=66); 68.1% warfarin; ICH 67.2%; median time to 4F-PCC 66.5 vs 206.5 min (p<0.001); pharmacist at bedside only independent predictor (beta -163.5 min, 95% CI -249.4 to -77.7); no difference in hemostasis or mortality; ICU LOS 2 vs 5 d (p<0.01); hospital LOS 5.5 vs 8 d (p=0.02). [verified: abstract]
- Korsch, Knapp. Improving massive transfusion protocol response in the rural Level III trauma center setting. J Trauma Nurs. 2026;33(1):60-66. doi:10.1097/JTN.0000000000000897. PMID 41505189. QI at 2 rural Level III centers Aug 2023-Feb 2024; 35 respondents across 8 MTP activations incl 5 pharmacists (14%); appropriate calcium administration 87.5% of cases; barriers: inaccurate paging 50%, communication 62.5%, rapid infuser issues 50%. [verified: abstract]
- Andexanet alfa (Andexxa): FDA safety communication Dec 18, 2025 concluded risks outweigh benefits (postmarketing serious/fatal thromboembolic events); AstraZeneca ended US sales Dec 22, 2025 and requested voluntary BLA withdrawal; ANNEXA-I thrombosis 14.6% vs 6.9% usual care. Sources: FDA "Update on safety of Andexxa" https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/update-safety-andexxa-astrazeneca-fda-safety-communication ; AABB news 2025-12-23 https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2025/12/23/astrazeneca-withdraws-factor-xa-reversal-agent-from-u.s.-market (accessed 2026-11-20). [secondary: search digest of FDA/AABB pages; to verify]
- IV fluids: FDA announced resolution of 0.9% sodium chloride injection shortage Aug 8, 2025 (reported Aug 8-13 by AHA/AABB); other IV fluids still listed in shortage at that time. Baxter North Cove (Marion, NC) damaged by Hurricane Helene late Sept 2024; restarted Nov 2024; pre-hurricane production by Feb 2025; inventory fully restored May 2025. Sources: AHA News 2025-08-11 https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2025-08-11-fda-announces-end-iv-saline-solution-shortage ; AABB 2025-08-11 https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2025/08/11/fda-announces-resolution-of-nationwide-iv-saline-shortage (accessed 2026-11-20). [secondary: search digest]
- Mercer K, Ofoegbuna I, Razzack H, Feinstein J, Gonzalez M, et al. Presence of emergency medicine pharmacists is associated with shorter time to administration of prothrombin complex concentrate administration regardless of dosing strategy. Am J Emerg Med. 2025;95:147-152. doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2025.05.032. PMID 40450951. Single center 2016-2022; 147 pts (94 weight-based, 47 fixed-dose); fixed-dose without pharmacist 58.5 vs weight-based 71 min; fixed-dose WITH pharmacist 21 vs 58.5 min without (p<0.0001), i.e., 37.5 min faster; pharmacist presence not associated with less protocol-discordant use (p=0.0783). [verified: abstract]
- Corio JL, Sin JH, Hayes BD, Goldstein JN, Fuh L. Impact of a pharmacist-driven prothrombin complex concentrate protocol on time to administration in patients with warfarin-associated intracranial hemorrhage. West J Emerg Med. 2018;19(5):849-854. doi:10.5811/westjem.2018.6.37932. PMID 30202498; PMC6123083. Pharmacist approves/doses/prepares/delivers 4F-PCC (previously blood bank). n=48 (24 vs 24); median time to 4F-PCC 35 min (IQR 25-62) vs 70 min (IQR 34-89) pre-protocol (p=0.034); no difference in dosing appropriateness. [verified: abstract]
- Imanaka S, et al. Emergency ICU pharmacist intervention may reduce time to 4F-PCC. J Pharm Health Care Sci. 2020;6:7. doi:10.1186/s40780-020-00161-z. PMID 32266076. Japan, n=10; order-to-administration 21 vs 60 min (p=0.02); presentation-to-administration 103 vs 111 min (NS). [verified: abstract] (small, non-US)
- Santiago R, Slocum G, Riggi G. Resuscitating massive transfusion knowledge: an update for the pharmacist. Am J Health Syst Pharm. 2025;82(24):1314-1327. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxaf164. PMID 40580496. Review: MT most commonly defined as >=10 u RBC in 24 h; pharmacist role in calcium supplementation and TXA; EM/critical care pharmacists are active participants at bedside resuscitation. [verified: abstract]
- Open-fracture antibiotic timing (protocol, not pharmacist-specific): Morgan D, et al. Eur J Orthop Surg Traumatol. 2024;35(1):23. doi:10.1007/s00590-024-04141-z. PMID 39579212. Level I adult; standardized multidisciplinary protocol cut median arrival-to-antibiotic 69 to 39 min (p<0.001); within 1 h 42.9% to 79.0%; penetrating injuries delayed (80 vs 54 min). [verified: abstract]
- Fimmel FA, et al. Improving time to antibiotic administration in open fractures. Pediatr Qual Saf. 2026;11(4):e890. doi:10.1097/pq9.0000000000000890. PMID 42524689; PMC13412662. Level 1 pediatric trauma center QI (Sept 2020-Mar 2025, n=111); order set with standardized antibiotics; time to antibiotics 113 to 36 min. [verified: abstract]
- Muniz AD, et al. Time savings and safety of EMS administration of antibiotics for open fractures. Prehosp Emerg Care. 2024;28(8):1046-1052. doi:10.1080/10903127.2024.2347291. PMID 38661320. ESO 2019-2022, n=523; dispatch-to-antibiotic median 31 min; median 15 min saved; no epinephrine for anaphylaxis. [verified: abstract]
