All URLs were accessed 2026-11-20 unless stated. Out of scope (other researchers cover them): TXA, blood and reversal dosing; TBI; SCI; analgesia, RSI and VTE; infection prophylaxis. Those topics appear here only where they bear on pharmacist impact, errors, or supply.

## Q1. Pharmacist in trauma: standards (ACS 2022, ASHP 2021) and measured impact

### Takeaway
The ACS 2022 trauma standards (revised July 2025) never mention pharmacists. Nothing in them requires a pharmacist to respond to trauma activations at Level I or II.

Pharmacy enters only through three protocol requirements:
- a massive transfusion protocol (MTP), Type I, all levels;
- a rapid anticoagulation reversal protocol, Type II, all levels;
- geriatric medication reconciliation, Type II, Level I/II.

The evidence for pharmacist presence is observational but consistent. With a pharmacist present:
- 4F-PCC reached the patient 35 to 140 min faster;
- open-fracture antibiotics were guideline-concordant 81% vs 47% of the time;
- post-intubation sedation started about 29 min faster;
- the first analgesic dose came 4 min faster.

### Cited Findings

**Standards**
- ACS COT, *Resources for Optimal Care of the Injured Patient (2022 Standards)*. Released March 2022; revised Dec 2022, Dec 2023 and July 2025. A full-text search of the July 2025 revision for "pharm" returned 0 hits, so there is no pharmacist standard. [verified: pdftotext search] — [ACS 2022 Standards rev. July 2025 (state-hosted PDF)](https://lern.la.gov/wp-content/uploads/resources-for-optimal-care-of-the-injured-patient-2022-standards-revised-july-2025.pdf)
- **Std 5.8, Massive Transfusion Protocol.** TYPE I; levels LI, LII, LIII, PTCI, PTCII. [verified] — same source
  - Text: "All trauma centers must have a massive transfusion protocol (MTP) that is developed collaboratively between the trauma service and the blood bank."
  - Additional information: the MTP includes a trigger for activation, a process for cessation, and strategies to preserve unused blood. Clotting studies should be "immediately available."
- **Std 5.9, Anticoagulation Reversal Protocol.** TYPE II, all levels. "All trauma centers must have a rapid reversal protocol in place for patients on anticoagulants... The protocol should include therapeutic options and indications for the use of each reversal agent." [verified] — same source
- **Std 5.6, Care Protocols for the Injured Older Adult.** TYPE II, LI and LII. Required protocols include "Medication reconciliation and avoidance of inappropriate medications." Level I and II centers also need a geriatric provider liaison. [verified] — same source
- **Std 5.10, Pediatric Readiness.** TYPE II, all levels. Each ED must complete a pediatric readiness assessment (pedsready.org) during the verification cycle and have a plan to close the gaps found. [verified] — same source
- **Verification consequences.** Noncompliance with any Type I standard means the center is not verified. Up to 3 Type II deficiencies still allow verification, with a 1-year certificate. [verified] — same source
- **Edition facts.** The 2022 edition has 110 standards (387 in 2014). Verification under the 2022 standards began in late 2023 (New York from Sept 1, 2023). - **ASHP guideline.** Ortmann MJ, Johnson EG, Jarrell DH, Bilhimer M, Hayes BD, Mishler A, Pugliese RS, Roberson TA, Slocum G, Smith AP, Yabut K, Zimmerman DE. Its list of geriatric ED interdisciplinary best practices includes "A pharmacist for medication review." **Prevalence and activities**
- **Porter AL, Zaeem M, Hewes A, Hale S, Jones C, Gestring ML, et al.** *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2019;76(16):1226-1230. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxz124. PMID 31369114. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajhp/zxz124)
  - Survey of ACS trauma centers, March 2017; 33% response (110/335).
  - Pharmacist involvement rose from 23% (2007) to 70% (2017), p<0.001. At 71% of those centers the pharmacist was ED-based.
  - Bedside roles: dose calculation 96%, preparing medications 89%, drug information 79%.
  - Administrative roles at 45% of centers: education, medication safety, QI, ACS accreditation preparation.
  - Main reason for having no pharmacist: unfamiliarity with the role.
- **Patanwala AE, Hays DP.** *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2010;67(18):1536-8. doi:10.2146/ajhp090311. PMID 20811031. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.2146/ajhp090311)
  - Level I center with an ED pharmacist 40 h/week; 304 trauma interventions in 2 months.
  - Dosing recommendations 60%, drug information 27%.
  - Top drug classes: analgesics, sedatives, antimicrobials, vaccines, fluids.
  - The pharmacist facilitated drug administration in 83% of interventions.
- **Nguyen MH, Gordon K, Reachi B, Bair J, Chauv S, Fontaine GV, et al.** *Am J Emerg Med*. 2024;82:63-67. doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2024.05.011. PMID 38805940. Overnight EM pharmacist (2300-0700) at a Level I trauma/comprehensive stroke center: 820 interventions over 107 shifts; cost avoidance $612,974; 46% were hands-on care. [verified: abstract]
- **Patel H, Wee M, Tejani AM, Lau A.** *Pharmacy (Basel)*. 2025;13(4):89. doi:10.3390/pharmacy13040089. PMID 40700260. Scoping review of 26 records on pharmacists in cardiac and trauma resuscitation (search Sept 2024). Too heterogeneous for meta-analysis. Outcomes were mostly ACLS compliance and medication errors; mortality and LOS were rarely reported. [verified: abstract]

**Time to 4F-PCC, with vs without a pharmacist**
- **Masic D, Hidalgo DC, Kuhrau S, Chaney W, Rech MA.** *J Emerg Med*. 2019;57(5):620-628. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2019.06.027. PMID 31447188. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2019.06.027)
  - Retrospective, 2014-2018, n=116 (68% warfarin, 67% ICH).
  - Median time to 4F-PCC: 66.5 min with a bedside PharmD vs 206.5 min without (p<0.001).
  - PharmD presence was the only independent predictor (beta -163.5 min; 95% CI -249.4 to -77.7).
  - No difference in hemostasis or mortality.
  - Shorter ICU LOS (2 vs 5 d) and hospital LOS (5.5 vs 8 d).
- **Mercer K, Ofoegbuna I, Razzack H, Feinstein J, Gonzalez M, et al.** *Am J Emerg Med*. 2025;95:147-152. doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2025.05.032. PMID 40450951. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2025.05.032)
  - n=147, 2016-2022.
  - Without a pharmacist: fixed-dose 4F-PCC 58.5 min vs weight-based 71 min.
  - Fixed-dose with a pharmacist: 21 min vs 58.5 min without (p<0.0001).
  - Pharmacist presence did not reduce protocol-discordant use (p=0.078).
- **Corio JL, Sin JH, Hayes BD, Goldstein JN, Fuh L.** *West J Emerg Med*. 2018;19(5):849-854. doi:10.5811/westjem.2018.6.37932. PMID 30202498. [verified: abstract]
  - Pharmacist-driven protocol for warfarin ICH with INR ≥2: the pharmacist approves, doses, prepares and delivers 4F-PCC (previously done by the blood bank).
  - Median time 35 min (IQR 25-62) vs 70 min (IQR 34-89), p=0.034; n=24 per group.
- **Imanaka S, et al.** *J Pharm Health Care Sci*. 2020;6:7. doi:10.1186/s40780-020-00161-z. PMID 32266076. Japan, n=10. Order-to-administration 21 vs 60 min (p=0.02). Door-to-drug time did not differ. [verified: abstract]

**Time to antibiotics for open fractures**
- **Harvey S, Hall AB, Wilson K.** *Am J Emerg Med*. 2018;36(2):290-293. doi:10.1016/j.ajem.2017.10.039. PMID 29079370. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2017.10.039)
  - Trauma alerts with open fractures, May 2014 to June 2016.
  - Guideline-concordant initial antibiotic: 81% with an EM pharmacist vs 47% without (p<0.01).
  - Median door-to-antibiotic: 14 vs 20 min (p=0.02).
- Protocol studies without a pharmacist comparison:
  - Morgan D, et al. *Eur J Orthop Surg Traumatol*. 2024;35(1):23. doi:10.1007/s00590-024-04141-z. PMID 39579212. Median time to antibiotics fell from 69 to 39 min; share within 1 h rose from 42.9% to 79.0%; penetrating injuries were slower (80 vs 54 min). [verified: abstract]
  - Fimmel FA, et al. *Pediatr Qual Saf*. 2026;11(4):e890. doi:10.1097/pq9.0000000000000890. PMID 42524689. Pediatric Level 1 order-set QI: 113 to 36 min. [verified: abstract]

**Post-intubation sedation and trauma-bay analgesia**
- **Johnson EG, Meier A, Shirakbari A, Weant K, Baker Justice S.** *J Emerg Med*. 2015;49(1):43-9. doi:10.1016/j.jemermed.2014.12.028. PMID 25797938. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jemermed.2014.12.028)
  - n=106 ED RSI.
  - Time to sedation or analgesia: rocuronium 34±36 min vs succinylcholine 16±21 min (p=0.002).
  - With an EM pharmacist: 20±21 min vs 49±45 min without (p<0.001).
- **Hwang C, Michaels B, Park K, Dang A, Vo C, Lee S, Coralic Z, et al.** *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2025;82(Suppl 3):S2929-S2936. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxaf037. PMID 40037283. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajhp/zxaf037)
  - 3 EDs, n=2,059.
  - Rocuronium was associated with lower rates of sedation (aHR 0.75) and analgesia (aHR 0.73).
  - A bedside ED pharmacist was associated with more sedation (aHR 1.14, 95% CI 1.03-1.27).
  - Median initial propofol rate was 20 mcg/kg/min, which raises the risk of awareness during paralysis.
- **Montgomery K, Hall AB, Keriazes G.** *J Trauma Nurs*. 2015;22(2):87-90. doi:10.1097/JTN.0000000000000112. PMID 25768964. Level II center, n=340. Mean time to first IV opioid 17 vs 21 min with vs without a pharmacist (P=.03). Pain-score reduction was similar. [verified: abstract]
- **Ender V, et al.** *Air Med J*. 2025;44(5):416-419. doi:10.1016/j.amj.2025.05.003. PMID 40849159. 10-50% of intubated ED patients receive no sedation. "EDs have used pharmacists with success to improve post-intubation analgesia and sedation practices." [verified: abstract]
- **Reviews and guidelines.** [verified: abstracts]
  - Hampton JP, Hommer K, Musselman M, Bilhimer M. RSI and the role of the EM pharmacist: 2022 update. *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2023;80(4):182-195. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxac326. PMID 36306474.
  - Acquisto NM, et al. SCCM RSI guideline. *Crit Care Med*. 2023;51(10):1411-1430. doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000006000. PMID 37707379. The guideline panel included pharmacists.

**Massive transfusion and calcium**
- **Santiago R, Slocum G, Riggi G.** *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2025;82(24):1314-1327. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxaf164. PMID 40580496. Massive transfusion review for pharmacists. MT is usually defined as ≥10 units RBC in 24 h. Calcium and TXA are the pharmacist-relevant adjuncts. [verified: abstract]
- **Korsch, Knapp.** *J Trauma Nurs*. 2026;33(1):60-66. doi:10.1097/JTN.0000000000000897. PMID 41505189. MTP response QI at rural Level III centers; pharmacists were 14% of respondents. Calcium was given appropriately in 87.5% of activations. [verified: abstract]

### Inferences
- ACS verification does not require pharmacist trauma response. Pharmacists can still own or co-author three auditable documents: the adjuncts in the Std 5.8 MTP (calcium, TXA), the Std 5.9 reversal protocol (agents, indications, doses, preparation workflow), and Std 5.6 geriatric medication reconciliation.
- The time savings come mainly between the order and administration (preparation, bedside delivery, dose calculation), not from earlier ordering.
- Pharmacist presence and a simpler protocol help independently. Fixed-dose PCC with a pharmacist was fastest (21 min).

### Gaps
- No primary study was found on pharmacist presence and time to TXA in trauma, or on RSI dosing errors during trauma activations. The RSI evidence (Johnson 2015, Hwang 2025) comes from all-comer ED RSI.
- The text of the ASHP 2021 guideline could not be retrieved, so its trauma-specific wording is [recalled].
- Pharmacist language in the ACS 2014 standards was not checked, so this note makes no claim about a change between editions.

## Q2. Medication errors and safety in trauma resuscitation

### Takeaway
The highest-consequence errors found:
- TXA given neuraxially in place of bupivacaine. FDA now requires a boxed warning and recommends premixed bags.
- Duplicate TXA when the EMS dose is not handed off.
- Wrong pediatric weight estimates.
- Concentrated or vesicant agents: 23.4% NaCl, calcium chloride, and mannitol crystals.

Recent cohort data show less tissue injury than feared from peripheral 23.4% NaCl and peripheral 10% CaCl2, but a written policy is still needed.

### Cited Findings

**TXA wrong-route and look-alike errors**
- **Early alerts.** ISMP National Alert Network warning, Sept 9, 2020. FDA alert, Dec 3, 2020: TXA was given intrathecally in place of spinal anesthetics (outcomes included seizures, paraplegia and death). Container and carton labels were to be revised to highlight the IV route. [secondary] — [FDA 2020 alert](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-alerts-healthcare-professionals-about-risk-medication-errors-tranexamic-acid-injection-resulting)
- **FDA update** (page shows "content current as of 10/21/2025"). [verified: FDA page] — [FDA update](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-provides-update-health-care-professionals-about-risk-inadvertent-intrathecal-spinal)
  - Requires a **Boxed Warning** on medication errors involving inadvertent neuraxial administration.
  - Adds a neuraxial **contraindication**.
  - Revises Dosage and Administration to state IV-only use.
  - Reported outcomes included "prolonged hospitalization and death."
  - Storage: keep vials and ampules apart from local anesthetics and spinal/epidural kits (separate carts outside the OR, or locked barcode-scanned bins).
  - Labeling: a "contains tranexamic acid" auxiliary label on all vials and ampules.
  - "Use commercially available or pharmacy-prepared intravenous infusion bags of tranexamic acid, when possible." The premix is 1,000 mg/100 mL in NaCl.
- **ISMP/APSF joint announcement, Nov 26, 2025:** calls for forcing functions (for example NRFit neuraxial connectors) and for removing TXA vials and ampules from perioperative areas. [secondary: search digest; the page returned 403] — [APSF](https://www.apsf.org/news-updates/preventing-wrong-drug-wrong-route-errors-involving-tranexamic-acid-and-local-anesthetics/)
- **Case reports** [verified: abstracts]
  - Samuel RO, et al. *Case Rep Anesthesiol*. 2024:4731010. doi:10.1155/2024/4731010. PMID 39445087. TXA prepared as "hyperbaric bupivacaine 0.5%" was injected intrathecally. Seizures followed; the patient died at 21 h.
  - Hatch DM, et al. *Int J Obstet Anesth*. 2016;26:71-5. doi:10.1016/j.ijoa.2015.11.006. PMID 26775897. A used TXA ampoule was found on the spinal tray. Refractory status epilepticus and VT; magnesium was used.
  - Liu H, et al. *Acta Anaesthesiol Scand*. 2017;61(1):11-22. doi:10.1111/aas.12821. PMID 27766633. Review: management relies on CSF lavage plus cardiopulmonary support.

**Duplicate prehospital plus ED TXA**
- **Barrett WJ, Kaucher KA, Orpet RE, Campion EM, Goodloe JM, Fischer PE, Colwell CB, Lyng JW.** NAEMSP/ACEP/ACS-COT joint position statement on TXA in trauma. *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2025;99(3):357-363. doi:10.1097/TA.0000000000004727. PMID 40842057. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000004727)
  - EMS may give either 1 g IV/IO (followed by a 1 g hospital infusion over 8 h) or 2 g IV/IO.
  - Give only to patients with signs of hemorrhagic shock, and no later than 3 h after injury.
  - Pediatric prehospital dosing has not been established.
  - "Prehospital TXA administration, if used, should be clearly communicated to receiving health care professionals to promote appropriate monitoring and to avoid duplicate administration(s)."
- **STAAMP trial.** Guyette FX, et al. *JAMA Surg*. 2020;156(1):11-20. doi:10.1001/jamasurg.2020.4350. PMID 33016996. After a 1 g prehospital dose, patients were randomized to abbreviated, standard, or repeat-bolus in-hospital regimens. The right ED action therefore depends on exactly what EMS gave. [verified: abstract]

**Pediatric weight estimation**
- **Wells M, et al.** *Resuscitation*. 2017;121:9-33. doi:10.1016/j.resuscitation.2017.09.026. PMID 28958796. Meta-analysis: "just over 50%" of Broselow estimates fall within 10% of actual weight. The tape was frequently used incorrectly and does not carry enough dosing information on its own. [verified: abstract]
- **Cosmos Yakubu R, et al.** *Pediatr Emerg Care*. 2022;38(9):e1517-e1522. doi:10.1097/PEC.0000000000002638. PMID 36040471. Ghana; estimates within 10% of actual weight: Broselow 47.5%, Mercy 73.1%, PAWPER XL-MAC 77.6%, PAWPER XL 81.7%. [verified: abstract]
- **Oommen JZ, et al.** *Cureus*. 2021;13(6):e15807. PMID 34306874. Peru: Broselow underestimated weight (bias 1.60 kg; 62.8% within 10%), worse at higher BMI. [verified: abstract]

**Calcium chloride (vesicant)**
- **Patel S, Dillon R.** *J Emerg Nurs*. 2022;48(4):484-491. doi:10.1016/j.jen.2021.12.005. PMID 35787779. 72 peripheral doses of 10% CaCl2 in an adult ED. Infusion-related adverse events 6%, all grade 0-1; no permanent tissue injury. [verified: abstract]
- **Salt equivalence.** 1 g CaCl2 is 13.6 mEq (about 273 mg elemental Ca); 1 g calcium gluconate is 4.65 mEq (93 mg), roughly 3:1. Calcium gluconate is preferred peripherally. Mixing up "1 g calcium" between the two salts is a known error. [recalled]

**23.4% sodium chloride (8,008 mOsm/L)**
- **Erstad BL.** *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2023;80(15):1032-1035. doi:10.1093/ajhp/zxad103. PMID 37166355. Title: "Peripheral intravenous administration of 23.4% sodium chloride solution: a plea for caution." [verified: citation/title]
- **Silva CR, Nagy J, Zullo AR, Greene RA, et al.** *Neurocrit Care*. 2025;43(3):978-985. doi:10.1007/s12028-025-02342-w. PMID 40841787. 863 peripheral 30 mL boluses. Extravasation 6.4%; no necrosis, no surgery, 1 hyaluronidase treatment. Diabetes raised the risk (RR 2.39). [verified: abstract]
- **Faiver L, et al.** *Neurocrit Care*. 2021;35(3):845-852. doi:10.1007/s12028-021-01248-7. PMID 34173156. 57 peripheral vs 242 central doses: no soft-tissue injury and similar ICP reduction. By convention the drug is otherwise limited to central or IO access. [verified: abstract]
- **Iskaros O, et al.** *Neurohospitalist*. 2024;14(2):122-128. doi:10.1177/19418744231216851. PMID 38666283. Slow IV push over 2-5 min vs a 30 min infusion. Order-to-completion 25 vs 73 min. Infusion-related adverse events 31% vs 23% (not significant), mostly hypotension. [verified: abstract]
- ISMP's high-alert list includes hypertonic sodium chloride above 0.9% as a concentrated electrolyte. [recalled]

**Kcentra preparation** (prescribing information, DailyMed setid eee1afb8-324c-42e4-8bf0-f0c9da5e6d42) [verified: label digest] — [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/search.cfm?labeltype=all&query=kcentra)
- Reconstitute with 20 mL (500 U kit) or 40 mL (1000 U kit) of the supplied sterile water, using Mix2Vial. "Gently swirl... Do not shake."
- Start "promptly or within 4 hours."
- Infuse at 0.12 mL/kg/min (about 3 units/kg/min), maximum 8.4 mL/min (about 210 units/min), through a separate infusion line.
- Store at 2-25 °C. Boxed warning for thromboembolism.

**Mannitol crystals and filter** (Osmitrol prescribing information, Baxter, setid 0d914965-7001-45cb-ba51-d7c5964b05bc) [verified: label digest] — [DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/search.cfm?labeltype=all&query=osmitrol)
- "OSMITROL solutions may crystallize when exposed to low temperature... At higher concentrations, the solutions have a greater tendency to crystallize."
- "If crystals are visible, re-dissolve by warming the solution up to 70°C, with agitation." Do not warm in a water bath or microwave.
- "Use administration sets with a final in-line filter because of the potential for OSMITROL crystals to form."
- No filter pore size is stated. Filter wording in other manufacturers' 25% vial labels was not checked.

**Cefazolin dose in obesity**
- 2 g standard; 3 g if weight ≥120 kg. Bratzler DW, Dellinger EP, Olsen KM, et al. Clinical practice guidelines for antimicrobial prophylaxis in surgery. *Am J Health Syst Pharm*. 2013;70(3):195-283. doi:10.2146/ajhp120568. [recalled: dose threshold and citation not re-verified this session]

### Inferences
A trauma-bay safety bundle a pharmacist can own:
- TXA stocked only as premixed bags in the trauma bay and OR. Remove the vials or label them. Make "TXA given prehospital? dose/time" a required documentation field.
- One calcium product per indication, with the salt and mEq always written out.
- 23.4% NaCl kept in pharmacy-controlled kits. Prefer central or IO access, and have a written peripheral-use policy (gauge, site, monitoring) for herniation emergencies.
- Mannitol stored warm, with filter sets attached.
- A PCC kit with the diluent premeasured and a fixed-dose option.
- Length-based or habitus-adjusted weight tools (PAWPER or Mercy outperform Broselow), plus a pharmacist double-check.

### Gaps
- No US study was found that measures how often duplicate prehospital plus ED TXA occurs.
- No study was found that times 4F-PCC reconstitution itself (bench minutes).
- The ISMP alert text was not read directly. The cefazolin 3 g threshold and the ISMP high-alert listing are [recalled].

## Q3. Supply and regulatory status, 2024 to November 2026

### Takeaway
- The post-Hurricane Helene IV saline shortage was declared resolved in August 2025.
- As of 2026-11-20 the FDA shortage list shows:
  - fluids and electrolytes: dextrose, sterile water, amino acids, sodium bicarbonate;
  - most RSI, analgesic and anesthetic injectables: rocuronium, etomidate, fentanyl, midazolam, morphine, hydromorphone, lidocaine, bupivacaine;
  - heparin.
- TXA, calcium chloride, 23.4% NaCl, mannitol, PCC, levetiracetam, ketamine and cefazolin are not listed as shortages.
- Andexanet left the US market on Dec 22, 2025.
- The first FDA-licensed freeze-dried plasma (EZPLAZ) was approved on July 29, 2026.

### Cited Findings

**IV fluids after Hurricane Helene**
- Helene damaged Baxter's North Cove plant (Marion, NC) in late September 2024.
- Production restarted in November 2024, reached pre-hurricane levels by February 2025, and inventory was "fully restored" by May 2025.
- FDA declared the 0.9% sodium chloride injection shortage resolved on August 8, 2025 (reported August 8-13) and said other IV fluids remained in shortage.

**Andexanet alfa (Andexxa) withdrawal**
- FDA safety communication, December 18, 2025: risks outweigh benefits, based on postmarketing serious and fatal thromboembolic events.
- AstraZeneca ended US sales on December 22, 2025 and asked to withdraw the BLA voluntarily. It cited commercial reasons and no agreement with FDA on a path to traditional approval.
- ANNEXA-I: thrombosis 14.6% with andexanet vs 6.9% with usual care.
- The product remains conditionally approved by EMA and MHRA.

## Q4. Older adults on antithrombotics

### Takeaway
Older adults are under-triaged because their vital signs mislead: beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers blunt tachycardia, and their baseline blood pressure is often high. ACS TQIP (November 2023) therefore lists geriatric activation criteria:
- SBP <110;
- HR >90;
- shock index >1;
- any ground-level fall in a patient on an antithrombotic.

In mild TBI, DOACs carry less risk than warfarin of immediate ICH and of neurosurgery. The risk of delayed ICH after a negative CT is similar for both, about 1-2.5%, and rarely needs surgery.

### Cited Findings
- **ACS TQIP, *Best Practices Guidelines: Geriatric Trauma Management*, November 2023.** This is the latest edition; no 2024 edition was found. [verified: full PDF] — [PDF](https://www.facs.org/media/ubyj2ubl/best-practices-guidelines-geriatric-trauma.pdf)
  - **Triage:** "Injury mechanisms and vital signs do not reliably triage older adult patients." Standard adult criteria "can lead to undertriage." The National Trauma Triage Protocol suggests an SBP threshold of 110 for older adults (Brown JB et al. *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2015;78(2):352-359, "110 is the new 90"). The guideline also recommends lowering the HR criterion from 120 to 90. "An SI greater than 1 reliably predicts mortality."
  - **Box 1, Geriatric Trauma Activation Criteria:**
    - ground-level fall (GLF) on antithrombotic agents;
    - SBP <110;
    - HR >90;
    - SI >1;
    - GLF not on anticoagulants with GCS <14 and signs of head trauma.
  - **Blunted tachycardia:** caused by "catecholamine insensitivity, atherosclerosis, myocyte fibrosis, conduction abnormalities, and common medications such as beta and calcium channel blockers." Patients whose usual SBP is 130-150 often have occult hypoperfusion despite a "normal" reading.
  - **Shock markers:** SI ≥0.7 has 83% specificity for transfusion need at age ≥65. Lactate ≥2.4 mmol/L or an abnormal base deficit suggests occult hypoperfusion; repeat at 30-45 min.
  - **Transfusion:** mortality is higher if transfusion is delayed beyond 4 h. Survival to discharge after massive transfusion was 60% at age >65. "Age alone is not a contraindication."
  - **Anticoagulation:**
    - Take an anticoagulant history from every older trauma patient, then a coagulation profile.
    - "Trauma protocols for rapid anticoagulation reversal are associated with improved outcomes."
    - Consider TEG for patients on DOACs or antiplatelets.
    - Reverse for any ICH. "Repeat the CT scan at 6 to 8 hours to check for stability of the ICH."
  - **Medication reconciliation:**
    - Use the 2023 AGS Beers Criteria.
    - Continue or taper drugs with withdrawal potential: SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, chronic benzodiazepines, chronic opioids, anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, MAOIs, clonidine, corticosteroids.
    - Avoid starting new benzodiazepines.
    - "Continue a beta blocker or start one, if indicated."
- **Pandit V, Rhee P, Hashmi A, et al.** *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2014;76(4):1111-5. doi:10.1097/TA.0000000000000160. PMID 24662879. [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000000160)

  - SI ≥1 (sensitivity 81%, specificity 79%; present in 3%) was the strongest predictor of mortality: OR 3.1 (95% CI 2.6-3.3).

  - NTDB 2007, blunt non-neurologic injury, n=189,574.
  - In patients older than 55, age × SI predicted 48-h mortality better (AUC 0.693) than HR (0.626), SBP (0.657) or SI (0.684).

- **Kim SY, Hong KJ, Shin SD, et al.** *J Korean Med Sci*. 2016;31(12):2026-2032. doi:10.3346/jkms.2016.31.12.2026. PMID 27822945. n=45,880 patients older than 65. Median age-SI 64.0 in non-survivors vs 41.5 in survivors. AUROC: age-SI 0.740, SI 0.674, MSI 0.682. [verified: abstract]

  - Meta-analysis of 12 studies, n=5,289 (92% ground-level falls).

  - Delayed ICH: DOAC 1.6% vs VKA 1.9% (overall 1.7%).

- **EAST geriatric guideline.** Calland JF, et al. Evaluation and management of geriatric trauma: an EAST practice management guideline. *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2012;73(5 Suppl 4):S345-50. Recalled recommendations: lower the activation threshold at age ≥65, check INR promptly on warfarin, and begin reversal within 2 h of ICH. [recalled: not confirmed]

### Inferences
Pharmacist ED tasks for injured older adults:
- Identify the antithrombotic agent, last dose, and renal function at the bedside.
- Own the reversal-protocol update now that andexanet is withdrawn.
- Flag beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers that can mask shock, and calculate SI and age × SI.
- Prompt activation for any ground-level fall on an antithrombotic.
- Start a Beers-based medication reconciliation, including benzodiazepine and opioid withdrawal risk and the beta-blocker hold-vs-continue decision.

Clinical pattern: DOACs lower the risk of immediate ICH compared with warfarin, but delayed bleeding after a negative CT is similar (about 1-2.5%) and rarely surgical. Concurrent antiplatelet therapy recurs among the delayed cases.

### Gaps
- The EAST 2012 geriatric guideline text was not retrieved, so its items are [recalled]. No 2024 TQIP geriatric update was found; the 2023 edition is current.
- The age × SI >50 cutoff was not verified from the Zarzaur full text.
- No high-quality data were found comparing mortality on DOAC vs warfarin across all injuries in geriatric falls (as opposed to TBI only).

## Q5. Pregnancy in trauma

### Takeaway
EAST published a new pregnancy practice management guideline in 2025 (Appelbaum, *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*). It recommends:
- resuscitative hysterotomy as soon as possible at ≥20 weeks in traumatic arrest;
- 4-6 h of fetal observation when the pregnancy is viable;
- no withholding of indicated imaging because of radiation concerns;
- Kleihauer-Betke (KB) testing in Rh-negative patients to dose anti-D;
- fetal assessment after the primary survey.

Pharmacy points:
- Anti-D within 72 h: 300 mcg covers 15 mL fetal RBC, about 30 mL whole blood.
- O-negative blood until crossmatched blood is available.
- Vasopressors only for hypotension that does not respond to fluids.
- Tetanus vaccine is safe.
- Left uterine displacement.

### Cited Findings
- **Appelbaum RD, Yorkgitis B, Rosen J, et al.** Trauma in pregnancy: EAST systematic review, meta-analysis and practice management guideline. *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2025;99(2):298-309. doi:10.1097/TA.0000000000004661. PMID 40454982 (epub May 30, 2025). [verified: abstract] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000004661)
  - Gestational age ≥20 weeks with resuscitative thoracotomy for traumatic arrest: conditional recommendation for resuscitative hysterotomy "as soon as possible."
  - Viable pregnancy: conditional recommendation for formal observation of "at least 4 to 6 hours."
  - "Possible effects of ionizing radiation exposure should not prevent medically indicated diagnostic imaging."
  - "Kleihauer-Betke testing should be performed in patients who are Rh negative to determine an appropriate dose of Rh D immunoglobulin."
  - Fetal assessment "at the end of the primary survey after a rapid maternal evaluation." "The best early treatment of the fetus is the optimal resuscitation of the mother."
- **Barraco RD, Chiu WC, Clancy TV, et al.** EAST practice management guideline for injury in the pregnant patient. *J Trauma*. 2010;69(1):211-4. doi:10.1097/TA.0b013e3181dbe1ea. PMID 20622592. [verified: abstract]
  - Trauma affects 7% of pregnancies; admission in 4/1,000.
  - Just over half occurs in the 3rd trimester. Mechanisms: MVC 50%, falls 22%, assaults 22%.
  - Trauma is the leading nonobstetric cause of maternal death; maternal mortality 6-7%.
  - Fetal mortality up to 61% in major trauma and 80% with maternal shock.
  - Recalled 2010 recommendations, now superseded: KB in all pregnant trauma patients >12 weeks; cardiotocography ≥6 h after 20 weeks; perimortem cesarean considered from 24-26 weeks. [recalled]
- **Jain V, Chari R, Maslovitz S, Farine D, et al.** SOGC guidelines for the management of a pregnant trauma patient. *J Obstet Gynaecol Can*. 2015;37(6):553-74. doi:10.1016/s1701-2163(15)30232-2. PMID 26334607. [verified: recommendations listed in abstract]

  - Rec 8: transfuse O-negative blood until crossmatched blood is available (I-A).
  - Rec 15: maternal CT "should not be deferred or delayed due to concerns regarding fetal exposure to radiation."
  - Rec 17: coagulation panel including fibrinogen. Rec 21: admit for 24 h if fibrinogen <200 mg/dL or other risk factors.

  - Rec 28: "Tetanus vaccination is safe in pregnancy and should be given when indicated."

  - Left lateral tilt for hypotension. Do not hesitate to obtain CT.
  - Cardiotocography for ≥4 h after even minor abdominal trauma if the fetus is viable (about 24 weeks).
  - Perimortem cesarean may benefit both mother and fetus.

  - Dose: 20 mcg per mL of RBC exposure, rounded up to the next whole syringe.
  - The label is internally inconsistent: Section 2.1 mentions 2.5 mL.

  - Recalled content: continuous manual left uterine displacement when the fundus is at or above the umbilicus; perimortem cesarean if there is no ROSC by 4 min, aiming for delivery within 5 min; standard ACLS drugs and doses are unchanged. [recalled]
- **TXA in pregnant trauma patients.**
  - No trauma-specific RCT was found this session.

### Inferences
Pharmacy checklist for an injured pregnant patient:
- Confirm pregnancy and gestational age. The viability threshold is ≥20 weeks in EAST and ≥23 weeks in SOGC.
- Send a type and screen plus KB.
- Give RhIG 300 mcg within 72 h to Rh-negative patients. Add doses if KB shows more than 15 mL fetal RBC (30 mL whole blood).
- Use O-negative uncrossmatched blood.
- Choose drugs for the mother. Do not withhold TXA, analgesia, RSI agents, tetanus vaccine or CT contrast.
- Use vasopressors only after volume, and keep the uterus displaced to the left throughout.
- Have a perimortem cesarean or hysterotomy kit ready at ≥20 weeks.

- The ACOG statement on trauma in pregnancy was not retrieved; ACOG's site was not checked.
- No guidance was found naming a preferred vasopressor for pregnant trauma patients (phenylephrine vs norepinephrine). SOGC says only "intractable hypotension."
- TXA use in pregnant trauma patients remains extrapolated from other populations.

## Q6. Pediatric trauma pharmacy points

### Takeaway
The 2023 Pediatric Traumatic Hemorrhagic Shock Consensus Conference (PTHSCC):
- prefers blood products over crystalloid;
- supports MTP-driven balanced or whole-blood resuscitation;
- says TXA within 3 h "might be considered," preferred over aminocaproic acid, but gives no TXA dose;
- suggests against permissive hypotension.

Weight estimation is a leading source of error. MATIC-2 (whole blood vs components; TXA vs placebo) is recruiting, with primary completion expected June 2028. The PECARN TIC-TOC TXA dose-finding trial was planned to start in October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Russell RT, Esparaz JR, Beckwith MA, et al.** Pediatric Traumatic Hemorrhagic Shock Consensus Conference recommendations. *J Trauma Acute Care Surg*. 2023;94(1S Suppl 1):S2-S10. doi:10.1097/TA.0000000000003805. PMID 36245074; PMC9805499. 21 statements. [verified: PMC full text] — [doi](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0000000000003805)
  - **Fluids and blood:**
    - Prioritize blood products over crystalloid. In 712 children at 24 centers, each crystalloid bolus after the first was associated with more ventilator and ICU days.
    - Low-titer O whole blood (LTOWB, titer ≤200) "might be considered."
    - With components, target plasma:RBC 1:1 and platelet:RBC 1:1.
  - **TXA:**
    - "The empiric use of tranexamic acid within 3 hours of injury might be considered."
    - TXA is preferred over aminocaproic acid, which is associated with AKI.
    - No pediatric trauma RCTs of TXA were found. **The statement gives no TXA dose.**
  - **Other hemostatic agents:** evidence is insufficient for empiric PCC, empiric fibrinogen or cryoprecipitate, or VEM-guided TXA. Replace fibrinogen when it is low; most centers correct below 150 mg/dL.
  - **Blood pressure:** "We suggest against a permissive hypotension strategy and suggest resuscitation goals that optimize end organ perfusion and adequate oxygen delivery." Per ATLS, a child keeps a low-normal BP after 30% blood-volume loss and becomes hypotensive only after 45%.
  - **MTP:** start with an empiric MTP and balanced products, then move to goal-directed therapy, using VEM when available. The main text gives no mL/kg MTP trigger.
  - **Mortality:** 30-day mortality in pediatric traumatic hemorrhagic shock is 36-50%, vs 20-25% in adults.
- **MATIC-2** (NCT06070350; record accessed 2026-11-20). [verified: registry] — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06070350)
  - Status: recruiting.
  - Design: phase 3 Bayesian adaptive platform, 2×2 (LTOWB vs component therapy; TXA vs placebo); n=1,000.
  - Inclusion: age <18 with MTP activation plus at least 2 of: hypotension below the 5th percentile, tachycardia above the 95th percentile, or exam findings of severe bleeding.
  - The TXA domain excludes children who received TXA before enrollment or are more than 3 h from injury.
  - Primary outcome: 24-h mortality.
  - Dates: start 2024-11-01; primary completion 2028-06-01; completion 2028-10-01.
  - About 23 US sites (2 terminated, 1 withdrawn). BARDA is a collaborator. Includes a TXA PK/PD objective.
- **TIC-TOC efficacy trial** (PECARN; NCT04387305). [verified: registry] — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04387305)
  - Status: not yet recruiting. Planned start 2026-10-01; primary completion 2031-03-31; n=2,000.
  - Arms: TXA **15 mg/kg over 30 min, then 2 mg/kg/h × 8 h**; TXA 30 mg/kg, then 4 mg/kg/h; or placebo. A 45 mg/kg + 6 mg/kg/h arm may be added.
- **Common pediatric TXA regimen:** 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) over 10 min, then 2 mg/kg/h for 8 h (max 1 g). The UK RCPCH regimen is similar. [recalled] The 15 mg/kg + 2 mg/kg/h regimen is TIC-TOC's lower arm [verified].
- **"TXA 25 mg/kg (ACS 2026)":** no ACS document confirming this was found. [unverified; see Gaps]
- **Dysthe J, Leister J, Culp A, Louie JP.** *Pediatr Emerg Care*. 2026;42(3):233-241. doi:10.1097/PEC.0000000000003480. PMID 41749508. Review: TXA is used off-label in pediatrics; seizures and allergic reactions are rare; more trials are needed. The abstract gives no dose. [verified: abstract]
- **Weight estimation** [verified: abstracts; full citations in Q2]:
  - Wells 2017 meta-analysis: Broselow estimates fall within 10% only "just over 50%" of the time, and the tape is often misused.
  - Yakubu 2022: within 10%, Broselow 47.5% vs PAWPER XL 81.7%.
  - Oommen 2021: Broselow underestimates weight at higher BMI.
- **Calcium in pediatric massive transfusion:** 10% CaCl2 20 mg/kg (0.2 mL/kg, max 1 g), or 10% calcium gluconate 60 mg/kg (0.6 mL/kg, max 3 g). Central access is preferred for the chloride salt. [recalled: standard PALS doses, not verified this session]
- **Pediatric MTP trigger:** no verified source was found for a 20 mL/kg trigger. Massive transfusion in children is commonly defined as ≥40 mL/kg of blood products in 24 h, and activation is often prompted by ongoing bleeding after about 20 mL/kg of crystalloid or blood. [recalled; unverified]
- **PECARN head CT rule.** Kuppermann N, et al. *Lancet*. 2009;374(9696):1160-70. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(09)61558-0. PMID 19758692. In 42,412 children with GCS 14-15 within 24 h of head trauma, age-specific rules identify children at very low risk who can skip CT. NPV 100% under age 2 and 99.95% at age 2 and older; no neurosurgical case was missed. [verified: abstract]
- **ACS Std 5.10** requires a pediatric readiness assessment and gap plan at every verified center. [verified]

### Inferences
Pediatric trauma-bay pharmacy priorities:
- Use a measured or habitus-adjusted weight; prefer PAWPER or Mercy over Broselow. Read doses back in both mg and mL.
- Keep premade pediatric TXA dose charts while MATIC-2 and TIC-TOC are pending.
- Prefer calcium gluconate peripherally. Use calcium chloride by IO or central line in arrest.
- Do not apply adult permissive-hypotension targets to children.
- Document any EMS-given TXA. Children who received TXA earlier are excluded from MATIC-2's TXA arm.

### Gaps
- The "TXA 25 mg/kg (ACS 2026)" dose was not found in any ACS or TQIP document this session. The ACS TQIP pediatric or massive-transfusion best-practice documents may contain pediatric TXA dosing but were not retrieved. Verify before including.
- No authoritative source was found for a 20 mL/kg pediatric MTP activation trigger. The PTHSCC main text does not define one.
- Pediatric calcium doses and the TXA max-1-g regimen are [recalled]. They need a reference (for example PALS, Lexicomp, or an institutional MTP) before print.
- MATIC-2's TXA dose is not stated in the registry record.
