## Q1. European guideline, 6th edition (Rossaint 2023): verbatim recommendations and grades; any 7th edition?

### Takeaway
All recommendation texts below were extracted verbatim from the PMC/Europe PMC full text (PMC9977110) this session [verified]. Key handout values confirmed in primary: TXA 1 g over 10 min then 1 g over 8 h within 3 h, prehospital if feasible (1A); SBP 80-90 mmHg (MAP 50-60) without brain injury (1B) and MAP >= 80 mmHg with severe TBI GCS <= 8 (1C); noradrenaline if fluids fail (1C); fibrinogen trigger Clauss <= 1.5 g/L, initial 3-4 g = 15-20 single-donor cryo units (1C*/2C); platelets > 50 x 10^9/L bleeding, > 100 x 10^9/L TBI (2C); ionised Ca within normal range (normal range stated as 1.1-1.3 mmol/L in rationale), CaCl2 preferred (1C); PCC + vitamin K 5-10 mg IV for VKA (1A); PCC 25-50 U/kg for FXa inhibitors if andexanet unavailable or edoxaban (2C); idarucizumab 5 g (1C); NO recommendation on desmopressin; pharmacologic + IPC thromboprophylaxis within 24 h after bleeding controlled (1B). No 7th edition was found as of Nov 2026 [see gaps].

Verbatim recommendations [all verified in PMC9977110 full text]:
- Rec 5: "We recommend that the shock index (SI) and/or pulse pressure (PP) be used to assess the degree of hypovolaemic shock and transfusion requirements (Grade 1C)." Rationale: "A SI >= 0.9-1.0 was retrospectively associated with increased MT (25%)..."; SI cut-off 0.81: sensitivity 85%, specificity 64%, NPV 98% for MT. 
- Rec 11: "We recommend the early and repeated monitoring of haemostasis, using either a traditional laboratory determination such as prothrombin time (PT)/international normalised ratio (INR), Clauss fibrinogen level and platelet count and/or point-of-care (POC) PT/INR and/or a viscoelastic method (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 12: "We recommend that the routine use of POC platelet function devices for platelet function monitoring in trauma patients on antiplatelet therapy or with suspected platelet dysfunction be avoided (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 13 (permissive hypotension): "In the initial phase following trauma, we recommend the use of a restricted volume replacement strategy with a target systolic blood pressure of 80-90 mmHg (mean arterial pressure 50-60 mmHg) until major bleeding has been stopped without clinical evidence of brain injury (Grade 1B)." / "In patients with severe TBI (GCS <= 8), we recommend that a mean arterial pressure >= 80 mmHg be maintained (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 14 (vasopressors): "If a restricted volume replacement strategy does not achieve the target blood pressure, we recommend the administration of noradrenaline in addition to fluids to maintain target arterial pressure (Grade 1C)." / "We recommend infusion of dobutamine in the presence of myocardial dysfunction (Grade 1C)." Rationale discusses Sims et al. RCT (n=100): "low-dose arginine vasopressin (bolus of 4 IU followed by 0.04 IU/min) decreases blood product requirements" and concludes "additional research is needed" (no vasopressin recommendation).
- Rec 15 (fluids): "We recommend that fluid therapy using a 0.9% sodium chloride or balanced crystalloid solution be initiated in the hypotensive bleeding trauma patient (Grade 1B)." / "We recommend that hypotonic solutions such as Ringer's lactate be avoided in patients with severe head trauma (Grade 1B)." / "We recommend that the use of colloids be restricted due to the adverse effects on haemostasis (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 16: "If erythrocyte transfusion is necessary, we recommend a target haemoglobin of 70-90 g/L (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 18: "We recommend early application of measures to reduce heat loss and warm the hypothermic patient to achieve and maintain normothermia (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 21: "...We suggest that REBOA be considered in patients with noncompressible life-threatening traumatic haemorrhage to bridge the gap between haemodynamic collapse and haemorrhage control (Grade 2C)." [predates UK-REBOA 2023]
- Rec 4 (prehospital blood): "No clear recommendation or suggestion in favour or against the use of pre-hospital blood products can be provided at this time."
- Rec 23 (TXA): "We recommend that tranexamic acid (TXA) be administered to the trauma patient who is bleeding or at risk of significant bleeding as soon as possible, if feasible en route to the hospital, and within 3 h after injury at a loading dose of 1 g infused over 10 min, followed by an i.v. infusion of 1 g over 8 h (Grade 1A)." / "We recommend that the administration of TXA not await results from a viscoelastic assessment (Grade 1B)."
  - Rationale cites CRASH-2 "reduction in mortality of 1.5% and a reduction in bleeding deaths by one-third"; CRASH-3 (n=12,737; treated within 3 h n=9202) head-injury death 18.5% vs 19.8% (RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.86-1.02); mild-moderate RR 0.78 (0.64-0.95); severe "RR 0.99, 95% CI 0.91-1.7" [NOTE: guideline typo; CRASH-3 primary paper reports 0.91-1.07 — recalled, verify before printing]; Rowell prehospital TBI trial n=966, GOS-E >4 at 6 months 65% combined TXA vs 62% placebo; STAAMP n=927, 30-day mortality 8.1% TXA vs 9.9% placebo (NS); STAAMP 3 in-hospital dosing routes 9.3% / 7.8% / 7.3% vs 10%; TXA within 1 h and SI <0.9 HR 0.35 (0.19-0.65) for 30-day mortality vs delayed TXA (post hoc).
- Rec 24: "We recommend that monitoring and measures to support coagulation be initiated immediately upon hospital admission (Grade 1B)."
- Rec 25 (initial ratios) [verified in Europe PMC XML; bullets drop out of the PMC JSON text]: "In the initial management of patients with expected massive haemorrhage, we recommend one of the two following strategies: Fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate and pRBC (Grade 1C); FFP or pathogen-inactivated FFP in a FFP/pRBC ratio of at least 1:2 as needed (Grade 1C). In addition, we suggest a high platelet/pRBC ratio (Grade 2B)." Rationale: "A recent literature review suggested that MT protocols in adult trauma patients should utilise ratios between 1:1:1 and 1:1:2."
- Rec 26: "We recommend that resuscitation measures be continued using a goal-directed strategy, guided by standard laboratory coagulation values and/or VEM (Grade 1B)."
- Rec 27: "If a FFP-based coagulation resuscitation strategy is used, we recommend that further use of FFP be guided by standard laboratory coagulation screening parameters (PT and/or APTT > 1.5 times normal and/or viscoelastic evidence of a coagulation factor deficiency) (Grade 1C)." / "We recommend that the use of FFP be avoided for the correction of hypofibrinogenemia if fibrinogen concentrate and/or cryoprecipitate are available (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 28 (PCC/FXIII): "If a CFC-based strategy is used, we recommend treatment with factor concentrates based on standard laboratory coagulation parameters and/or viscoelastic evidence of a functional coagulation factor deficiency (Grade 1C)." / "Provided that fibrinogen levels are normal, we suggest that prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) is administered to the bleeding patient based on evidence of delayed coagulation initiation using VEM (Grade 2C)." / "We suggest that monitoring of FXIII be included in coagulation support algorithms and that FXIII be supplemented in bleeding patients with a functional FXIII deficiency (Grade 2C)."
- Rec 29 (fibrinogen): "We recommend treatment with fibrinogen concentrate or cryoprecipitate if major bleeding is accompanied by hypofibrinogenemia (viscoelastic signs of a functional fibrinogen deficit or a plasma Clauss fibrinogen level <= 1.5 g/L) (Grade 1C)*" / "We suggest an initial fibrinogen supplementation of 3-4 g. This is equivalent to 15-20 single donor units of cryoprecipitate or 3-4 g fibrinogen concentrate. Repeat doses should be guided by VEM and laboratory assessment of fibrinogen levels (Grade 2C)." Footnote *: proposed grade was 2B; vote split (50% objected, 39% supported 2B, 11% abstained), "The group therefore decided to revert the grading to 1C".
- Rec 30 (platelets): "We suggest that platelets be administered to maintain a platelet count above 50 x 10^9/L in trauma patients with ongoing bleeding and above 100 x 10^9/L in patients with TBI (Grade 2C)." / "If administered, we suggest an initial dose of four to eight single platelet units or one aphaeresis pack (Grade 2B)." Rationale: "No randomised trials have investigated specific platelet transfusion thresholds in trauma patients."
- Rec 31 (calcium): "We recommend that ionised calcium levels be monitored and maintained within the normal range following major trauma and especially during massive transfusion (Grade 1C)." / "We recommend the administration of calcium chloride to correct hypocalcaemia (Grade 1C)." Rationale (verbatim excerpts): "The normal range of ionised calcium (Ca) is 1.1-1.3 mmol/L and is pH-dependent, with a 0.1 unit increase in pH decreasing ionised calcium concentration by approximately 0.05 mmol/L"; "Each unit of pRBC or FFP contains approximately 3 g of citrate"; "Transfusion-induced hypocalcaemia, with ionised Ca levels below 0.9 mmol/L or serum total corrected calcium levels of 7.5 mg/dL or lower, should be corrected promptly, as ionised Ca levels below 0.8 mmol/L are associated with cardiac dysrhythmias"; "no data demonstrate that the prevention or treatment of ionised hypocalcaemia reduces mortality in patients with critical bleeding requiring MT"; "The preferred agent to correct hypocalcaemia is calcium chloride, 10 mL as a 10% solution contains 270 mg of elemental calcium. In comparison, 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate contains only 90 mg of elemental calcium."
- Rec 32 (rFVIIa): "We do not recommend the use of recombinant activated coagulation factor VII (rFVIIa) as first-line treatment (Grade 1B)." / "We suggest that the off-label use of rFVIIa be considered only if major bleeding and traumatic coagulopathy persist despite all other attempts to control bleeding, systemic homeostasis and best-practice use of conventional haemostatic measures (Grade 2C)."
- Rec 33 (VKA): "In the bleeding trauma patient, we recommend the emergency reversal of vitamin K-dependent oral anticoagulants with the early use of both PCC and 5-10 mg i.v. phytomenadione (vitamin K) (Grade 1A)."
- Rec 34 (FXa inhibitors): "We suggest the measurement of plasma levels of oral direct antifactor Xa agents such as apixaban, edoxaban or rivaroxaban in patients treated or suspected of being treated with one of these agents (Grade 2C)." / "...If not possible or available, we suggest low molecular weight heparin (LMWH)-calibrated anti-Xa assays as a reliable alternative (Grade 2C)." / "If bleeding is life-threatening in the presence of an apixaban or rivaroxaban effect, especially in patients with TBI, we suggest reversal with andexanet alfa (Grade 2C)." / "If andexanet alfa is not available, or in patients receiving edoxaban, we suggest the administration of PCC (25-50 U/kg) (Grade 2C)." Rationale: "We suggest an initial dose of 25 U/kg, as this dose has been shown to provide effective haemostasis without increased rates of thromboembolic events." Andexanet dosing quoted: 400 mg over 15 min then 480 mg over 2 h (low) or 800 mg over 30 min then 960 mg over 2 h (high). [Andexanet portion now SUPERSEDED in the US — see Q7.]
- Rec 35 (dabigatran): "We suggest the measurement of dabigatran plasma levels using diluted thrombin time ... (Grade 2C)." / "If measurement is not possible or available, we suggest measurement of the standard thrombin time to allow a qualitative estimation of the presence of dabigatran (Grade 2C)." / "If bleeding is life-threatening in those receiving dabigatran, we recommend treatment with idarucizumab (i.v. 5 g) (Grade 1C)."
- Rec 36 (antiplatelets): "We recommend that routine platelet transfusion be avoided in patients with ongoing bleeding who have been treated with APAs (Grade 1C)." Rationale on desmopressin (verbatim): "Data on desmopressin as a potential alternative to platelet transfusion for reversal of APA effects in trauma patients are scarce, therefore, we have not made a recommendation on the use of desmopressin in this setting." [Note: the 5th edition (2019) did have a desmopressin suggestion — recalled, not checked.]
- Rec 37 (thromboprophylaxis): "We recommend early initiation of mechanical thromboprophylaxis with intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) while the patient is immobile and has a bleeding risk (Grade 1C)." / "We recommend combined pharmacological and IPC thromboprophylaxis within 24 h after bleeding has been controlled and until the patient is mobile (Grade 1B)." / "We do not recommend the use of graduated compression stockings for thromboprophylaxis (Grade 1C)." / "We do not recommend the routine use of inferior vena cava filters as thromboprophylaxis (Grade 1C)."
- Viscoelastic (rationale, Rec 26 area) [verified text, but internally inconsistent]: one passage describes iTACTIC as "690 adults ... VEM 67%, CCT 64%, OR 1.15 (95% CI 0.76-1.73)" and TBI subgroup (n=74) 28-day mortality VEM 44% vs CCT 74%; another passage says iTACTIC enrolled "396 trauma patients". The primary iTACTIC paper should be used for n (see Q5).

### Inferences
- The guideline's literature cut-off (Dec 2021) means it predates PATCH-Trauma, CRYOSTAT-2, PROCOAG, UK-REBOA, SWiFT and the US andexanet withdrawal; a handout should present it as "2023 European guideline (evidence to 2021)" and overlay newer trials.
- "1.1-1.3 mmol/L" is the guideline's stated normal range of ionised calcium (rationale text), not a separately graded target; the graded recommendation says "within the normal range". Values like "iCa > 1.2 mmol/L" or "> 1.0" seen in secondary sources are not the guideline's wording.
- The 3-4 g fibrinogen dose is a grade 2C suggestion; the 1.5 g/L trigger is graded 1C only after a split vote (originally proposed 2B).

### Gaps
- No 7th edition or formal update found: web search for "seventh edition" returned only editions 1-6, and a PubMed search ("European guideline management major bleeding coagulopathy trauma", 2024 onward, sorted by date) surfaced the 6th edition (PMID 36859355) as the latest edition (Nov 2026). Absence in search is not proof of absence; check the Critical Care journal and the STOP the Bleeding campaign site before printing.

## Q2. ATLS 11th edition (2025): changes; hemorrhage class table

### Takeaway
The only PRIMARY (ACS) statements available online confirm that ATLS 11 launched in 2025 (ACS Brief 16 Sep 2025; ATLS 11 page says it "launches" in July) and that the algorithm is now xABCDE with "x" = control of exsanguinating external hemorrhage [verified on facs.org]. All clinical specifics (crystalloid as a bridge only, TXA 1 g + 1 g within 3 h and a 2 g bolus option for TBI, LTOWB or 1:1:1, permissive hypotension until hemorrhage control) come from SECONDARY summaries; the ATLS 11 manual itself is not publicly accessible and was not read. Whether ATLS 11 kept the 4-class hemorrhage table could not be confirmed from a primary source; one 2026 study states it staged hemorrhagic shock "according to the Advanced Trauma Life Support 11th edition" (indirect evidence that a staging scheme persists) [secondary].

## Q3. ACS TQIP transfusion / massive transfusion and anticoagulant reversal guidance

### Takeaway
ACS Trauma Quality Programs released a NEW "Best Practices Guidelines: Transfusion in Trauma" in November 2026 (71-page PDF, read in full for the relevant sections this session [verified]); it explicitly updates the 2014 Massive Transfusion in Trauma BPG and folds in anticoagulant/antiplatelet reversal. Headline US-specific values that DIFFER from the European guideline: TXA 2 g IV bolus is the key-point regimen after MHP activation (1 g + 1 g/8 h also acceptable; never > 2 g total; caution > 3 h); permissive hypotension SBP 90 mmHg (SBP 110 mmHg with TBI) rather than 80-90 / MAP >= 80; calcium after each >= 2 units (CaCl2 10% 20 mg/kg max 1 g or Ca gluconate 10% 40 mg/kg max 2 g) with iCa kept 1.0-1.3 mmol/L; MHP trigger examples SI > 1.0 with SBP < 110, ABC >= 2, > 3 units in 1 h; LTOWB or 1:1:1 components; fibrinogen only lab-guided ("Empiric use of fibrinogen has not been shown to improve outcomes"); PCC and rFVIIa NOT for empiric MHP use; 4F-PCC first line for VKA (25-50 IU/kg by INR or fixed 1500 IU, 2000 IU if INR > 10 or > 100 kg) and for FXa inhibitors (25 IU/kg minor, 50 IU/kg major, or fixed 3000 IU); andexanet "withdrawn from the US market in December 2025"; idarucizumab first line for DTIs; platelets recommended for antiplatelet-associated ICH needing surgery; single-dose DDAVP "may be considered".

## Q4. TXA trials with exact numbers and dosing

### Takeaway
CRASH-2 established 1 g IV over 10 min + 1 g over 8 h within 8 h of injury (all-cause mortality 14.5% vs 16.0%, RR 0.91); the 2011 exploratory timing analysis showed benefit <= 1 h (RR 0.68) and 1-3 h (RR 0.79) but apparent harm for bleeding death > 3 h (RR 1.44). Prehospital trials in mature systems (Rowell TBI 2020, STAAMP, PATCH-Trauma 2023) missed their primary endpoints; PATCH-Trauma showed lower 28-day mortality (17.3% vs 21.8%, RR 0.79) as a secondary outcome. The 2 g bolus regimen comes from Rowell 2020 (bolus-only arm), not from a positive primary result.

- The robust, reproducible TXA signal is early (<= 3 h, ideally <= 1 h) administration in bleeding patients; the > 3 h "harm" is an exploratory CRASH-2 subgroup on bleeding death and underpins every guideline's 3-h cut-off (ACS 2026 prints it as a caution).

## Q5. Blood products, ratios, whole blood, viscoelastic testing, fibrinogen, PCC, REBOA, calcium and vasopressin trials, MT triggers

### Takeaway
No ratio, product, or adjunct trial since PROPPR has shown a mortality benefit in the hospital phase: PROPPR 1:1:1 vs 1:1:2 (no 24-h/30-day mortality difference; fewer exsanguination deaths, more hemostasis); CRYOSTAT-2 empiric early cryo (null, 25.3% vs 26.1%); PROCOAG empiric 4F-PCC (no product reduction, MORE thromboembolism 35% vs 24%); iTACTIC viscoelastic vs conventional (null); UK-REBOA (harm signal, 54% vs 42% 90-day mortality). Prehospital: PAMPer plasma positive (23.2% vs 33.0%) but COMBAT and RePHILL null; 2026 whole-blood trials SWiFT (RR 1.02) and TOWAR (aOR 1.24, NS) both null at 2 units. Vasopressin (Sims 2019) reduced blood products, not mortality; CAVALIER (calcium + vasopressin) still recruiting. FEISTY pilot: fibrinogen concentrate faster than cryo (29 vs 60 min); FEISTY II (n=900) ongoing.

### Inferences
- In the hospital phase the evidence base favors balanced (1:1:1 or LTOWB) empiric resuscitation, then lab/VHA-guided targeted therapy; empiric "add-on" products (cryo, PCC, rFVIIa) have not helped and PCC increased thrombosis.
- Low-titer O whole blood: US guidance favors it "when available" (ACS 2026, >250 US centers) based on observational data and logistics; the first two RCTs (SWiFT, TOWAR, both prehospital, 2 units) found no superiority. No in-hospital LTOWB RCT result was found as of Nov 2026.
- REBOA in the ED should be framed as not recommended outside trials/expert centers given UK-REBOA; the European 2023 Rec 21 "consider REBOA (2C)" predates it.

- LTOWB observational evidence (e.g., Sperry JL et al. J Am Coll Surg 2023;237(2):206-219, cited by ACS 2026) was not read.
- No in-hospital whole-blood RCT (e.g., TROOP or others) results were searched specifically; a targeted search could close this.
- iTACTIC TBI 28-day mortality figures quoted by the European guideline (44% vs 74%) not verified in the primary abstract.

## Q6. Hypocalcemia in trauma

### Takeaway
Admission ionized hypocalcemia affects roughly half of major trauma patients (56% pooled prevalence before transfusion, 2025 meta-analysis) and is consistently associated with higher mortality (pooled OR ~2.4-2.8), but no RCT yet shows that giving calcium improves outcomes (European guideline says so explicitly; CAVALIER is still recruiting). The "lethal diamond" term comes from Ditzel et al. 2020 (J Trauma Acute Care Surg). 10 mL of 10% CaCl2 = 270 mg elemental Ca vs 10 mL of 10% Ca gluconate = 90 mg (European guideline, verified). US dosing (ACS 2026): CaCl2 10% 20 mg/kg (max 1 g) or Ca gluconate 10% 40 mg/kg (max 2 g) after every >= 2 units, keep iCa 1.0-1.3 mmol/L.

### Cited Findings
- Incidence [verified abstract]: Vasudeva M, Mathew JK, Groombridge C, et al. Hypocalcemia in trauma patients: A systematic review. J Trauma Acute Care Surg. 2021;90(2):396-402. doi:10.1097/TA.0000000000003027. PMID 33196630; PMCID PMC7850586. "Admission ionized hypocalcemia was present in up to 56.2% of the population"; associated with increased mortality in all three included studies, transfusion in two, coagulopathy in one. — [PubMed 33196630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33196630/)
- Prevalence and mortality before transfusion [verified abstract]: Srichuachom W, Krintratun S, Chenthanakij B, Wongtanasarasin W. Prevalence and outcomes of hypocalcemia on ED arrival in traumatic patients before blood transfusions: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Scand J Trauma Resusc Emerg Med. 2025;33(1):43. doi:10.1186/s13049-025-01361-y. PMID 40098024. 15 studies; prevalence 56% (95% CI 37%-74%; I2 99.8%); mortality OR 2.44 (1.76-3.40). — [PubMed 40098024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40098024/)
- Mortality association [verified abstract]: Palitnonkiat V, Thampongsa T, Sirikun J, Owattanapanich N. - The handout can say "hypocalcemia is common and prognostic; treatment is reasonable and guideline-endorsed (European 1C), but outcome benefit is unproven pending CAVALIER."
- Target ranges differ by source: European "normal range" stated as 1.1-1.3 mmol/L; ACS 1.0-1.3 mmol/L. Neither is a trial-derived target.

## Q7. Anticoagulant and antiplatelet reversal in trauma, 2026

### Takeaway
Andexanet alfa (Andexxa) is no longer sold in the US: FDA safety communication dated 18 Dec 2025; AstraZeneca ended US commercial sales 22 Dec 2025 and requested voluntary BLA withdrawal [verified FDA page]. US trauma guidance (ACS 2026 BPG) now names 4F-PCC first line for FXa inhibitors (25 IU/kg minor, 50 IU/kg major, or fixed 3000 IU) and for VKA (25-50 IU/kg by INR or fixed 1500 IU / 2000 IU if INR > 10 or > 100 kg) [verified]. Kcentra label (VKA only): 25/35/50 units/kg for INR 2-<4 / 4-6 / >6, dosing weight capped at 100 kg (max 2500/3500/5000 units), give vitamin K concurrently, repeat dosing not recommended [verified DailyMed]. Idarucizumab 5 g (two 2.5 g/50 mL vials) [verified label]. Platelet transfusion for antiplatelet-associated spontaneous ICH was harmful in PATCH (Lancet 2016) [verified]; ACS 2026 recommends platelets only for antiplatelet-associated ICH needing surgery and says a single DDAVP dose "may be considered" [verified]. The European guideline (2023) made NO desmopressin recommendation [verified].
