### Batch 2 - CDC tetanus page + EAST 2022-2026 PubMed sweep + open fracture literature 2021-2026 [verified = abstract/page read]
- CDC "Clinical Guidance for Wound Management to Prevent Tetanus" (cdc.gov/tetanus/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html; published May 15, 2024; updated/reviewed Sept 8, 2026). Read via WebFetch summary: [verified-page, paraphrased by fetch tool]
  - Unknown history, unvaccinated, or incomplete primary series -> vaccinate for ALL wounds.
  - Complete primary series, clean and minor wound -> vaccinate if last dose >=10 years ago.
  - Complete primary series, dirty or major wound -> vaccinate if last dose >=5 years ago.
  - Last dose <5 years ago and primary series complete -> no vaccine regardless of wound.
  - Dirty/major wound examples: penetrating or puncture wounds; wounds with dirt, soil, feces, or saliva (animal/human bites); devitalized tissue (burns, compound fractures, crush injuries, frostbite, necrotic/gangrenous wounds).
  - TIG: clean minor wounds -> TIG "never indicated"; dirty/major wounds -> TIG for unknown history, never vaccinated, incomplete primary series, HIV infection, or severe immunodeficiency. Dose 250 international units IM. CDC does not stockpile TIG; commercial purchase.
  - "Topical or systemic antibiotics are not recommended to prevent tetanus."
  - Page uses "complete primary series" wording (older MMWR table wording is ">=3 doses") [the >=3-dose wording is recalled from Liang 2018 Table, not re-read].

### Batch 3 - EAST 2022 tube thoracostomy full text (PMC9608538) + CDC adult schedule notes [verified]
- Freeman 2022 EAST TT full text (read): Final recommendation wording: "We conditionally recommend that antibiotic prophylaxis be given at the time of insertion to reduce empyema in adult patients who require TT for traumatic hemothorax or pneumothorax." [verified full text]
  - The PMG does NOT name a specific drug, dose or duration in its recommendation. Evidence base: "The most common antibiotic used in the studies was cefazolin." "Antibiotic protocols in the other studies ranged from a single preprocedure dose to 48 hours post TT removal." [verified]
  - Trials: Grover (clindamycin to 1 d post-removal or 5 d), Stone (cefamandole to 2 d post-removal), LeBlanc (cephapirin to 24 h post-removal), LoCurto (cefoxitin to 12 h post-removal), Brunner (cefazolin for duration of drainage), Cant (cefazolin 24 h, isolated chest stab wounds), Nichols (cefonicid to 24 h post-removal), Gonzalez & Holevar (cefazolin for TT duration), Maxwell (multicenter RCT; single vs continuous dosing), Villegas-Carlos (cephalothin 7 d), Heydari 2014 (cefazolin 24 h, no difference), AAST observational studies (cefazolin 84.1% of prophylaxis; no association with empyema). [verified]
  - Prospective pooled: empyema 44/1057 with prophylaxis vs 96/1018 without; penetrating RCTs 5/358 vs 29/326. [verified]
  - Not unanimous: "majority (13 of 16) with dissent"; three dissenters noted declining benefit over 30 years (newer studies show no benefit). Harms discussed: C. difficile (~450,000 infections and 29,000 US deaths/yr cited), resistance (Maxwell). [verified]
  - History: EAST 2000 PMG = level III recommendation for prophylaxis; 2012 update = no recommendation; 2022 = conditional recommendation (GRADE). [verified]
  - Practical inference (NOT stated in the PMG): a single dose of cefazolin (e.g., 2 g IV; 3 g if >=120 kg per ASHP surgical prophylaxis convention) at insertion is the regimen most consistent with the evidence; no support for continuing until tube removal. [inference]

  - Key points box (p.6), verbatim: "Patients with open fractures should receive intravenous antimicrobials within one hour of presentation to reduce the risk of infection." NOTE: this CONFLICTS with CDC/ACIP (open fracture = dirty/major wound -> toxoid if >=5 years; TIG only if <3 doses/unknown/incomplete or HIV/severe immunodeficiency, NOT for >10 years since booster in a fully primed patient).
